# Predictions log

*Started November 22, 2026. This is a public, dated record of what For John predicts, written before the answers are known. When trials, studies or reviewers report, each prediction will be marked right, wrong or unresolved. Entries are never edited after the fact; corrections and outcomes are added below each one with a date.*

**Why keep this.** Anyone can explain results after they arrive. Committing to predictions in advance is the honest test of whether connecting the published evidence, as this project does, finds real signals. If our hit rate is poor, that is worth knowing too.

**How to read it.** Each prediction states what we expect, what would prove it wrong, how and roughly when it could be tested, and our confidence: a rough probability that it turns out right, which is our judgement, not a calculation. Most are hypotheses for researchers to test. **Not medical advice.**

## Outcomes

*Each outcome is added here with its date, source and verdict (right, wrong or unresolved), without changing the prediction above.*

- **P15, 2026-11-24: unresolved by its own rule.** Pooled hazard ratio per SD 0.99 (95% CI 0.74–1.32) across three independent cohorts (110 patients, 53 events), against 2.30 in the discovery cohort. Not right (no association seen); not wrong by the stated rule, because the upper bound is 1.32, not below 1.3. The discovery-size effect is ruled out. Source: `reports/Engine cycle 1 - does RAMP1 predict relapse.md` (our calculation).
- **P16, 2026-11-24: unresolved by its own rule.** CXCR4 RNA in lung metastases against primary tumours, six datasets, 29 against 51: +0.43 standard deviations (95% CI −0.05 to +0.91). Not right (the lower bound is below 0); not wrong by the stated rule (the upper bound is above 0.5). The lean disappeared after adjusting for immune cells (+0.06). Source: `reports/Engine cycle 2 - do lung metastases carry more CXCR4.md` (our calculation).
- **P17, 2026-11-24: unresolved by its own rule.** CXCL12 RNA, same comparison: −0.08 standard deviations (−0.55 to +0.39). No decrease seen; not wrong by the stated rule, because the lower bound is −0.55, not above −0.5. Same source.
- **P18, 2026-11-24: unresolved by its own rule.** CDKN2A deep deletion, metastasis or relapse against primary, two cohorts, 130 against 211 patients: pooled odds ratio 1.10 (95% CI 0.59–2.06). Not right (the lower bound is below 1); not wrong by the stated rule (the upper bound is above 1.5). Source: `reports/Engine cycle 3 - is 9p21 deletion more common at relapse.md` (our calculation).
- **P19, 2026-11-24: right.** Of 25 recruiting or soon-to-recruit trials an osteosarcoma patient with MTAP loss could join on paper, one accepts patients from age 12 (NCT06796699, sites in China only). The other 24 start at 18. Source: `reports/Engine cycle 4 - MTAP-selected trials open to osteosarcoma.md` (our calculation from registry records).
- **P20, 2026-11-24: unresolved by its own rule, and underpowered.** Only two MTAP-lost osteosarcoma lines had knockout data. Primary measure (MAT2A knockout): difference +0.11 (95% CI −0.14 to +0.37), against −0.11 across all cancers. Source: `reports/Engine cycle 5 - do osteosarcoma cells without MTAP depend on PRMT5.md` (our calculation).
- **P21, 2026-11-24: unresolved by its own rule.** Slope of PKMYT1 knockout score on CCNE1 RNA across 17 osteosarcoma lines: −0.059 (95% CI −0.151 to +0.033), against −0.031 across all cancers. Source: `reports/Engine cycle 6 - do osteosarcoma cells with more CCNE1 depend on PKMYT1.md` (our calculation).
- **P22, 2026-11-24: unresolved by its own rule.** CCNE1 amplification, metastasis or relapse against primary, 130 against 211 patients: pooled odds ratio 1.14 (95% CI 0.52–2.48). Source: `reports/Engine cycle 7 - CCNE1 amplification at relapse and the trials open to it.md` (our calculation).
- **P23, 2026-11-24: right.** Of nine recruiting or soon-to-recruit trials an osteosarcoma patient with CCNE1 amplification could join on paper, one accepts patients from age 12 (lunresertib, NCT04855656; sites in five countries including the United States). Given a 10% chance in advance. Same source.
- **P24, 2026-11-24: right.** The scan recovered 9p21 loss with PRMT5 and with WDR77 as replicated hits. Source: `reports/Engine cycle 8 - a scan for drug weaknesses that go with osteosarcoma's gene changes.md` (our calculation).
- **P25, 2026-11-24: ambiguous, not scored.** The only replicated hit off the known list whose dependency gene has a late-stage drug in the project's table is MCL1 gain with MCL1 itself. That meets the wording, but the prediction was about finding an unanticipated druggable pair, and this is a tumour depending on its own amplified gene, already published for osteosarcoma. Same source.
- **P26, 2026-11-24: right.** TP53-altered lines needed MDM2 less (+0.65, 95% CI +0.61 to +0.70); RB1-lost lines needed CDK4, CDK6 and CCND1 less (+0.45, +0.36, +0.81, all intervals excluding zero). Source: `reports/Engine cycle 9 - weaknesses that go with the tumour-suppressor genes osteosarcoma loses.md` (our calculation).
- **P27, 2026-11-24: right.** RB1 loss with SKP2, E2F3 and CDK2 all replicated in the knockdown screen. Same source.
- **P28, 2026-11-24: ambiguous, not scored.** The only unlisted replicated pair with a drug in the project's table is RB1 loss with TP53, which is an artefact of how the screen scores TP53 (knocking out working TP53 speeds growth), with an MDM2 inhibitor listed against TP53. Same source.
