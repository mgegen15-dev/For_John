# Osteosarcoma's forty-year plateau hides tractable gaps

Childhood osteosarcoma survival has not moved since the mid-1980s: about 70% at five years for localized disease, 20–30% for metastatic disease and under 30% after relapse. The notes point to design and infrastructure, more than a shortage of ideas, as the reason. The genome is a structural catastrophe with almost no recurrent druggable mutation, so precision-medicine matching rarely applies (13% in Pediatric MATCH), and the field spent decades adding more cytotoxics to a backbone that was already at its ceiling. Neuroblastoma, by contrast, raised high-risk survival from under 15% to roughly 50–60% event-free (figure marked "verify" in the notes) by asking one randomized question at a time in biology-defined groups, sharing data across 8,800 patients, and letting parent-funded consortia carry projects industry would not. The 2026 frontier in osteosarcoma is real but fragile: B7-H3 antibody-drug conjugates and a HER2 Listeria vaccine look promising, yet the headline results are press-reported or historically controlled. The most valuable overlooked gaps are mostly about connecting existing pieces: re-analysing published genomes with the 2025 chromothripsis framework, building a pooled historical-control dataset for relapse trials, testing therapies in the resected-lung-metastasis setting with randomization, and adapting treatment on early markers such as ctDNA instead of late necrosis. Several of these need data, organisation and money more than a wet lab, which is where an independent researcher or small team can contribute. Every gap and transfer idea in this report is a hypothesis drawn from the notes, not an established finding.

## How far the evidence behind this report can be trusted

This report synthesises six sets of research notes and adds nothing from outside them. The notes are uneven. Much of the osteosarcoma biology and trial history rests on abstracts, PubMed/PMC records and institutional pages. Several recent results rest on search-result snippets because the full pages could not be opened. Many neuroblastoma figures cite landmark papers that were not re-fetched and are marked "verify". I carry those labels through: **"verify"** means the notes flagged the number as unconfirmed, **"unverified background"** means it came from a researcher's prior knowledge without a retrieved source, and **"press-reported"** means no peer-reviewed publication was found.

Four disagreements between the notes are unresolved and should be checked before anything is quoted:

## One step-change in 1986, then forty years of negative trials

The only large jump in outcomes came from adding chemotherapy after surgery. The MIOS trial randomized just 36 patients and found two-year relapse-free survival of **66% with chemotherapy against 17% without** ([Link 1986](https://www.qxmd.com/r/3520317)). Since then the curve has been flat. US registry data describe "no substantial improvement since the 1980s" ([NCI PDQ](https://https.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK65736.27)), with five-year cause-specific survival of 71.8% without metastasis and 30.4% with it ([PMC8534404](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8534404)). After relapse, five-year survival is under 30% ([FOSTER meta-analysis](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10220907/1/Wilson_Relapsed%20OS%20prognostic%20factors%20accepted.pdf)).

The largest trial ever run, EURAMOS-1, registered 2,260 patients and answered both of its questions negatively. Adding ifosfamide and etoposide for patients whose tumours responded poorly gave an event-free survival hazard ratio of 0.98 while doubling severe non-blood toxicity from 12% to 24% ([ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/News/43899)). Adding interferon for good responders gave a hazard ratio of 0.83 with a confidence interval crossing 1, but only 271 of 357 assigned patients started the drug and 105 of those stopped early ([Bielack 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26033801/)). The notes draw two inferences. Poor response looks like fixed biology that more of the same drug class cannot overcome. And tumour necrosis at surgery predicts outcome but does not tell doctors what to change, partly because it is measured once and late.

Mifamurtide, an immune stimulant, remains unresolved. Its trial (INT-0133) reported an overall-survival hazard ratio of 0.63 per the manufacturer ([NICE](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ta235/chapter/the-manufacturers-submission)), yet an FDA panel voted 12–2 that efficacy was not shown ([Healio](https://cm.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20120325/fda-panel-rejects-mifamurtide-for-osteosarcoma-in-children)). The notes add, as unverified background, that no confirmatory randomized trial was ever run.

Trial design compounded the problem. In relapsed disease, the benchmark is four-month event-free survival of 12% from 96 pooled patients ([Lagmay 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5012712)), and three later trials together managed 7% ([ASCO abstract](https://ace.asco.org/content/344819/abstract/197938)). Scan-based response is unreliable because the calcified tumour matrix does not shrink. A FOSTER review of 98 relapse trials from 2003–2016 found designs too heterogeneous to compare and no good historical cohorts ([UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10178055/)). Most patients have too little tissue sampled at diagnosis for molecular work ([PMC7829872](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7829872)).

What does work after relapse is surgery. In 576 relapsed patients, five-year survival was 39% with a second complete surgical remission and 0% without ([Kempf-Bielack 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12586810/)). In 219 patients with a single lung relapse, five-year survival reached 51.2%, and relapse chemotherapy "does not seem to improve survival" ([Mettmann 2023](https://doaj.org/article/7c87cf91104a4367a88394805f691d43)). These series are retrospective and biased toward patients with less disease.

The cost of the standard regimen is lifelong: heart damage from doxorubicin, hearing and kidney damage from cisplatin. Sodium thiosulfate was approved in 2022 to protect hearing, but the relevant trial subset was 77 patients and questions about survival effects remain ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-sodium-thiosulfate-reduce-risk-ototoxicity-associated-cisplatin-pediatric-patients); [OncLive](https://onclive.com/view/pedmark-reduces-ototoxicity-risk-in-cisplatin-treated-pediatric-solid-tumors)). The notes hold no quantified survivorship data.

## B7-H3 and a HER2 vaccine lead a frontier built on thin evidence

Checkpoint inhibitors, the drugs that transformed melanoma, have failed here. Pembrolizumab produced a response in 1 of 22 osteosarcoma patients ([SARC028](https://classic.clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/results/NCT02301039)). Pairing a checkpoint drug with a blood-vessel-targeting pill (camrelizumab plus apatinib) raised the response rate to 20.9% but still missed its target ([PMC7223462](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7223462)). The pills alone are the only class with repeatable activity in relapse: regorafenib extended progression-free time from 1.7 to 3.6 months ([SARC024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31013172/)), and cabozantinib gave an 11.9% response rate ([PMC9918141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9918141)). None has shown a survival benefit. In the OLIE trial, adding lenvatinib to chemotherapy missed its endpoint, and median survival was reported as longer in the control arm ([ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/news/october-2024/novel-treatment-options-for-children-and-young-adults-with-relapsed-osteosarcoma/)). The class is now being tested at diagnosis for the first time in COG's AOST2032, cabozantinib added to standard chemotherapy ([St. Jude](https://www.stjude.org/treatment/clinical-trials/cabozantinib-with-chemotherapy-for-osteosarcoma.html)).

Borrowed targets have mostly disappointed. The neuroblastoma antibody dinutuximab, given after lung metastases were removed, left 11 of 39 patients event-free at 12 months against a required 16 ([AOST1421](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8dx5s3gd/qt8dx5s3gd.pdf)). Zoledronate, a bone drug, trended toward harm when added to first-line chemotherapy ([ASCO Post](https://www.ascopost.com/News/42704)). The breast-cancer-derived conjugates against gpNMB and HER2 are reported as negative in one notes file from search snippets; the other file could not retrieve results.

Two results stand out, and both need caution. The B7-H3 antibody-drug conjugate from Hansoh/GSK gave a 17.4% response rate in a phase 2 study ([Springer review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44178-025-00217-w)). Its Chinese randomized phase 3, ARTEMIS-011 (122 patients), is **press-reported** to have cut the risk of progression by 67% ([FierceBiotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/gsks-b7-h3-adc-drives-67-cancer-risk-reduction-phase-3)); the notes rest on a search snippet, with no peer-reviewed paper. Second, OST-HER2, a Listeria-based vaccine first tested in pet dogs, is **press-reported** to show three-year survival of 72.7% against 45.8% in a combined historical control after lung-metastasis surgery ([CancerNetwork](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/ost-her2-shows-significant-final-3-year-os-benefit-in-osteosarcoma-trial)). That comparison is company-reported, not randomized and not peer-reviewed.

Cell therapy has changed its design more than its targets. HER2 CAR-T cells looked inactive in 2015 when given at low dose without conditioning; with conditioning and repeat doses, half of treated patients had clinical benefit in 2024, though without durable remissions ([Baylor](https://www.bcm.edu/news/car-t-cell-therapy-targeting-her2-antigen-shows-promise-against-advanced-sarcoma-in-phase-i-trial)). St. Jude now plans B7-H3 CAR-T as consolidation after first-line chemotherapy (NCT07428993, 41 patients, primary completion 2034) and after radiation (RAD3CAR) ([PMC12729273](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12729273)).

The pattern across all of this is that three different treatments have been aimed at the same clinical state, the patient whose lung metastases have just been removed. One failed, one looks positive against historical controls, one is starting. The published benchmark for that state is a 12-month control rate of 20% with a confidence interval of 10–34%, wide enough that single-arm trials there are poorly powered. As the notes put it, the field's credibility problem is design, not drugs.

## Neuroblastoma advanced by asking one randomized question at a time

High-risk neuroblastoma shares osteosarcoma's basic character: few point mutations, driven by large chromosomal changes. Its survival nonetheless rose from under 15% in the 1980s to roughly 50–60% long-term event-free survival (verify; the notes could not confirm the latest pooled figures). Each gain came from a randomized trial that kept the backbone constant and changed one thing. High-dose chemotherapy with stem-cell rescue improved three-year event-free survival from 22% to 34%, and retinoic acid added more ([Matthay 1999](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199910143411601)). The antibody dinutuximab with immune stimulants raised two-year event-free survival from 46% to 66% ([Yu 2010](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0911123)). Tandem transplant added a further step ([Park 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2019.11642)).

Three structural habits sit behind that record. First, risk is assigned before treatment from biology. The international INRG system pooled 8,800 patients to define risk groups by age, stage, MYCN status and chromosome changes ([Cohn 2009](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2008.16.6785)), and its data commons grew into the Pediatric Cancer Data Commons ([PCDC](https://commons.cri.uchicago.edu/)). Osteosarcoma still assigns risk mainly after treatment, by metastases and necrosis. Second, biology was turned into prognosis: three mutually exclusive ways of maintaining chromosome ends define outcome in neuroblastoma ([Ackermann 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat6768)). Third, parents funded named projects. Band of Parents, formed in 2007 by about 100 families at Memorial Sloan Kettering, led a $2 million grant in 2013 for one specific antibody ([Band of Parents](https://bandofparents.org/?p=40)).

The most striking precedent is eflornithine (DFMO). A parent-backed consortium took a cheap, off-patent drug through a single-arm trial of 92 patients, compared it with 852 cooperative-group patients as an external control (four-year event-free survival 84% against 72%), and won FDA approval in December 2023 after a 14–6 panel vote. FDA reviewers said they had never before relied on a single externally controlled trial for an oncology approval ([TargetedOnc](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/fda-approves-eflornithine-to-reduce-risk-of-relapse-in-high-risk-neuroblastoma); [Pink Sheet](https://pink.citeline.com/PS148995/US-WorldMeds-Neuroblastoma-Drug-External-Controls-Confirmatory-Evidence-And-A-Concern-About-Precedent)).

Academic cell therapy also delivered. GD2-CART01 gave a 63% response rate in 27 relapsed patients, with three-year survival of 60% at the recommended dose ([ESMO](https://esmo.org/oncology-news/gd2-cart01-may-induce-sustained-antitumour-effect-in-a-proportion-of-patients-with-relapsed-or-refractory-neuroblastoma)).

Neuroblastoma is not solved. Relapsed high-risk disease remains largely fatal, MYCN cannot be drugged directly, and tumour cells escape GD2 therapy by switching to a state that loses the target ([van Groningen 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3899)). One half of its model cannot transfer either: neuroblastoma has a favourable subgroup that regresses on its own in infants, which made safe de-escalation possible. Osteosarcoma has no such group, so it can copy only the other half, a biological definition of high risk made before treatment starts.

The notes also explain why the GD2 antibody worked in one disease and failed in the other. In neuroblastoma it is given after intensive chemotherapy alongside other immune agents, and in relapse it is combined with chemotherapy. The osteosarcoma trial gave it largely alone, without selecting patients by GD2 level. This reading is an inference from the notes, not a tested conclusion.

## A rare disease inside an underfunded category, with few market incentives

Osteosarcoma affects about 800 people a year in the US ([Drug Discovery News](https://www.drugdiscoverynews.com/nci-grant-pushes-forward-bone-cancer-research-9981)). Pediatric cancer as a whole receives **less than 5% of the National Cancer Institute budget**, and NIH does not break pediatric spending down by disease ([AACR 2025](https://cancerprogressreport.aacr.org/pediatric/pcpr25-contents/pcpr25-a-message-from-the-aacr/)). No public dollar figure for osteosarcoma research exists in the notes. Much disease-specific money comes from small family foundations. MIB Agents has awarded $2.9M to 37 investigators per the notes ($2.5M per the source headline), funded by family funds whose members sit on peer review ([Clinical Research News](https://www.clinicalresearchnewsonline.com/news/2025/06/12/mib-agents'-community-of-families-has-awarded-2-5m-in-grants-to-research-pediatric-bone-cancer)).

Federal funding held in fiscal 2026. Congress enacted $47.493B for NIH, up 1.0%, against a presidential request that would have cut it 40.6% ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-05-18_R43341_3cc14d4f8e161830185f61cc3883386300b058ba.html)). The Childhood Cancer Data Initiative was announced as doubling to $100M ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-doubles-ai-backed-childhood-Cancer-research-funding.html)), though the final enacted amount conflicts across sources.

Industry incentives are weak. The RACE Act requires pediatric studies when an adult cancer drug's target matters in a childhood cancer. After it took effect, 42.9% of approved cancer therapies carried a pediatric requirement, up from none, but the median time to a final report is 5.96 years ([Friends of Cancer Research](https://friendsofcancerresearch.org/blog/assessing-the-impact-of-the-research-to-accelerate-cures-and-equity-race-act/)), and only 6.9% of trials of drugs with RACE-relevant targets include children ([Children's Cancer Cause](https://www.childrenscancercause.org/blog/race-act-effectiveness)). The law bites least where there is no defined molecular target, which is osteosarcoma's situation: only 13% of osteosarcoma patients in Pediatric MATCH had a matchable alteration, the lowest of the non-brain tumours ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20190517/pediatric-tumorsequencing-trial-finds-higher-rate-of-targetable-alterations-than-expected)). The main financial carrot is the rare pediatric disease priority review voucher, which lapsed in December 2024 and was reauthorized in February 2026 through September 2029, with a required government review of its effectiveness. One voucher sold for $200M in January 2026 ([Lowenstein Sandler](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/congress-reauthorizes-pediatric-priority-review-voucher-program-in-fy2026-budget-renewed-incentives-for-rare-pediatric-drug-development-fdalife-sciences)).

Data and tissue are scattered. Genomic data sit in controlled-access repositories that, per the notes' unverified background, require an institutional signing official. The most open new source is the Osteosarcoma Project, where patients in the US and Canada donate records, saliva or blood and stored tumour directly, with data released in batches ([Dana-Farber](https://danafarberbostonchildrens.org/news/count-me-launches-osteosarcoma-project)). The notes found no counts of paired diagnosis-and-relapse samples anywhere.

Patients between 15 and 39 fall between pediatric and adult systems. Survival gains in 15–19-year-olds (56% to about 66%) lagged those in younger children (40% to 76%) ([NCI PDQ](https://https.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK65736.27)). The notes infer, without sourced data, that lower trial enrolment and care outside pediatric centres contribute.

The notes' reading of all this is that the binding constraint is not trial slots. It is a shortage of credible agents and the lack of a shared benchmark for judging small trials. Europe's FOSTER consortium is working on both, including a randomized cabozantinib trial at diagnosis that was planned to open in 2025 ([Onkologi i Sverige](https://onkologiisverige.se/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/OIS-nr-5-Kliniska-studier.pdf)).

## Overlooked gaps: twelve hypotheses the notes surface

Everything in the two tables below is a hypothesis. Each was marked as an inference or speculation in the notes, and "not found" means not found in the searches run, which did not include a direct registry query.

| # | Gap (hypothesis) | Why the notes think it is open | Type of work |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No pooled, standardized historical-control dataset for relapse trials | FOSTER's review names the absence ([UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10178055/)) | Data curation |
| 2 | No randomized comparison in the resected-lung-metastasis setting | Three single-arm programmes used it; benchmark interval is 10–34% ([AOST1421](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8dx5s3gd/qt8dx5s3gd.pdf)) | Trial design, advocacy |

| 5 | Front-line immune stimulation never got a clean confirmatory trial | Mifamurtide and interferon both leaned favourable but inconclusive ([Bielack 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26033801/)) | Trial design |
| 6 | The four-month benchmark may discard drugs that act on residual disease | Benchmark built on measurable, unresectable patients ([Lagmay 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5012712)) | Methods |
| 7 | Published genomes not re-read with the 2025 chromothripsis framework | No source links the TP53 intron-1 hotspot to the new mechanism, or applies it to the canine genomes ([Gardner 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6642146)) | Computational |

| 9 | Single-cell "replication" is largely re-use of two or three datasets | Earlier work covered only 17 samples in total ([J Hematol Oncol 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13045-024-01598-7)) | Literature audit |
| 10 | Inherited risk is probably under-counted | 28% of 1,004 patients carried a pathogenic variant ([Mirabello 2020](https://dceg.cancer.gov/news-events/news/2020/osteosarcoma-susceptibility)); standard tests miss TP53 intron-1 inversions ([Ribi 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4480712)); non-European data absent | Genetics |
| 11 | Signals in existing trials were never followed up | PD-L1 ≥5% and lung-only disease trended better on camrelizumab/apatinib; drug contributions never separated ([PMC7223462](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7223462)) | Clinical research |

Two further questions from the notes concern harm reduction, not cure. Nobody has randomized removing cisplatin or doxorubicin for good responders, and the survival safety of the hearing-protection drug has not been shown specifically in osteosarcoma. Both matter to families because the late effects are permanent.

Gaps 1, 7, 8 and 9 stand out for a specific reason: they need no new patients, only existing data and careful work.
