# For John: the best leads, and who could test them

*Last revised November 14, 2026, after an independent audit of its claims against primary sources; updated November 19, 2026 from the new-evidence scan (`reports/Whats new in osteosarcoma research.md`), and re-ranked November 20, 2026 after a repo-wide consistency check and an audit of the three newest reports (`research_notes/Audit/`; see the change log). This ranks the open leads from the project's reports and matches each to the institutions and programmes best placed to act on it. Partners come from public NIH grant records (`data/nih_osteosarcoma_projects.csv`, checked against NIH RePORTER), ClinicalTrials.gov sponsors and the published literature. No one at these institutions has been contacted, and none has endorsed anything in this document.*

## Summary

This page ranks fourteen leads that could help children and young adults whose osteosarcoma has spread or come back. The top three are to test whether blocking inflammation after surgery (IL-1 or IL-6) keeps cancer out of the lungs, to find out whether gene copies in the tumour predict who benefits from VEGFR2-blocking kinase pills, and to publish the pooled control dataset this project has built. A pet-dog study of FAK or DKK1 blockade remains a strong lead, but dog programmes for both targets already exist or are planned, so the useful step now is to support and learn from them, not to start one. The surface-target lead is now split in two, because the evidence has diverged: LRRC15 has been confirmed as protein in patient tumours and already has trials, while PTK7 still lacks a series of stained patient tumours. Two approved-drug ideas from this project's repurposing screen, AKT blockers and CXCR4 blockers, enter at the bottom of the list: both have consistent animal data but rest on abstracts and carry real contradictions. Every lead is a hypothesis drawn from published work; none is a treatment recommendation. Partners are named as institutions and programmes, and none has been asked to take part yet.

**How to read the labels.** After key numbers we say where they come from:

- **(registry)**: an official database record, such as ClinicalTrials.gov, NIH RePORTER or an FDA notice.
- **(paper, full text)**: read in the full published paper.
- **(paper, abstract only)**: read only in the abstract.
- **(press release only)**: from a company announcement, conference coverage or a web listing, with no peer-reviewed paper.
- **(our calculation)**: counted or computed by this project.
- **(preprint, abstract only)**: read only in the abstract of a preprint, which has not been peer-reviewed.

**Scope.** Osteosarcoma is the target. Ideas are borrowed from anywhere: other sarcomas, neuroblastoma, adult cancers, other diseases, and dogs.

## How leads are scored

Each lead is scored 1 to 3 on four things, for a total out of 12:

- **Evidence:** how well the underlying finding holds up after checking. 3 = confirmed in full text and supported by more than one independent group or setting; 2 = at least one checked result that bears on the endpoint, but small, single-group or indirect; 1 = abstract-only or indirect.
- **Open:** whether it is still undone. 3 = nobody found doing it; 2 = partly done or adjacent work exists; 1 = already under way by others.
- **Feasibility:** how cheaply and quickly it could be done.
- **Impact:** whether it could change outcomes for patients with metastatic or relapsed disease, the patients osteosarcoma kills.

Leads with equal totals keep their previous order; new leads go below existing leads with the same total.

## The leads in detail

### 1. Block IL-1 or IL-6 after surgery to stop lung metastasis

**Why:**
- Lung tissue releases IL-1α, which drives osteosarcoma cells to make IL-6 and IL-8 and colonize the lung. Blocking IL-1, or IL-6 together with IL-8, reduced lung metastasis in mice (paper, abstract only; PMIDs 40882022, 30135299).
- A canine trial of the opposite approach, boosting immunity with inhaled IL-15 after amputation, was halted for futility: disease-free and overall survival were worse than historical controls given amputation and chemotherapy (paper, abstract only; NCI-COTC030, PMID 41209004). Its data also showed IL-6 rising after amputation and after chemotherapy.
- That suggests the weeks after surgery may be the time to *block* inflammation, not stimulate it. This is our inference, not the trial authors' conclusion.
- The drugs are approved and inexpensive: anakinra (IL-1) and tocilizumab (IL-6), the latter available as biosimilars. Neither has a registered efficacy trial in osteosarcoma on ClinicalTrials.gov; tocilizumab appears only in an adult sarcoma immunotherapy combination trial (NCT04616248) whose endpoints are safety and dose (registry). NIH has funded one osteosarcoma grant on IL-6/IL-8 (K08 CA201638, 2016–2020; registry).
- IL-6's osteosarcoma literature has low retraction (6 of 178 papers since 2010, 3.4%) and microRNA-type (8%) shares; the smaller IL-6 receptor literature is flagged high (our calculation, `reports/Evidence risk flags for osteosarcoma genes.md`). In this project's repurposing screen, tocilizumab's target scores 45.9 of 100, held down by low tumour RNA, which fits the lead's logic that IL-6 comes from the lung and the host after surgery, not the tumour (our calculation, `reports/Approved drugs worth testing in osteosarcoma.md`).

**Cheapest test:** a pet-dog study after amputation, with metastasis-free survival as the endpoint. A necropsy study of 15 untreated dogs found a pre-metastatic macrophage change in the lung (paper, abstract only; PMID 39526499), which supports the rationale. Those counts were made at necropsy, so using them as an early readout in live dogs (for example at four weeks) is untested.

**Partners:**
- The NCI Comparative Oncology Program, which ran COTC030 and holds its data.
- The NIH-funded metastatic-colonization programme at Nationwide Children's Hospital (R01 CA260178; registry).
- The UC Davis veterinary and surgical oncology team that ran the canine inhaled IL-15 work.

**Details:** `reports/Stopping lung metastasis in osteosarcoma.md`.

### 2. Do gene amplifications predict who benefits from VEGFR2-blocking pills?

**Why:**
- VEGFR2-blocking kinase pills are the most consistently active targeted drugs in relapse. Given alone, they leave about 52.5% of patients free of progression at four months, or 41.4% if the cabozantinib trial is counted by its own primary endpoint (our calculation, `reports/Osteosarcoma pooled control dataset.md`), against about 14% for other single agents. They are not the only treatments above the benchmark: IE chemotherapy alone keeps 42–66% progression-free at four months in two randomized control arms (paper, full text), and a B7-H3 conjugate beat chemotherapy in a randomized trial (ARTEMIS-011, lead 7). The pills' benefit lasts months.
- Overall survival has not improved. In OAIE, median survival was 18.1 months with apatinib plus IE against 22.9 months with IE alone (HR 1.48, 95% CI 0.76–2.89; paper, full text), though its control arm received ifosfamide and etoposide on days 1–5 against days 1–3 in the apatinib arm. In OLIE, the hazard ratio was 1.28 at the primary analysis and 0.93 at the final analysis, so no difference either way (paper, full text).
- In open data from 81 children's and young adults' tumours at diagnosis, about a quarter (23.5%) carried a clear amplification of VEGFA or the KDR/PDGFRA/KIT region by a strict threshold, and nearly half (46.9%) by a looser one (our calculation; see `reports/Copy-number changes and the kinase-pill biomarker.md`). A US cancer-centre sequencing cohort of 66 patients reports at least 40% (VEGFA 24% of 59 evaluable patients; paper, full text; PMID 31175097). The two regions overlapped in 4 of 81 TARGET-OS tumours, so they should be counted once per patient.
- We found no clinical study that checks whether those patients are the ones who respond. If they are, the pills could be given to the patients they help, and not to the rest.
- **Record MYC status too.** MYC is amplified in 32.1% (26 of 81) of TARGET-OS tumours (our calculation, copy-number report). In 105 children and young adults, MYC amplification went with 3-year survival of 27% against 74% (adjusted HR 3.4; paper, abstract only; *Cancer* 2025, PMID 41199591), and MYC-driven mouse osteosarcoma cells were about 8 times less sensitive to cabozantinib in the laboratory (preprint, abstract only; bioRxiv 2026; fold change our calculation). The amplification test should therefore enter MYC as a variable, so that a MYC effect is not mistaken for a VEGFA or 4q12 effect (our inference). In TARGET-OS, however, MYC amplification did not predict survival (amplified against not, HR 1.18, 95% CI 0.54–2.56; at 8 or more copies, adjusted HR 1.45, 0.65–3.23; our calculation, `reports/Do the leads predict survival.md`), which argues against an effect as large as HR 3.4; it remains worth recording.
- Ipsen's CabOSTar maintenance trial (NCT06341712) posted results on 29 October 2026, but they contain no efficacy data: the planned analyses "were not performed as the study was terminated early" (registry).

**Cheapest test:** genotype archived tumour or banked blood DNA from the randomized trials (SARC024, REGOBONE, OLIE, OAIE) and the single-arm trials (CABONE, COG ADVL1622, ITCC-050). Together they treated about 309 osteosarcoma patients with a pill. If 60% have usable DNA, pooling them gives about 97% power to detect a halving of the progression hazard in amplified tumours; proving that amplification predicts benefit *from the pill* needs roughly 400 or more randomized patients, more than the four randomized trials hold (242) (our calculation, copy-number report). Using the randomized trials allows a true drug-versus-control comparison, though underpowered.

**Partners:**
- SARC (SARC024).
- The French Sarcoma Group (REGOBONE).
- Eisai and the ITCC consortium (OLIE, ITCC-050).
- COG (ADVL1622).
- MD Anderson Cancer Center's NIH-funded programme for genomically selected drug testing in osteosarcoma patient-derived models (U01 CA199221; registry).

**Details:** `reports/Why kinase-inhibitor pills work in osteosarcoma.md`.

### 3. Publish the pooled control dataset

**Why:**
- The dataset holds registry results, published trials and osteosarcoma arms from basket trials (`reports/Osteosarcoma pooled control dataset.md`). It shows that the 12% four-month benchmark, which COG built from 96 patients given single agents for measurable disease (paper, full text; PMID 27400942), does not apply to chemotherapy combinations: ifosfamide and etoposide alone keep 42–66% of patients progression-free at four months in the two randomized control arms (paper, full text).
- A short paper or preprint would make the dataset citable.

**Partners:**
- FOSTER, the European osteosarcoma consortium, and its funders.
- The COG bone tumour committee.
- The Pediatric Cancer Data Commons.

**We can do most of this ourselves**, ideally with a scientist co-author.

### 4. Pet-dog study of FAK or DKK1 blockade: support and learn from the existing programmes

**Why:** these remain the best-supported drug targets against lung metastasis that have not reached a human osteosarcoma trial, and the full-text check strengthened both. It also showed that dog work on both is already planned or under way, so this lead is now about supporting those programmes and filling the gaps they leave.
- **FAK:**
  - Defactinib itself reduced lung metastasis in mice. In an orthotopic model (143B cells engineered to overexpress LOX, injected into the shin bone), 2 of 6 treated mice developed lung metastases against 6 of 6 controls (P = 0.03), with no change in primary tumour weight (paper, full text; Genes & Diseases 2023, PMID 37013056). It is small, uses one engineered cell line, and reports an unusually low dose (50 μg/kg twice daily).
  - Defactinib is FDA-approved (accelerated approval, 8 May 2025, with avutometinib for KRAS-mutant low-grade serous ovarian cancer; registry).
  - Colorado State University's veterinary cancer centre lists a study adding defactinib to losartan and toceranib in dogs with lung-metastatic osteosarcoma (press release only: web listing; status and results not found). Its NIH FAK grant (R37 CA303990, to August 2030) also plans a FAK-inhibitor study with platinum chemotherapy in dogs with naturally occurring osteosarcoma (registry).
- **DKK1:**
  - The best mouse result held up on full-text reading: lung metastasis in 2 of 10 treated against 7 of 9 untreated (paper, full text; PMID 39805917).
  - An independent group reported the same direction with an oral compound (paper, full text; PMID 39280613).
  - Texas A&M's NIH grant (R21 CA286523, to December 2027) is developing a canine version of its DKK1 morpholino, with the stated goal of trials in client-owned dogs with osteosarcoma (registry).
  - We found no report of the clinical anti-DKK1 antibody, DKN-01 (sirexatamab), being given to dogs.
- The lung metastasis report adds a reason to favour FAK: it sits where several metastasis signals converge (IL-8, CXCL1, integrins).

**What is still open:** a post-amputation study aimed at preventing metastasis (the existing FAK dog study treats dogs whose lung metastases are already visible); measuring FAK, activated FAK and DKK1 in banked dog tumours; and replication of the DKK1 mouse results in a model that neither original laboratory owns.

**Partners:**
- Colorado State University's veterinary cancer centre and its NIH-funded FAK group.
- Texas A&M's NIH-funded canine DKK1 morpholino programme (College of Medicine with the Department of Small Animal Clinical Sciences).
- Albert Einstein College of Medicine's DKK-1 metastasis programme (NIH R01 CA262802, funded through June 2026; registry).
- The NCI Comparative Oncology Program.

**Details:** `briefs/Brief - pet dog trial of FAK or DKK1 blockade.md`.

### 5. LRRC15 as a surface target: follow and inform the trials already running

**Why:**
- In 88 patient tumours compared with 54 normal tissues, LRRC15 was made in almost every osteosarcoma and very little in vital organs, and it stays in the top 3 under every variant of the method. The same method ranked B7-H3, the current leading target, 15th, and HER2, which failed, near the bottom (our calculation, `reports/New surface targets for osteosarcoma.md`).
- **Protein confirmed.** LRRC15 is now measured as protein in patient tumours by independent groups: 63 of 69 osteosarcomas stained, 74% at 2+ or 3+ (paper, abstract only; Cui 2020, PMID 32902907; percentage our calculation); 18 paediatric primaries with a median H-score of 113.5 (paper, full text; O'Reilly 2026, PMID 41627173); staining that matched RNA in a spatial cohort including relapses (paper, full text; Moquin-Beaudry 2026, PMID 42399228); and a top hit in the proteomes of 22 patient-derived xenografts (paper, full text; Mooney 2026, PMID 42647168). An expert workshop names an LRRC15 conjugate with an osteosarcoma-suited payload as promising (paper, abstract only; PMID 42613365). Not yet shown: positivity rates in lung metastases and relapses, and protein in human growth plate and marrow. PubMed returns 11 papers for "LRRC15 AND osteosarcoma" (registry, November 19, 2026).
- **Companies have already moved.** Three treatment trials target it: Lantheus's radiolabelled antibody LNTH-2403 in relapsed or refractory osteosarcoma only (Lu-TARGO, NCT07357519, phase 1/2, recruiting since February 2026); SOT106 in osteosarcoma and soft-tissue sarcoma (NCT07801222, not yet recruiting); and ZL-6201 in sarcoma and other tumours (NCT07374848, recruiting) (registry). A fourth record, NCT07272642 (Radiopharm Theranostics, an imaging and safety study in carcinomas), is not yet recruiting; it names its drug "LNTH-2403 (177Lu-DOTA-DUNP19)", the same antibody as Lu-TARGO (registry, November 19, 2026). Following these trials matters more than starting anything new, which is why this lead scores low on "open".

**Cheapest test:** one tissue microarray of primaries with paired lung metastases and relapses, plus paediatric growth plate and marrow, stained for LRRC15 (clone EPR8188(2), H-score and % positive cells). Before that, ask Lantheus and SOTIO whether they will publish their LRRC15 screening data. The same array could include TMEM119, which our RNA filter excluded but which shows surface staining in patient tumours (paper, full text; PMID 42647168).

**Partners:**
- The LRRC15 trial sponsors (Lantheus for LNTH-2403; the SOT106 and ZL-6201 developers).
- Any group with an osteosarcoma tissue microarray, such as COG's biobank or FOSTER's sample programme.

**Details:** `reports/New surface targets for osteosarcoma.md`; `reports/Whats new in osteosarcoma research.md` (protein check).

### 6. PTK7 as a surface target: stain patient tumours

**Why:**
- PTK7 ranked near the top of the same RNA screen, but its rank is sensitive to method: it falls from 64th to 551st when all 54 normal tissues are used (our calculation).
- Three antibody-drug conjugates are in active early trials for other cancers, plus IDEAYA's B7-H3 × PTK7 bispecific conjugate IDE034 (NCT07503808, recruiting), and Lilly's LY4175408 (NCT07046923) is possibly PTK7-directed (inferred: the record does not name a target but excludes prior PTK7 conjugates of the same payload class). Pfizer's earlier PF-06647020 trials are completed, and AbbVie's later trial of the same drug, cofetuzumab pelidotin (NCT04189614), was terminated (registry). Cofetuzumab caused grade 3 or worse neutropenia in 25% of patients (paper, abstract only; PMID 34083232).
- **Protein evidence is partial:** two patient-derived xenografts (one from a relapse) and two cell lines stained positive, and a paediatric normal-tissue array was negative or weak in every organ (paper, full text; PMID 37343516). No series of patient tumours has been stained. Fewer than five osteosarcoma papers in our tagged set name PTK7 (our calculation), and PubMed returns one record for "PTK7 AND osteosarcoma" (registry, November 20, 2026).

**Cheapest test:** add PTK7 to the lead 5 tissue array, stained with the clinical-trial assay and cut-off used in the lung-cancer trials. If patient tumours stain, an existing conjugate could be tested in osteosarcoma models, which is cheaper than building a drug for a new antigen.

**Partners:**
- The companies developing PTK7 drugs (HWK-007, KIVU-107, DAY301, IDE034).
- The tissue-array holders named under lead 5.

**Details:** `reports/New surface targets for osteosarcoma.md`.

### 7. Randomized trial after lung-metastasis surgery, run inside a platform

**Why:**
- Single-arm trials in this setting cannot separate a drug's effect from who gets enrolled. Two very different drugs, denosumab and dinutuximab, gave almost identical results: 10 of 38 (26%) and 11 of 39 (28%) event-free at 12 months (paper, abstract only; PMIDs 41159913, 35809374). The COG benchmark for this setting is 20% at 12 months, and its 10–34% is the 95% confidence interval from one 42-patient trial, AOST0221 (paper, full text; PMID 27400942).
- A November 2026 COG preprint, not yet peer-reviewed, pooled 99 patients with resected lung-only relapse from three COG phase 2 trials: 22% event-free at 12 months (95% CI 15–31%), 14–29% across trials. The authors conclude that single-arm trials "will only be able to identify large effect sizes" and that smaller effects "will require randomization" (preprint, abstract only; Chou et al., medRxiv, doi 10.64898/2026.11.13.26368045). That is independent support for this lead.
- A randomized phase 3 has now shown benefit in relapse and is published: ARTEMIS-011, the B7-H3 conjugate risvutatug rezetecan against gemcitabine–docetaxel, median progression-free survival 5.5 against 1.4 months, HR 0.33 (95% CI 0.20–0.55), with serious treatment-related events in 64% against 29% (paper, abstract only; Ann Oncol 2026, PMID 42933637). It was open-label, run only in China and in measurable disease, but it shows randomized relapse trials in osteosarcoma can recruit and finish.
- A randomized, placebo-controlled saracatinib trial was tried (NCT00752206). It ran from March 2009 to December 2017 (primary completion August 2017), enrolled 38 patients (37 analysed) and was closed for slow accrual and futility; progression-free survival was 19.4 against 8.6 months, not significant (P = 0.47) (registry; paper, abstract only, PMID 32398945).
- So the obstacle is recruitment. Across osteosarcoma trials, recruitment problems ended five to six times as many trials as lack of efficacy (our calculation, `reports/Osteosarcoma clinical trials landscape.md`). The question needs a large platform such as FOSTER's new one (NCT07871864, recruiting since October 2026, about 530 planned; registry), possibly enriched for patients whose metastases did not respond to chemotherapy before surgery.

**Partners:** FOSTER; the COG bone tumour committee.

### 8. Treatment adapted on ctDNA blood tests

**Why:**
- In osteosarcoma, tumour DNA was detectable in the blood of 57% of patients at diagnosis, and the risk of relapse and death rose with its level (paper, abstract only; Shulman 2018, PMID 30131550). The clean survival split in that study, and the published validation from the LEOPARD study, are in Ewing sarcoma (paper, abstract only; PMID 42743455). LEOPARD's osteosarcoma results are not yet published; its primary completion date is January 2027 (registry, NCT06068075).
- The lung metastasis report adds a specific use: ctDNA could trigger earlier lung surgery, with complete removal as the outcome.
- We found no osteosarcoma trial that changes treatment based on ctDNA. Earlier detection by scans alone has not been shown to improve survival.

**Partners:** Dana-Farber Cancer Institute's NIH-funded paediatric sarcoma ctDNA programme, which leads LEOPARD (R37 CA244355, K08 CA290061; registry).

### 9. Block the IL-8 receptor (CXCR1/2)

**Why:**
- An anti-IL-8 antibody reduced lung metastasis in mice (paper, abstract only; PMID 38302407).
- Signalling through the CXCR2 receptor helps osteosarcoma cells stick to lung blood vessels (paper, abstract only; PMIDs 32079335, 34760697).
- Oral CXCR1/2 blockers exist (SX-682, reparixin, ladarixin). None has a registered efficacy trial in osteosarcoma on ClinicalTrials.gov (registry), but Colorado State University lists a dog trial adding ladarixin to losartan and toceranib after treatment for non-metastatic osteosarcoma (press release only: web listing; status not confirmed). That dog study is the natural first source of evidence, which lowers this lead's "open" score.
- This fits naturally alongside lead 1.

**Partners:** Colorado State University's veterinary cancer centre; the partners for lead 1.

### 10. Add metastasis to an existing aspirin-versus-blood-thinner trial

**Why:**
- Platelets help osteosarcoma cells survive in the bloodstream and settle in the lung.
- Blocking tissue factor, LPAR1 or podoplanin reduced lung metastasis in mice (paper, abstract only; PMIDs 26763081, 34302117, 35381070). In this project's repurposing screen, platelet drugs score in the same band as the best new candidates (abciximab 69.3, vorapaxar 68.2, aspirin 61.7 of 100; our calculation).
- An oral LPAR1 blocker, admilparant, is in phase 3 for lung fibrosis (registry; NCT06003426, NCT06025578).
- Separately, a Massachusetts General Hospital trial is randomizing about 2,868 orthopaedic oncology patients, with a primary bone tumour stratum, to aspirin or enoxaparin to prevent blood clots (NCT03244020, enrolling by invitation, completion July 2028; registry). It enrols adults only, after pelvic or lower-limb surgery, so few osteosarcoma patients will be in it, which is why its impact score is 1. Recording metastasis-free survival in its bone-sarcoma patients would still cost little.

**Partners:** the NCT03244020 study team at Massachusetts General Hospital.

### 11. Chase the remaining unreported trials

**Why:** four large randomized trials, together planning about 1,080 patients, still have no public results (registry; our calculation, `reports/Osteosarcoma clinical trials landscape.md`):
- a 362-patient PD-L1 antibody maintenance phase 3 (ZKAB001, NCT04359550; its earlier phase I/II in 21 patients is published, PMID 36469563);
- a 400-patient lithium trial (NCT01669369);
- an Endostar trial (NCT01002092);
- the Brazilian metastatic metronomic trial (NCT03063983).

Also without public results: an inhaled lipid cisplatin phase 2 completed in 2018 with 28 patients (NCT01650090; registry). The earlier phase Ib/IIa in 19 patients is published (PMID 23255417) and is a different study. Beyond these, about 21 finished trials with about 810 patients have no results we could find, many of them supportive-care or imaging studies (our calculation).

The completed paediatric trial of the CD248 antibody MORAb-004 (NCT01748721) is not unreported: it is published (Pediatr Blood Cancer 2018, PMID 29292843; 27 patients), and a randomized soft-tissue sarcoma trial of the same antibody was negative (PMID 31034598). Only its registry results are missing.

**Partners:** each trial's sponsor. A short, polite email asking whether results exist.

### 12. Re-analyse dog genomes from NCI's canine trials

**Why:**
- NCBI's Sequence Read Archive holds 743 open canine osteosarcoma whole-genome sequencing experiments (747 runs; registry). These are not 743 dogs: about 91 dogs have deep tumour–normal genomes, 55 of them from NCI's COTC021/022 trials, whose outcomes are known (our calculation, `reports/Dog genome re-analysis scope.md`). Many of the other runs are shallow or blood-only.
- Checking whether the 2025 genome-shattering pattern predicts survival in dogs would strengthen dogs as a model for testing therapies.

**Partners:** the NCI Comparative Oncology Program and the NCI Center for Cancer Research's genetics programme.

**Details:** `reports/Dog genome re-analysis scope.md`.

### 13. AKT blockers (capivasertib) with chemotherapy

**Why:**
- In the NCI paediatric preclinical testing programme, two AKT inhibitors, MK-2206 and GSK690693, each prolonged event-free survival in all 6 osteosarcoma xenografts tested, more consistently than in any other tumour type, but shrank none (paper, abstract only; PMIDs 22102563, 20740623). An AKT inhibitor with cisplatin was synergistic in a patient-derived model (paper, abstract only; PMID 39994220).
- **No osteosarcoma paper tests capivasertib itself:** PubMed has none naming capivasertib or AZD5363 with osteosarcoma (registry, November 20, 2026). The lead is the AKT class; capivasertib is simply the approved oral AKT blocker (approved 2023 for a breast cancer subtype; registry). It has no osteosarcoma trial on ClinicalTrials.gov; the only hit is an adult all-comer basket (NCI-MATCH, NCT02465060) that does not list osteosarcoma (registry).
- **Contradictions:** AKT inhibition boosted cisplatin only in p53-normal cells, and most osteosarcomas have lost p53 function (paper, abstract only; PMID 25482935). Drugs one step downstream (mTOR) have 21 osteosarcoma trials, largely negative (our calculation). The AKT1 literature carries an "elevated" paper-mill flag (our calculation). It ranked first among new candidates in the repurposing screen (score 84.4 of 100; our calculation).

**Cheapest test:** check osteosarcoma cell lines' dependence on AKT1 and AKT2 in the public DepMap CRISPR data, which we can do ourselves at no cost; then capivasertib with cisplatin or doxorubicin in an existing patient-derived panel, with p53 status recorded.

**Partners:** NCI's paediatric preclinical testing programme, which ran the original AKT xenograft panels; groups holding osteosarcoma patient-derived panels, such as the MD Anderson programme named under lead 2.

**Details:** `reports/Approved drugs worth testing in osteosarcoma.md`.

### 14. CXCR4 blockers (mavorixafor) against lung metastasis

**Why:**
- Lung tissue is rich in CXCL12, the signal CXCR4 responds to. CXCR4 RNA was found in 63% of 47 high-grade tumours and went with shorter survival (paper, abstract only; PMID 15814634), and CXCR4 protein was positive in 33% of primaries but 67% of matched lung metastases in 30 patients (paper, abstract only; PMID 16528367). Blocking peptides cut lung metastases in mice, by 50% in one study (paper, abstract only; PMIDs 18071913, 15701832).
- An oral blocker, mavorixafor, was approved in 2024, and plerixafor since 2008 (registry). Neither has an anti-metastasis trial in osteosarcoma on ClinicalTrials.gov; plerixafor appears only in a withdrawn stem-cell-mobilisation study (NCT03612466; registry).
- **Contradictions:** the 50% reduction held consistently only when cells were treated before injection (PMID 18071913). Blocking the pathway can increase primary growth (PMID 27300512) and can stop killer T cells entering the tumour (PMID 29735547), and a second receptor, CXCR7, can take over (PMID 28468584) (all paper, abstract only). The immune question could make it harmful.
- **Patient data point the other way.** In 223 patient tumours, strong CXCR4 and CXCL12 staining went with a better outcome and fewer metastases (paper, abstract only; PMID 22173290), the opposite of the mouse results. The literature knowledge graph flagged this as a genuine mice-versus-patients disagreement (`reports/Contradictions and hidden links in the osteosarcoma literature.md`). Any test should first settle which way CXCR4 points in patients.
- **Our own test found no support.** Engine cycle 2 compared 29 lung metastases with 51 primary tumours in six public datasets. CXCR4 RNA leaned higher in lung metastases (+0.43 standard deviations, 95% CI −0.05 to +0.91), but the lean disappeared once immune cells in the sample were accounted for (+0.06), and four patients with both tumours showed no difference. The pre-registered verdict was inconclusive (our calculation; `reports/Engine cycle 2 - do lung metastases carry more CXCR4.md`). Whole-tissue RNA cannot settle this; staining tumour cells in matched pairs could.

**Cheapest test:** oral mavorixafor after removal of the primary tumour in an immune-competent mouse model, measuring lung metastases and tumour T cells. The model must metastasise after the primary is removed: the lung metastasis report notes that in the intratibial K7M2 model the lungs are seeded almost at once, so removing the injected leg 30 minutes later did not change metastatic burden (paper, abstract only; PMID 29601385).

**Partners:** the mouse-model groups named under lead 1, which already study lung colonization; the drug's maker, for supply.

**Details:** `reports/Approved drugs worth testing in osteosarcoma.md`; `reports/Stopping lung metastasis in osteosarcoma.md`.
