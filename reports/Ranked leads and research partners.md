# For Johnny: the ten best leads, and who could test them

*November 14, 2026. This ranks every lead from the project's five reports and matches each to the research groups best placed to act on it. Partners are drawn from public NIH grant records (`data/nih_osteosarcoma_projects.csv`), ClinicalTrials.gov sponsors and the published literature. Nobody named here has been contacted or has endorsed anything in this document.*

## How leads were scored

Each lead is scored 1 to 3 on four things, for a total out of 12:

- **Evidence:** how well the underlying finding holds up after checking.
- **Open:** whether it is still undone. A lead already under way scores low.
- **Feasibility:** how cheaply and quickly it could be done.
- **Impact:** whether it could change outcomes for children with metastatic or relapsed disease, the patients osteosarcoma kills.

Leads 1 to 4 tie on score. They are ordered by which most directly tests a new treatment, and lead 9 is placed last among the top scores because it helps patients only indirectly, by protecting other research from bad evidence.

**Deliberately dropped: MDM2 inhibitors.** MDM2 looked like the strongest untested target by paper count, but the close reading found the evidence rests on one cell line, and MDM2 amplification is common only in low-grade osteosarcoma that surgery usually cures. That negative result is worth stating plainly so others don't chase it.

## The leads in detail

### 1. Pet-dog trial of a FAK or DKK1 drug

**Why:** FAK and DKK1 have the best evidence of any untested target. Blocking either reduced tumour growth or metastasis in mice, and drugs exist: defactinib (FAK, FDA-approved in 2025 for an ovarian cancer) and DKN-01 (DKK1, in trials for gastric cancer). Neither drug has been tested in osteosarcoma models, and neither target has any dog data. Dogs get osteosarcoma about ten times as often as people, and trials in pet dogs after amputation can answer the metastasis question in one to two years.

**Partners:**
- Daniel Regan, Colorado State University: holds the NIH grant on FAK inhibition in osteosarcoma, at a veterinary school.
- David Loeb, Albert Einstein College of Medicine: holds the NIH grant on targeting DKK-1 to prevent metastasis. Carl Gregory, Texas A&M: holds a grant on DKK-1 antisense therapy.
- NCI Comparative Oncology Program (Amy LeBlanc), which runs multi-site pet-dog trials; UC Davis (Robert Rebhun) and Tufts (Cheryl London), which run NIH-funded canine osteosarcoma studies.

**Pitch:** "Your target has the strongest untested evidence in a 33,000-paper analysis of osteosarcoma, and a clinical drug exists. Would a pet-dog study of the clinical drug be feasible, and what would it cost?"

### 2. Open pooled control dataset

**Why:** Small relapse trials can't tell whether a drug works without reliable control numbers, and FOSTER names this as a gap. This project has already built a first version from 41 trials' posted results. Adding published results, starting with the COG trials pooled in 2016, would make it citable.

**Partners:** the FOSTER consortium (Europe); the Children's Oncology Group bone tumour committee; the Pediatric Cancer Data Commons. **This one we can largely do ourselves**, then offer it to them.

### 3. Recover lost trial results

**Update (November 14, 2026):** the first pass is done. Three "missing" trials were in fact published, all negative, including the larger Brazilian metronomic maintenance trial (296 children randomized, no benefit). Still unreported: the 362-patient PD-L1 antibody maintenance phase 3 (ZKAB001), the 400-patient lithium trial, the Endostar trial and the Brazilian metastatic trial. See the trials report.

**Partners:** the remaining trials' sponsors and investigators: Lee's Pharmaceutical (ZKAB001), Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center (lithium), Simcere (Endostar) and GRAACC in Brazil. A short, polite question asking whether results exist is the next step.

### 4. Randomized trial in completely resected relapse

**Why:** In the posted results, two drugs with completely different mechanisms left almost the same share of patients event-free at 12 months (26% and 28%), both within the historical range. Single-arm trials in this setting can't separate a drug's effect from who gets enrolled. Only a randomized trial can.

**Partners:** COG bone tumour committee; FOSTER, whose new trial platform (NCT07871864) could host a randomized arm. **Our role:** advocacy, backed by the pooled data from lead 2.

### 5. A randomized test of kinase-inhibitor pills

**Why:** These pills (cabozantinib, regorafenib, lenvatinib, apatinib) are the only drug class clearly above the relapse benchmark: about 52% of patients progression-free at four months when given alone, against about 12% for older single agents. Combinations with chemotherapy must be judged against chemotherapy alone, which does better than the old benchmark; in the one randomized trial where the gap was large (OAIE, China), apatinib + IE gave 66% against 42%. Yet COG's first-line cabozantinib trial (AOST2032) is suspended, and the industry maintenance trial CabOSTar closed after 10 patients.

**Partners:** COG; FOSTER; families and foundations such as MIB Agents, whose voice carries weight on why trials stall. **Our role:** documenting the recruitment problem with the trials data.

### 6. Treatment adapted on ctDNA blood tests

**Why:** Tumour DNA in the blood predicts relapse, and the LEOPARD study has validated it at diagnosis. No trial yet changes treatment based on it, and ctDNA has received only $3.3M of NIH osteosarcoma funding in eleven years.

**Partners:** Brian Crompton and David Shulman, Dana-Farber, who hold the NIH ctDNA grants and lead LEOPARD.

### 7. Re-analyse public dog tumour genomes

**Why:** A 2025 study found a specific genome-shattering pattern in about half of human osteosarcomas. Nobody has looked for it in dogs, and 24 dog genomes are openly available. Finding it would strengthen dogs as a model for testing therapies; not finding it would be important too.

**Partners:** Paul Meltzer (NCI, osteosarcoma genome); the NCI Comparative Oncology Program. **We can do the analysis ourselves**, with a genomics collaborator to check it.

### 8. Approved IL-6 or IL-8 blockers against lung metastasis

**Why:** Mouse work shows lung tissue drives IL-6 and IL-8 signals that help osteosarcoma colonize the lung, and IL-6 has 270 osteosarcoma papers. Tocilizumab, an IL-6 blocker, is approved and available as cheaper biosimilars. No osteosarcoma trial has tested blocking either signal.

**Partners:** Ryan Roberts, Nationwide Children's, who holds an NIH grant on mechanisms of metastatic colonization in osteosarcoma.

### 9. Publish a paper-mill risk flag

**Why:** MicroRNA-type osteosarcoma papers are retracted nine times as often as other papers, and some trial targets lean on them (37% of CDK6's papers). A public, per-gene flag would help any researcher judge evidence before building on it.

**Partners:** research-integrity groups and journals. **We can do this ourselves**; the data is already built.

### 10. A CDK2 inhibitor in cyclin-E1-high models

**Why:** Cyclin E1 protein is high in about 61% of tumours, and CDK2 inhibitors now exist, but none has been tested in an osteosarcoma model. The evidence is weak, so this is a cheap laboratory test, not a trial.

**Partners:** Richard Gorlick, MD Anderson, who runs an NIH-funded programme testing genomically selected drugs in patient-derived osteosarcoma models.
