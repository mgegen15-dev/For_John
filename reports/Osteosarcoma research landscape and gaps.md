# Osteosarcoma research landscape and gaps

*Last revised November 19, 2026 (new-evidence update; see the change log). Built from six sets of sourced research notes (`research_notes/`), a direct check of every trial ID on ClinicalTrials.gov on November 13, 2026 (`research_notes/Osteosarcoma gaps verification pass/`), and an independent audit of the report's claims against primary sources on November 14, 2026 (`research_notes/Audit/audit_A_gaps_leads_brief.md`). Not medical advice.*

## Summary

Survival for children and young adults with osteosarcoma has barely changed since the mid-1980s: roughly 70–76% at five years when the cancer has not spread, about a quarter when it has, and under 30% after relapse. The main obstacles look like design and infrastructure more than a shortage of ideas: the tumour's genome is shattered, with almost no recurring drug target; trials are small, single-arm and judged against weak benchmarks; and many stop because they cannot recruit. Neuroblastoma, a childhood cancer with a similar genome, improved by pooling data, assigning risk before treatment and asking one randomized question at a time, and those habits can transfer even where its drugs did not. The newest osteosarcoma results are promising but thin: a B7-H3 antibody-drug conjugate beat chemotherapy on progression in a randomized phase 3 trial, now published, though survival data are immature and we have read only the abstract; and a HER2 vaccine's claim still rests on a company report and a historical comparison. Several of the most useful remaining gaps need data and organisation rather than a laboratory, which is where an independent team can help.

**How to read the labels.** After key numbers we say where they come from:

- **(registry)**: an official database or agency record, such as ClinicalTrials.gov, NIH RePORTER, an FDA or NICE notice, or national cancer statistics.
- **(paper, full text)**: read in the full published paper.
- **(paper, abstract only)**: read only in the abstract.
- **(press release only)**: from a company announcement, conference coverage, advocacy material or news report, with no peer-reviewed paper.
- **(our calculation)**: counted or computed by this project.
- **(preprint, abstract only)**: read only in the abstract of a preprint, which has not been peer-reviewed.

Every gap and transfer idea in this report is a hypothesis drawn from the evidence, not an established finding.

## How far the evidence behind this report can be trusted

The report synthesises six sets of research notes. Every trial ID was then checked on ClinicalTrials.gov, and the key figures and citations were re-checked against PubMed, Europe PMC, NIH RePORTER and agency pages by an independent audit. Most figures still rest on abstracts rather than full papers, and the labels above say which. Some recent results exist only as conference or company reports.

Where the original notes disagreed, the checks settled the question:

- **HER2 positivity** has no single answer: about 30% in one review and 42.2% pooled across 16 studies of 934 patients, with single studies ranging from 12% to over 60% depending on the staining method (paper, full text; PMC10706029, PMC4907646).
- **The gpNMB and HER2 antibody-drug conjugates failed.** COG AOST1521 (glembatumumab vedotin) produced 1 partial response in 22 patients, with no link between gpNMB level and response (paper, abstract only; PMID 31586757). The trastuzumab deruxtecan osteosarcoma trial, PEPN1924, did not go beyond its first stage: 41 of 50 screened tumours passed a lenient HER2 staining cut-off, but only 1 of 9 treated patients had stable disease past 24 weeks (paper, abstract only; PMID 42131734).
- **NCT07428993 is St. Jude's 3CAR4OS trial** of B7-H3 CAR-T cells as consolidation in newly diagnosed high-risk disease (registry).
- **MIB Agents' grant totals** are both real and cumulative: $2.5M to 32 investigators by June 2025 and $2.9M to 37 by June 2026 (press release only).

The original notes did not search several topics: patient-derived xenografts, organoids, zebrafish, CDK4/6 inhibitors, oncolytic viruses, macrophage-directed therapy, NK cells, survivorship statistics, outcomes in low- and middle-income countries, and transfers of ideas *into* neuroblastoma. Silence on those topics here is not evidence of absence. "We found no" means no result in the registry and literature searches we ran.

## Biology and genomics

*Links for each item are in `research_notes/Osteosarcoma and neuroblastoma research gaps/osteosarcoma_biology_genomics.md`.*

**Where the tumour starts.** Mouse studies point to a bone-forming cell somewhere between an uncommitted stem cell and an early osteoblast. Deleting Trp53 in committed osteoblasts gives osteosarcoma in nearly all mice, and the amount of Rb1 lost shifts which tumour types appear. One study argues that earlier cell-of-origin claims may be artefacts of how the mouse tools switch on, and one relevant paper was retracted in February 2026. The question is not settled.

**Growth and risk.** Risk tracks growth. A height genetic score raised risk by about 10% per standard deviation (OR 1.10; 864 cases, 1,879 controls; paper, abstract only; PMID 30311632), and birth weight of 4,046 g or more raised it by about 35% (OR 1.35; paper, abstract only; PMID 21465145). We found no genetic study testing whether the IGF-1 growth pathway itself is causal.

**A shattered genome with one constant.** Osteosarcoma has few recurring point mutations and a great deal of structural damage.
- TP53 is the only gene consistently altered, often by rearrangement rather than small mutation. In Chen 2014 (20 discovery and 14 validation tumours), TP53 was altered in every discovery tumour, and 9 of the 20 carried a translocation in its first intron (paper, abstract only; PMID 24703847).
- Rearrangements in the first intron of TP53 appeared in 16% of osteosarcomas (46 of 288) and in none of 1,090 other tumours (paper, abstract only; Ribi 2015, PMID 25762628).
- A 2025 Cell paper describes a shattering-and-amplification mechanism (loss-translocation-amplification, or LTA, chromothripsis) in about half of high-grade tumours, ongoing shattering in subclones in 74%, and genome-wide loss of heterozygosity as a strong predictor of outcome. It also shows that a single double-strand break can inactivate TP53 and amplify an oncogene at once, linking the TP53 hotspot to the mechanism (paper, abstract only; PMID 39814020).
- A "BRCA-like" DNA-repair signature was reported in 2015, but it rests on exome data and does not line up with mutations in repair genes.

**When the damage happens.** Studies disagree. A 2023 study of 8 patients places the complex rearrangements before diagnosis; the 2025 paper finds shattering still under way. Another study finds metastases seeded by a single clone, with much of the later diversity caused by cisplatin itself, which confounds every study of post-chemotherapy tissue.

**The tumour's surroundings.** Single-cell maps show a macrophage-heavy, T-cell-poor environment that chemotherapy pushes further toward immune suppression. The first large map profiled 100,987 cells from 11 tumour samples (7 primary, 2 recurrent, 2 lung metastases; paper, abstract only; Zhou 2020, PMID 33303760), and a 2024 spatial atlas followed. At least 8–10 independent single-cell cohorts now exist, but many later papers re-analyse the same early public datasets, so apparent replication is partly dataset reuse.

**Subtypes and inherited risk.** Several subtype schemes exist, drawn largely from TARGET data, alongside independent methylation and multi-omic schemes; we found no head-to-head comparison. Inherited risk is higher than once thought: 281 of 1,004 patients (28.0%) carried a harmful germline variant, against 12.1% of controls (paper, abstract only; Mirabello 2020, PMID 32191290). Gene panels cannot see the TP53 intron rearrangements, so the true rate may be higher (hypothesis).

**Models.** Dogs develop osteosarcoma naturally with similar genome complexity (59 dogs; paper, abstract only; Gardner 2019, PMID 31341965), and most die of lung metastases within a year or two even with chemotherapy (review; PMID 32165755). A 2026 preprint from the Jacks lab sequences engineered-mouse tumours; whether any model reproduces the 2025 shattering mechanism is still unclear.

**Drug targets from the biology.** Laboratory work supports dependence on the ATR, CHK1 and WEE1 DNA-damage checkpoints, but one negative result (an ATR plus WEE1 pair failing in a cell line) is rarely cited. In patients, the olaparib plus ceralasertib trial (NCT04417062) left 5 of 34 event-free at four months, and 4 of 10 at 12 months in its resected-relapse cohort (registry; see `reports/Osteosarcoma pooled control dataset.md`).

## One step-change in 1986, then forty years of negative trials

The only large jump in outcomes came from adding chemotherapy after surgery. The MIOS trial randomized 36 patients and found two-year relapse-free survival of **66% with chemotherapy against 17% without** (paper, abstract only; [Link 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3520317/)). Since then the curve has been flat ([NCI PDQ](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK65736/)). US five-year relative survival is 76% for osteosarcoma that has not spread beyond the bone and 24% for distant disease (registry: SEER, via the [American Cancer Society](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/osteosarcoma/detection-diagnosis-staging/survival-rates.html)). After relapse, five-year survival is under 30% (paper, abstract only; [systematic review, PMID 40731087](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40731087/)).

The largest trial ever run, EURAMOS-1, registered 2,260 patients and answered both of its questions negatively. Adding ifosfamide and etoposide for patients whose tumours responded poorly gave an event-free survival hazard ratio of 0.98 (0.78–1.23) while doubling grade 4 non-blood toxicity from 12% to 24% (paper, abstract only; [Lancet Oncol 2016, PMID 27569442](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27569442/)). Adding interferon for good responders gave a hazard ratio of 0.83 with a confidence interval crossing 1, but only 271 of 357 assigned patients started the drug and 105 of those stopped early (paper, abstract only; [Bielack 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26033801/)). Two inferences follow. Poor response looks like fixed biology that more of the same drug class cannot overcome. And tumour necrosis at surgery predicts outcome but does not tell doctors what to change, partly because it is measured once and late.

Mifamurtide, an immune stimulant, remains unresolved. Its trial (INT-0133) reported an overall-survival hazard ratio of 0.71 (0.52–0.96) and six-year survival of 78% against 70% (paper, abstract only; [Meyers 2008, PMID 18235123](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18235123/)); NICE's appraisal gives 0.72 (0.53–0.97) ([NICE](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ta235/chapter/the-manufacturers-submission)). An FDA advisory panel nonetheless voted 12–2 in May 2007 that efficacy was not shown ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/fda-panel-said-idm-pharma-inc-s-bone-cancer-drug-is-not-effective)). A randomized confirmatory phase 2, Sarcome-13/OS2016 (NCT03643133, 60 patients), is active but no longer recruiting, with primary completion due in September 2026 and no results yet (registry).

Trial design compounded the problem. In measurable relapsed disease, the COG benchmark is four-month event-free survival of 12% from 96 patients pooled across seven single-agent trials (paper, full text; [Lagmay 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5012712)); a later ASCO abstract reports that three further trials together managed about 7% (paper, abstract only; not re-checked; [ASCO abstract](https://ace.asco.org/content/344819/abstract/197938)). Scan-based response is unreliable because the calcified tumour matrix does not shrink. A FOSTER review comparing 98 relapse trials from 2003–2016 with 71 from 2017–2022 found designs too heterogeneous to compare and no good historical cohorts (paper, abstract only; [PMID 37738712](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37738712/)). Most patients have too little tissue sampled at diagnosis for molecular work ([PMC7829872](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7829872)).

What does work after relapse is surgery. In 576 relapsed patients, five-year survival was 39% with a second complete surgical remission and 0% without (paper, abstract only; [Kempf-Bielack 2005, PMID 15659502](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15659502/)). In 219 patients with a single lung relapse, five-year survival reached 51.2%, and relapse chemotherapy "does not seem to improve survival" (paper, abstract only; [Mettmann 2023, PMID 37548393](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37548393/)). These series are retrospective and biased toward patients with less disease.

The cost of the standard regimen is lifelong: heart damage from doxorubicin, hearing and kidney damage from cisplatin. Sodium thiosulfate was approved in September 2022 to protect hearing (registry: [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-sodium-thiosulfate-reduce-risk-ototoxicity-associated-cisplatin-pediatric-patients)). The supporting evidence came from 77 children with localized tumours of several types in one trial (hearing loss 44% against 58%) and a hepatoblastoma-only trial, so osteosarcoma patients were only a fraction of the evidence, and questions about survival effects remain ([ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/news/september-2022/fda-approves-sodium-thiosulfate-to-reduce-the-risk-of-ototoxicity-associated-with-cisplatin); [OncLive](https://onclive.com/view/pedmark-reduces-ototoxicity-risk-in-cisplatin-treated-pediatric-solid-tumors)). The notes hold no quantified survivorship data.

## B7-H3 and a HER2 vaccine lead a frontier built on thin evidence

Checkpoint inhibitors, the drugs that transformed melanoma, have shown minimal activity in small osteosarcoma cohorts. Pembrolizumab produced a response in 1 of 22 patients (registry; [SARC028, NCT02301039](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02301039)). Pairing a checkpoint drug with a VEGFR2-blocking pill (camrelizumab plus apatinib) gave responses in 9 of 43 (20.9%) but missed its six-month progression-free target (50.9% against 60%) (paper, full text; [PMC7223462](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7223462)).

VEGFR2-blocking kinase pills are the most consistently active targeted drugs in relapse, with activity repeated across several trials. They are not the only treatments above the 12% benchmark: ifosfamide and etoposide alone keep 42–66% of patients progression-free at four months in two randomized control arms (paper, full text; pooled control dataset), and the B7-H3 conjugate in ARTEMIS-011 (below) beat gemcitabine–docetaxel in a randomized trial. Regorafenib extended median progression-free time from 1.7 to 3.6 months (HR 0.42; paper, abstract only; [SARC024, PMID 31013172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31013172/)), and cabozantinib produced responses in 5 of 42 osteosarcoma patients (12%) (paper, full text, as read for the kinase-pill report; [CABONE, PMID 32078813](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32078813/)). Pooled, pills given alone leave about 52.5% of patients progression-free at four months, or 41.4% counting CABONE by its own primary endpoint (our calculation; `reports/Osteosarcoma pooled control dataset.md`). None has shown a survival benefit. In the randomized OLIE trial, adding lenvatinib to ifosfamide and etoposide delayed progression but missed its primary endpoint; median survival was 11.9 against 17.4 months at the primary analysis (HR 1.28), and the hazard ratio was 0.93 (0.53–1.62) at the final analysis, so no survival difference either way (paper, full text; [PMC11581622](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11581622/)).

The pill class has also been tested at diagnosis. MAPAC (NCT04351308, Peking University People's Hospital, started May 2020) added apatinib or camrelizumab to chemotherapy in newly diagnosed patients with a poor response, though its registry status has been unknown since 2020 and no results are posted (registry). COG's AOST2032 (NCT05691478), cabozantinib added to standard chemotherapy, is the first large cooperative-group trial at diagnosis, with 1,122 patients planned; the registry lists it as suspended since October 2026 "to extend the observation period for current patients" (registry).

Borrowed targets have mostly disappointed. The neuroblastoma antibody dinutuximab, given after lung metastases were removed, left 11 of 39 patients event-free at 12 months against a required 16 (paper, abstract only; [AOST1421, PMID 35809374](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35809374/)). Denosumab, a bone drug, left 1 of 15 patients with measurable disease event-free at four months, with no responses, and 10 of 38 with resected disease event-free at 12 months (paper, abstract only; [AOST1321, PMID 41159913](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41159913/)). Zoledronate trended toward harm when added to first-line chemotherapy ([ASCO Post](https://www.ascopost.com/News/42704)). The gpNMB and HER2 conjugates failed, as described above.

Two results stand out, and both need caution. The B7-H3 antibody-drug conjugate from Hansoh and GSK (HS-20093) gave a confirmed response rate of 20.0% in 30 osteosarcoma patients in its phase 2 update at ESMO 2025, up from 17.4% in an early 2024 readout (press release only; [OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/b7-h3-targeted-adc-hn-20093-shows-strong-efficacy-is-still-safe-at-higher-dose-in-r-r-sarcomas)). Its randomized phase 3 in China, ARTEMIS-011 (NCT06935409, completed; registry), was presented at ESMO 2026 ([OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/ris-rez-improves-pfs-vs-gemcitabine-docetaxel-relapsed-refractory-osteosarcoma)) and is now published (Xie et al., *Ann Oncol*, online October 26, 2026; [PMID 42933637](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42933637/)). In 122 patients aged 12 or over, after at least two prior lines, randomized 2:1 (80 to the conjugate, risvutatug rezetecan, and 42 to gemcitabine–docetaxel), median progression-free survival by blinded central review was 5.5 against 1.4 months, hazard ratio 0.33 (95% CI 0.20–0.55); overall survival is immature, with a hazard ratio of 0.51 (95% CI 0.21–1.23) after a median 7.1 months of follow-up. The cost was heavier toxicity: grade 3 or worse treatment-related events in 93% against 83%, serious treatment-related events in 64% against 29%, mostly blood-related, and dose reductions in 71% against 26% (paper, abstract only). Second, OST-HER2, a Listeria-based vaccine first tested in pet dogs, is reported to show three-year survival of 72.7% after lung-metastasis surgery in 41 patients, against 45.8% in a pooled historical control (press release only; [Targeted Oncology](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/final-3-year-os-ost-her2-daznelimgene-osteosarcoma)). That comparison is single-arm, company-reported and not peer-reviewed. The same Listeria-HER2 vaccine, given after standard therapy to 118 pet dogs in a single-arm trial, showed no benefit over historical controls (paper, abstract only; Mol Ther 2025, PMID 39955616), which makes the human claim harder to accept until the data are published.

Cell therapy has changed its design more than its targets. HER2 CAR-T cells looked inactive in 2015 when given at low dose without conditioning; with conditioning and repeat doses, half of treated patients had clinical benefit in 2024, and one osteosarcoma patient's remission lasted 42 months before relapse (paper, full text; [PMC11588040](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11588040/)). St. Jude now plans B7-H3 CAR-T as consolidation after first-line chemotherapy (3CAR4OS, NCT07428993, 41 patients, recruiting since August 2026, primary completion 2034; registry) and after radiation (RAD3CAR) ([PMC12729273](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12729273)).

The clearest pattern across all of this concerns one clinical state: the patient whose lung metastases have just been removed. At least five treatments have been aimed at it. Inhaled GM-CSF (COG AOST0221), placebo-controlled saracatinib (NCT00752206) and dinutuximab (AOST1421) failed; denosumab (10 of 38 event-free at 12 months) did not clearly beat the benchmark; and OST-HER2 looks positive only against historical controls. That benchmark, from AOST0221, is 20% event-free at 12 months with a 95% confidence interval of 10–34% from one 42-patient trial (paper, full text; [PMID 27400942](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27400942/)), an interval wide enough that single-arm trials against it are poorly powered. A November 2026 COG update pooling 99 such patients from three single-arm phase 2 trials gives 22% event-free at 12 months (95% CI 15–31%), ranging from 14% to 29% across the trials; relapse within two years of diagnosis did worse, and the authors conclude that smaller effects "will require randomization" (preprint, abstract only; Chou et al., medRxiv, [doi 10.64898/2026.11.13.26368045](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.13.26368045)). The older AOST0221 figure is kept here as history; the larger update barely narrows the interval. The field's credibility problem here is design, not drugs.

## Neuroblastoma advanced by asking one randomized question at a time

High-risk neuroblastoma shares osteosarcoma's basic character: few point mutations, driven by large chromosomal changes. Its long-term survival nonetheless rose from under 15% in the 1980s to roughly half (approximate; we did not find a single pooled source). Each gain came from a randomized trial that kept the backbone constant and changed one thing. High-dose chemotherapy with stem-cell rescue improved three-year event-free survival from 22% to 34%, and retinoic acid added more (paper, abstract only; [Matthay 1999](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199910143411601)). The antibody dinutuximab with immune stimulants raised two-year event-free survival from 46% to 66% (paper, abstract only; [Yu 2010](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0911123)). Tandem transplant added a further step ([Park 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2019.11642)).

Three structural habits sit behind that record. First, risk is assigned before treatment from biology. The international INRG system pooled 8,800 patients to define risk groups by age, stage, MYCN status and chromosome changes (paper, abstract only; [Cohn 2009](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2008.16.6785)), and its data commons grew into the Pediatric Cancer Data Commons ([PCDC](https://commons.cri.uchicago.edu/)). Osteosarcoma still assigns risk mainly after treatment, by metastases and necrosis. Second, biology was turned into prognosis: three mutually exclusive ways of maintaining chromosome ends define outcome in neuroblastoma ([Ackermann 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat6768)). Third, parents funded named projects. Band of Parents, formed in 2007 by about 100 families at Memorial Sloan Kettering, led a $2 million grant in 2013 for one specific antibody and reports more than $14M committed in total (press release only; [Band of Parents](https://bandofparents.org/?p=40)).

The most striking precedent is eflornithine (DFMO). A parent-backed consortium took a cheap, off-patent drug through a single-arm trial and compared it with cooperative-group patients as an external control (92 treated and 852 controls in the published analysis). FDA's own primary analysis matched 90 treated patients with 270 controls: four-year event-free survival was 84% against 73% (HR 0.48), and the drug was approved in December 2023 after a 14–6 advisory vote (registry: [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-eflornithine-adult-and-pediatric-patients-high-risk-neuroblastoma)). FDA reviewers said they had never before relied on a single externally controlled trial for an oncology approval ([Pink Sheet](https://pink.citeline.com/PS148995/US-WorldMeds-Neuroblastoma-Drug-External-Controls-Confirmatory-Evidence-And-A-Concern-About-Precedent)).

Academic cell therapy also delivered. GD2-CART01 gave a 63% response rate in 27 relapsed patients, with three-year survival of 60% at the recommended dose (paper, abstract only; Del Bufalo, NEJM 2023; [ESMO](https://esmo.org/oncology-news/gd2-cart01-may-induce-sustained-antitumour-effect-in-a-proportion-of-patients-with-relapsed-or-refractory-neuroblastoma)).

Neuroblastoma is not solved. Relapsed high-risk disease remains largely fatal, MYCN cannot be drugged directly, and tumour cells escape GD2 therapy by switching to a state that loses the target ([van Groningen 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3899)). One half of its model cannot transfer either: neuroblastoma has a favourable subgroup that regresses on its own in infants, which made safe de-escalation possible. Osteosarcoma has no such group, so it can copy only the other half, a biological definition of high risk made before treatment starts.

The GD2 antibody's different fate in the two diseases may also be instructive. In neuroblastoma it is given after intensive chemotherapy alongside other immune agents, and in relapse it is combined with chemotherapy. The osteosarcoma trial gave it largely alone, without selecting patients by GD2 level. This reading is our inference, not a tested conclusion.

## A rare disease inside an underfunded category, with few market incentives

Osteosarcoma is diagnosed in about 1,000 people a year in the US, about half of them children and teenagers (registry: [American Cancer Society](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/osteosarcoma/about/key-statistics.html)). Paediatric cancer as a whole receives less than 5% of the National Cancer Institute budget, by AACR's account, and NIH does not break paediatric spending down by disease (press release only; [AACR 2025](https://cancerprogressreport.aacr.org/pediatric/pcpr25-contents/pcpr25-a-message-from-the-aacr/)). NIH's osteosarcoma grants are now tracked from NIH RePORTER in `reports/NIH funding for osteosarcoma.md`. Much disease-specific money comes from small family foundations: MIB Agents has awarded $2.9M to 37 investigators, funded by family funds whose members sit on peer review (press release only; [Clinical Research News](https://www.clinicalresearchnewsonline.com/news/2026/06/17/mib-agents'-community-of-families-has-awarded-$2-9m-in-grants-to-research-pediatric-bone-cancer)).

Federal funding held in fiscal 2026. Congress enacted $47.493B for NIH, up 1.0%, against a presidential request that would have cut it 40.6% (registry: [CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-05-18_R43341_3cc14d4f8e161830185f61cc3883386300b058ba.html)). HHS announced on 30 September 2025 that the Childhood Cancer Data Initiative would double from $50M to $100M; that is an announcement, not an enacted appropriation (press release only; [HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-doubles-ai-backed-childhood-cancer-research-funding.html)).

Industry incentives are weak. The RACE Act requires paediatric studies when an adult cancer drug's target matters in a childhood cancer. Advocacy analyses report that after it took effect 42.9% of approved cancer therapies carried a paediatric requirement, that the median time to a final report is 5.96 years, and that only 6.9% of trials of drugs with RACE-relevant targets include children (press release only; [Friends of Cancer Research](https://friendsofcancerresearch.org/blog/assessing-the-impact-of-the-research-to-accelerate-cures-and-equity-race-act/); [Children's Cancer Cause](https://www.childrenscancercause.org/blog/race-act-effectiveness)). The law bites least where there is no defined molecular target, which is osteosarcoma's situation: in the 2019 interim report of the Pediatric MATCH screening trial, only 13% of osteosarcoma patients had a matchable alteration, the lowest of the non-brain tumours (press release only; [The Oncology Pharmacist](https://www.theoncologypharmacist.com/issues/2019/august-2019-vol-12-no-3/nci-cog-pediatric-match-trial-shows-high-rate-of-targetable-molecular-alterations)). The main financial carrot is the rare paediatric disease priority review voucher. It lapsed in December 2024 and was reauthorized on 3 February 2026 through September 2029, with a required government review of its effectiveness; one voucher sold for $200M in January 2026 (registry; [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/congress-reauthorizes-the-rare-pediatric-disease-priority-review-voucher-program-and-clarifies-orphan-drug-exclusivity.html)).

Data and tissue are scattered. Human genomic data sit in controlled-access repositories that require an institutional investigator and signing official. The most open new source is the Osteosarcoma Project, where patients in the US and Canada donate records, saliva or blood and stored tumour directly, with data released in batches ([Dana-Farber](https://danafarberbostonchildrens.org/news/count-me-launches-osteosarcoma-project)). We found no counts of paired diagnosis-and-relapse samples anywhere.

Patients between 15 and 39 fall between paediatric and adult systems. Survival gains in 15–19-year-olds (56% to about 66%) lagged those in younger children (40% to 76%) (registry: [NCI PDQ](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK65736/)). Lower trial enrolment and care outside paediatric centres probably contribute, but we found no sourced data on this.

The binding constraint appears to be neither trial slots nor ideas alone, but a shortage of credible agents, the lack of a shared benchmark for judging small trials, and recruitment. Across registered osteosarcoma trials, poor recruitment ended five to six times as many trials as lack of efficacy, and the 65 terminated trials had enrolled about 1,970 patients before stopping (our calculation; `reports/Osteosarcoma clinical trials landscape.md`). Europe's FOSTER consortium is working on both benchmark and recruitment through a study platform from diagnosis to relapse that opened in October 2026 (NCT07871864, about 530 planned; registry). An industry cabozantinib maintenance trial, CabOSTar (NCT06341712), was terminated after 10 patients "due to ongoing recruitment challenges" (registry).

## Twelve candidate gaps, and where each stands

Each gap below began as a hypothesis in the notes and has been checked against the registry and literature. "Status" is as of November 14, 2026.

| # | Gap | Status | Evidence now | Type of work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No pooled, standardized historical-control dataset for relapse trials | **Partly addressed** | FOSTER names the absence and reports a framework in development (paper, abstract only; [PMID 37738712](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37738712/)); COG refreshed its benchmark, and a 99-patient COG update for resected lung-only relapse (12-month event-free survival 22%, 95% CI 15–31%) is posted as a preprint (preprint, abstract only); this project has built a first public version (`reports/Osteosarcoma pooled control dataset.md`). Two further questions concern harm reduction, not cure. We found no randomized trial of removing cisplatin or doxorubicin for good responders, and the survival safety of the hearing-protection drug has not been shown specifically in osteosarcoma. Both matter to families because the late effects are permanent.

Gaps 1, 7 (for dogs), 8 and 9 stand out because they need no new patients, only existing data and careful work.

## Cross-disease transfers: what failed, what remains untested

Past transfers into osteosarcoma mostly failed, and the failures are instructive.

| Transfer already tried | Source field | Outcome in osteosarcoma |
|---|---|---|
| GD2 antibody alone | Neuroblastoma | Negative: 11 of 39 event-free at 12 months against 16 required (paper, abstract only; [AOST1421](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35809374/)) |
| Denosumab | Giant-cell tumour of bone | Negative: 1 of 15 event-free at four months in measurable disease (paper, abstract only; [AOST1321](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41159913/)) |
| HER2 and gpNMB conjugates | Breast cancer | Negative: 1 response in 22 for glembatumumab vedotin ([PMID 31586757](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31586757/)); trastuzumab deruxtecan did not pass its first stage, 1 of 9 with stable disease past 24 weeks ([PEPN1924, PMID 42131734](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42131734/)) (paper, abstract only) |
| Zoledronate | Bone metastasis care | Trend toward harm ([ASCO Post](https://www.ascopost.com/News/42704)) |
| PI3K/mTOR matching | Precision oncology | Negative: the Pediatric MATCH samotolisib arm treated 17 patients, 6 with osteosarcoma, with no objective responses (paper, abstract only; [APEC1621D, PMID 39298693](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39298693/)) |
| Checkpoint inhibitors | Melanoma, lung cancer | Minimal activity in small cohorts: 1 of 22 responded to pembrolizumab (registry; [SARC028](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02301039)) |

The common lesson is that the presence of a target is not enough: gpNMB was widely expressed and still did not predict response (paper, abstract only; PMID 31586757). Setting, combination partners and patient selection decided the neuroblastoma successes.

Some popular ideas have very little behind them. Elephant TP53 and LIF6 biology is cell-culture work from largely one lab, with no osteosarcoma application found. Repurposed drugs such as disulfiram and ivermectin have only cell-line or mouse data ([PubMed 36503300](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36503300/)); none should be presented as a treatment.

The dog evidence cuts both ways. Canine work drove the HER2 vaccine into human trials and nominated a growth-linked risk locus, GRB10, shared between species ([PMC11402579](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11402579)). But the larger canine vaccine trial, 118 dogs, showed no benefit over historical controls (PMID 39955616).

## Where an independent researcher or small team can add real value

These contribution paths need data, finance or organising skill instead of a laboratory. They are ranked by how directly they answer a need the field itself has named.

**Build the missing benchmark.** The highest-leverage project is a living, standardized evidence map of relapsed-osteosarcoma trials with a pooled historical-control dataset, which FOSTER says is missing (gap 1). A first public version now exists in this repository (`reports/Osteosarcoma pooled control dataset.md`). The neuroblastoma eflornithine approval shows what a credible external control can unlock. The next steps are publication and review by a FOSTER or COG clinician, so the result is trusted.

**Track the pipeline and the money.** Both trackers now exist: every registered osteosarcoma trial with its status and results (`reports/Osteosarcoma clinical trials landscape.md`) and NIH funding from RePORTER (`reports/NIH funding for osteosarcoma.md`). Keeping them current, adding foundation awards, and chasing the four large randomized trials that still have no public results are ongoing tasks.

**Analyse data that is already open.** In rough order of value:
1. Re-analyse the open dog genomes for the 2025 shattering mechanism and ecDNA, and test whether it predicts outcome in dogs from NCI's COTC021/022 trials (`reports/Dog genome re-analysis scope.md`).
2. Audit whether single-cell claims built on the earliest datasets hold up in the newer cohorts.
3. Compare the subtype schemes head to head on TARGET-OS open data.
4. Run a low-power drug-repurposing screen across DepMap, PRISM and LINCS.

Open-tier sources (Treehouse, DepMap, cBioPortal-processed data, Osteosarcoma Project releases) need no institutional credentials. Human read-level and germline data (dbGaP, St. Jude Cloud, EGA, Genomics England) all require an institutional investigator and signing official, so that work needs an academic partner.

**Grow the tissue and data supply.** Directing families to the Osteosarcoma Project increases the scarcest resource. Its original NIH award (U2C CA252974, ended August 2025) aimed to consent 3,000 patients across the Osteosarcoma and Leiomyosarcoma Projects combined; we found no current count (registry; [NIH RePORTER](https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/10048560)). Individual results cannot be returned in New York or Canada.

**Fund with structure.** A family fund can back specific projects through existing peer review. MIB Agents awards $100K and $50K grants, and families join review as stakeholders (press release only; [Clinical Research News](https://www.clinicalresearchnewsonline.com/news/2026/06/17/mib-agents'-community-of-families-has-awarded-$2-9m-in-grants-to-research-pediatric-bone-cancer)). The neuroblastoma lesson is to tie money to a named project at a high-volume centre. Good candidates are prospective validation of the released necrosis-scoring model and a canine trial of a cheap candidate drug (see `reports/Ranked leads and research partners.md`).

**Apply finance and policy skills.** The voucher programme now faces a mandated government effectiveness review that advocates can feed into, and RACE Act reporting is an active campaign ([Children's Cancer Cause](https://www.childrenscancercause.org/blog/race-act-effectiveness)). Modelling voucher economics for a disease of about 1,000 US cases a year is a concrete analysis we did not find done.

Three cautions apply. Many gaps shrink after a conversation with a specialist, so check with one before acting. Do not relay press-reported results to other families as established. And the questions that need randomized trials (gaps 2 to 5) cannot be run by a small team; the realistic role there is to assemble the case and bring it to cooperative groups and funders.

## Conclusion

Osteosarcoma is often framed as a disease waiting for a breakthrough drug. The more accurate picture is a field whose measuring instruments are too weak to recognise a moderately good drug when it appears: a relapse benchmark built on 96 patients given single agents, a resected-disease benchmark whose 95% confidence interval was 10–34% in a single 42-patient trial and is still 15–31% after pooling 99 patients (preprint, abstract only), trials that close for lack of patients far more often than for lack of effect, and a response marker that arrives after the decision it should inform. Neuroblastoma's advantage was less about biology than about habits of shared data, pre-treatment risk groups and steady randomized questions, and those habits are transferable even though its specific drugs mostly were not.

That shifts where effort pays. The B7-H3 and vaccine results will be confirmed or refuted by trials and publications already in motion. What an outsider can do is strengthen the instruments: publish and maintain the pooled control dataset and the trial and funding trackers, re-analyse data already public, and help build the case for randomized trials in the resected-lung setting inside platforms such as FOSTER's.
