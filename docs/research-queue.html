Research Queue

The For John research engine, in public. Each question is something public data or a partner laboratory can answer. Each is written down with its success and failure rules before the data are examined, then tested on patients it was not built from. Results are posted whatever they show. Last updated November 24, 2026.

How a Question Moves Through the Queue

Status Meaning
Proposed A question worth testing, with the data that could answer it. Not yet pre-registered.
Pre-registered Hypothesis, data, analysis and verdict rules are committed to git before the data are examined.
Replicated Held up in independent patients by the rules set in advance. Promoted to a lead and handed towards a laboratory test.
Refuted A meaningful effect ruled out by the rules set in advance. Closed.
Inconclusive Neither, by the rules set in advance. Recorded with what it would take to decide.
Needs a partner Cannot be answered from public data; needs samples, records or a laboratory.

The checks get stronger in this order: a prediction written down before looking, a test in independent patient data, then a laboratory experiment. Only the last one is proof.

Tested

# Question Result Date
1 Does tumour RAMP1 RNA predict relapse in patients outside the study it was found in? Inconclusive. Pooled hazard ratio 0.99 (0.74–1.32) across three independent cohorts, 53 events, against 2.30 in the discovery cohort. The large effect is ruled out; a small one is not. Full result Nov 24, 2026
2 Do lung metastases carry more CXCR4 and less CXCL12 than primary tumours? Inconclusive, both. Across six datasets (29 lung metastases, 51 primaries) CXCR4 leaned higher in lung metastases, +0.43 standard deviations (−0.05 to +0.91), but the lean disappeared once immune cells were accounted for (+0.06). CXCL12 was not lower (−0.08, −0.55 to +0.39). Lead 14 gains no support. Full result Nov 24, 2026
3 Are deletions of the 9p21 region (CDKN2A and MTAP) more common at relapse than in primary tumours? Inconclusive. In two hospital cohorts (130 relapse-stage patients, 211 with primary samples) the odds ratio was 1.10 (0.59–2.06). About one in five relapsed osteosarcomas carries the CDKN2A deletion (35 of 179), and in 30 patients tested twice it matched between primary and later sample in 28. MTAP itself was measured in only 24 patients. Full result Nov 24, 2026
9 Which drug trials that select patients by MTAP loss accept osteosarcoma, and from what age? Answered (a count, not a test). 25 trials recruiting or about to would take an osteosarcoma patient with MTAP loss on paper; one names bone sarcoma; 12 have a United States site; only one accepts under-18s, and it is in China. Full result Nov 24, 2026
10 Do osteosarcoma cell lines without MTAP depend on PRMT5 more than those with it? Inconclusive, underpowered. The link reproduced across all cancers, but only 3 of 27 osteosarcoma lines have lost MTAP. The one with knockdown data was the most PRMT5-dependent of five. Full result Nov 24, 2026
4 Do osteosarcoma cells with extra copies of CCNE1 depend on PKMYT1? Inconclusive. Across 17 lines the trend ran the expected way (slope −0.06, −0.15 to +0.03) but could be chance. Four amplified lines identified for a direct drug test. Full result Nov 24, 2026
12 How often is CCNE1 amplified in relapsed osteosarcoma, and would the trials take such a patient? Frequency: about one in ten (17 of 179 on hospital tests); not clearly more common than in primaries (odds ratio 1.14, 0.52–2.48, inconclusive). Trials: nine would take such a patient on paper, six with a United States site, and one, of the PKMYT1 inhibitor lunresertib, from age 12. Full result Nov 24, 2026
13 Which other gene changes in osteosarcoma have a matching dependency across all cancer cell lines? Scan done (discovery, not a test). 1.2 million comparisons, 14 replicated hits. Seven are established pairs, which shows the method works; no unknown druggable weakness turned up. Pointers: tumours whose 9p21 deletion reaches FOCAD depend on HBS1L (published 2025, drug in development; about 4% of osteosarcomas), and PTEN loss with FERMT2 needs an independent test. Full result Nov 24, 2026
16 A second scan for the tumour-suppressor genes osteosarcoma loses most often (RB1, TP53, ATRX, PTEN, NF1) Scan done (discovery, not a test). Both built-in checks passed; RB1 loss with SKP2, E2F3 and CDK2 recovered; no new druggable weakness. Without microsatellite-unstable lines, ATRX loss goes with FANCM, SMARCAL1 and ATR, as known. Osteosarcoma's own way of breaking TP53 and ATRX, large rearrangements, is invisible to these data. Full result Nov 24, 2026
21 Do the gene-change weaknesses from the knockout screens hold up with real drugs? Checks passed; both tests inconclusive. In a public drug screen of hundreds of cell lines, TP53-altered lines resisted all four MDM2 inhibitors and RB1-lost lines all three CDK4/6 inhibitors, so the method works. CCNE1-amplified lines were not clearly more sensitive to the WEE1 inhibitor adavosertib (−0.21 SD, −0.73 to +0.30, 16 lines), and MTAP-lost lines showed little or no response to an early PRMT5 drug not built for them (−0.05, −0.29 to +0.19). Full result Nov 25, 2026
22 Do lung metastases keep the surface targets that new drugs aim at (LRRC15, B7-H3 and six others)? LRRC15 maintained by the rule, with a caveat; B7-H3 inconclusive. No analysis showed LRRC15 dropping in 29 lung metastases against 51 primaries. B7-H3 was lower in whole-tissue samples but not clearly in tumour cells. The pre-registered adjustment for lung tissue proved unstable, so the next such test should measure tumour cells directly. Full result Nov 25, 2026
23 Across the whole registry, how often can a teenager with osteosarcoma get into a drug trial? Answered (a count, not a test). Of 57 recruiting drug trials naming osteosarcoma that take relapsed disease, 43 accept 12- to 17-year-olds (31 with a US site). Of 1,434 early-phase "solid tumour" drug trials, where new drugs first enter people, 78 (5%) do, and companies lead fewer of those. Full result Nov 26, 2026
24 In pet dogs with osteosarcoma, does tumour inflammation (IL6, IL1B, CXCL8), DKK1 or FAK RNA go with faster relapse? Inflammation inconclusive; DKK1 refuted; FAK inconclusive. In 59 dogs treated with amputation and chemotherapy, the inflammation score leaned towards faster relapse (hazard ratio 1.24, 0.93–1.66); DKK1 did not (0.92, 0.67–1.26). IL1B alone leaned harder (1.34, 1.04–1.72), an unplanned single-gene result. The built-in check was weak. Full result Nov 26, 2026
26 Does tumour macrophage signal go with a better outcome in patients outside the Leiden study that found it? Inconclusive. The Leiden result reproduces in its own data (hazard ratio 0.63, 0.44–0.91) but pools to 0.85 (0.67–1.08) across three independent cohorts, 155 patients and 74 events. CD163 alone and TARGET-OS lean the expected way. Full result Nov 26, 2026

Rules the Engine Follows

  1. A question enters testing only with a written prediction and the result that would count against it.
  2. The data used to test an idea are never the data it came from.
  3. Every result is published, including null and inconclusive ones.
  4. Ideas that survive are handed to people who can run the experiment; the engine does not claim a treatment works.
  5. Not medical advice.
