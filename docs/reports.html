Research Engine Results

Each was planned in writing, with its rules, and committed before its analysis was run.

  • Cycle 1: Does RAMP1 predict relapse? Inconclusive; the large effect ruled out.
  • Cycle 2: Do lung metastases carry more CXCR4? Inconclusive; likely explained by immune cells.
  • Cycle 3: Is 9p21 deletion more common at relapse? Inconclusive; about 1 in 5 relapsed tumours have it.
  • Cycle 4: MTAP-selected trials open to osteosarcoma: 25 open on paper to adults, one to under-18s.
  • Cycle 5: Do cells without MTAP depend on PRMT5? Too few cell lines to tell.
  • Cycle 6: Do cells with more CCNE1 depend on PKMYT1? Leans yes, not confirmed.
  • Cycle 7: CCNE1 at relapse and the trials open to it: about 1 in 10; one trial from age 12.
  • Cycle 8: A scan for drug weaknesses that go with osteosarcoma's gene changes: the known pairs, no hidden one.
  • Cycle 9: Weaknesses that go with the tumour-suppressor genes osteosarcoma loses: known RB1 pairs; public data miss osteosarcoma's main routes.
  • Cycle 10: Do the gene-change weaknesses hold up with real drugs? The known drug links show up; the CCNE1 and MTAP tests are inconclusive.
  • Cycle 11: Do lung metastases keep the surface targets? No clear sign that LRRC15 is lost; B7-H3 unclear.
  • Cycle 12: How often can a teenager with osteosarcoma get into a drug trial? Trials that list osteosarcoma usually take teenagers; early trials of new drugs almost never do.
  • Cycle 13: In pet dogs, does tumour inflammation go with faster relapse? Inconclusive for inflammation; DKK1 RNA does not mark dogs that do worse.
  • Cycle 14: Does tumour macrophage signal predict a better outcome in new patients? Reproduces in the original cohort; weaker and not clear in three new ones.
  • Cycle 15: Do tumours with MYC gain respond worse to chemotherapy? More MYC went with poorer response in three cohorts; modest, needs replication.
  • Cycle 16: Since the FDA guidance, are new trials more open to teenagers? No sign of it; 2.4% of new company-led early trials take teenagers.
  • Cycle 17: Before and after the FDA guidance, across all trials The share of early trials taking teenagers fell from 11% to 5%.
  • Cycle 18: Do sarcoma trials take teenagers, and who leaves them out? Ewing and rhabdomyosarcoma trials usually do; company-led trials less often.
  • Cycle 19: Where in the US can a teenager with relapsed osteosarcoma reach a trial? Sites in 33 states and DC (34 of 51); 17 states have none; 20 of the 32 trials are in only one or two states.
  • Cycle 20: Does tumour immune signal go with better chemotherapy response? Inconclusive; macrophage signal leans towards better response.
