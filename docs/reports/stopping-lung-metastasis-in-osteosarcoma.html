Stopping Lung Metastasis in Osteosarcoma: What Is Known, What Has Been Tried, and What Has Been Missed

November 14, 2026. Built from 33,242 PubMed records in data/literature/osteosarcoma_pubtator.jsonl, the 758 registered studies in data/osteosarcoma_trials.csv, and live ClinicalTrials.gov API v2, PubMed E-utilities and Europe PMC REST API queries run on the date above. Script: tools/lung_metastasis_map.py. Data written: data/lung_metastasis_mechanisms.csv, data/lung_metastasis_papers.csv, data/lung_metastasis_trials.csv. Full working notes with every citation: research_notes/Lung metastasis/notes.md.

How to read the labels. Key numbers carry one of these tags: (registry) means taken from a public record, here ClinicalTrials.gov or PubMed; (paper, full text) means the full article was read; (paper, abstract only) means only the abstract was available, which is true of nearly every publication cited here; (press release only) means a company or news announcement with no peer-reviewed publication; (our calculation) means we counted or derived it from the data files above; (preprint, abstract only) means only the abstract of a preprint, which is not peer-reviewed, was read. Findings are separated from hypotheses throughout.

Summary

Almost everyone who dies of osteosarcoma dies of lung metastases, and the biology of how the tumour colonizes the lung is now reasonably well described: lung lining cells release IL-1alpha, which switches on a small, slow-growing subpopulation of tumour cells to produce IL-6 and IL-8, and blocking that circuit reduced metastasis in mice with little effect on the primary tumour's growth (paper, abstract only). Drugs that block IL-1, IL-6 and the IL-8 receptor are approved or in late-stage trials for arthritis and other conditions, and none of them has a registered efficacy trial in osteosarcoma on ClinicalTrials.gov (registry). Meanwhile the ideas that were tested — inhaled GM-CSF, inhaled cisplatin, aerosol interleukin-2, inhaled interleukin-15 in dogs — all aimed to stimulate immunity inside the lung, and those that reported results were negative; the dog trial of inhaled IL-15 after amputation did worse than a historical control group that had amputation and chemotherapy, and the same paper reports that IL-6 rose after both surgery and chemotherapy while immune killing fell (paper, abstract only). The one randomized, placebo-controlled drug trial in the situation that matters most — a patient whose lung metastases have just been fully removed — did happen: it took nearly nine years to enrol 38 patients, was stopped for slow accrual and futility, and its 19.4-versus-8.6-month difference in progression-free survival was not statistically significant (p=0.47) (paper, abstract only; registry). On detection, there is no evidence in osteosarcoma that finding lung relapse earlier saves lives; what does predict survival is whether the surgeon removes every nodule, and whether the tumour responded to drugs before surgery.

1. The Biology: What Actually Has in Vivo Metastasis Evidence

We counted, for 56 mechanisms, how many papers exist, how many are about metastasis, and how many go beyond a dish to an animal with a metastasis endpoint. Finding (our calculation): of 29,305 non-review, non-retracted osteosarcoma papers, 9,130 are metastasis-focused, 3,579 mention the lung specifically, and only 1,093 (12%) have an in vivo metastasis endpoint. That share has been stable for twenty years (12.9% in 2005–09, 13.0% in 2020–24).

The Lung Niche Circuit, in Order

  1. Lung epithelium releases IL-1alpha. A 2025 Cancer Research paper combined an organotypic lung model, single-cell sequencing, human xenografts and immunocompetent mouse models and found metastasis is started by a hypo-proliferative subpopulation that makes IL-6 and IL-8 in response to lung-epithelium-derived IL-1alpha; disrupting IL-1 signalling genetically or with a drug "significantly reduced metastatic progression" (PMID 40882022, paper, abstract only; the abstract gives no effect size).
  2. IL-6 and IL-8 do the colonizing. Both are higher in lung metastases than in matched primary tumours from the same patients; expression correlated with lung colonization efficiency in mice; blocking both had minimal effect on proliferation in vitro or in vivo, but the combination inhibited metastasis across multiple models. The amplifier is a feed-forward loop with bronchial epithelial and bronchial smooth muscle cells (PMID 30135299, JCI Insight 2018, paper, abstract only).
  3. The receptor level is druggable. Lung blood vessels express CXCL1 and tumour cells express CXCR2, the receptor shared by CXCL1 and IL-8; CXCL1 from human pulmonary artery endothelial cells drove motility through CXCR2 → FAK → PI3K/Akt → NF-kB → VCAM-1, and in vivo CXCL1 promoted lung metastasis (PMID 32079335, PMID 34760697, paper, abstract only). Separately, IL-8 in medium from osteosarcoma and tumour-associated macrophages co-cultured together promoted growth and lung metastasis in vivo via FAK, and an anti-IL-8 antibody reduced the effect (PMID 38302407, paper, abstract only; the source cell of the IL-8 was not isolated).
  4. The lung is prepared before any cell arrives. Mice merely preconditioned with tumour cell secretome developed airway damage, inflammation, neutrophil infiltration and fibronectin/collagen deposition by resident activated fibroblasts, which accelerated metastasis. Depleting one secreted protein, EFEMP1, prevented lung metastasis in that model; EFEMP1 was present in the plasma of tumour-bearing mice and in patient biopsies with worse survival (PMID 38031171, paper, abstract only).
  5. The same happens in pet dogs, naturally. Pre-metastatic lungs from 10 dogs with untreated osteosarcoma had significantly more CD204+ and CD206+ macrophages and CD11d+ bone-marrow-derived cells than lungs from 10 cancer-free dogs; the bone-marrow cells were lower once metastases were established, so recruitment appears transient (PMID 39526499, paper, abstract only). These measurements were made at necropsy.
  6. Innate activation in the lung can block seeding. In a syngeneic model, particulate beta-glucan given before tumour cells "prevented tumour cell seeding in the lungs" and prolonged survival; it worked through macrophages and NK cells, and adding a CD40 agonist improved it further (PMID 41083282, J Immunother Cancer 2025, paper, abstract only).

Ezrin, the Oldest Lung-Metastasis Mechanism, and the Drug That Never Came

Ezrin has 17 in vivo metastasis studies (our calculation). Small molecules that bind it (NSC305787, NSC668394) inhibited lung metastasis of ezrin-sensitive but not ezrin-resistant cells after tail-vein injection (PMID 21706056, paper, abstract only). Locking ezrin either open or closed abolished metastasis, and both caused tumour cells to die soon after arriving in the lung; changes in lactate and oxygen consumption, reported for the closed form, tie motility to metabolism (PMID 22147261, paper, abstract only). A 2026 paper adds that the "inactive" closed form is itself an RNA-binding protein, and that it restored metastatic capability in zebrafish xenografts of ezrin-null cells (PMID 41843652, paper, abstract only). Finding: there is still no clinical-grade ezrin inhibitor. Also worth knowing: 5 of the 10 most recent ezrin-titled records in the dataset are retractions or expressions of concern, so this literature carries the paper-mill problem described in reports/Osteosarcoma literature map.md.

A Methodological Finding That Undercuts a Lot of the Rest

The widely used intratibial K7M2 mouse model is not a model of spontaneous metastasis. Lung metastases were present in 100% of mice within one week; burden grew over four weeks (0.9% → 40.3%) but the number of foci did not (36.4 vs 49.3, p=0.18); and amputating the injected leg thirty minutes after injection left metastatic burden unchanged (48.9% vs 40.9%, p=0.819). The authors conclude seeding is immediate and primary-tumour-independent, and the model "should not be used to study the early components of the metastatic cascade" (PMID 29601385, paper, abstract only).

Hypothesis. Both of the dominant designs — tail vein and intratibial — measure colonization and outgrowth, not dissemination or waking from dormancy. That would mean the 1,093 in vivo metastasis papers mostly do not model the clinical state that matters most: a patient with no visible disease after surgery whose lungs hold sleeping cells. Dormancy has 32 in vivo metastasis papers and only 14 with dormancy in the title, several of them not osteosarcoma-specific (our calculation).

Single-Cell and Cross-Species Data: The Newest and Least Used

A 2026 preprint assembled over a million cells and nuclei from human, canine, patient-derived xenograft and syngeneic mouse osteosarcoma at both bone and lung sites. Two conclusions stand out: canine osteosarcoma was a more faithful model of human tumour-host communication than the others compared, and lung metastases showed more intense matrix signalling than primary bone tumours, with tumour-derived fibronectin engaging integrin and syndecan receptors on lung epithelium to drive a profibrotic niche (PMID 41648517, bioRxiv preprint, not peer reviewed). A second 2026 preprint imaged living osteosarcoma lung metastases exposed to an MCL1 inhibitor and found surviving, drug-resistant ERK-high cells concentrated at the tumour–lung interface; tumour cell lysis itself amplified FGFR–ERK signalling in neighbouring cells, and adding the FGFR inhibitor fexagratinib or the broad kinase inhibitor pazopanib suppressed that adaptive signalling and improved the response to the MCL1 inhibitor ex vivo and in vivo (PMID 42856637, preprint, not peer reviewed).

Of the 758 registered studies in the local file, 224 mention lung, metastasis or inhaled terms and 73 are explicitly lung-directed (our calculation; data/lung_metastasis_trials.csv).

This is the single most useful negative result in the whole search. Giving an immune-stimulating inhaled drug in the window right after amputation left dogs doing worse than historical controls who had surgery and chemotherapy alone, and the paper's own correlative data show that window is immunosuppressed and IL-6-high (PMID 41209004). The authors do not conclude that IL-6 caused the failure; they say the correlative assays "suggest significant immunological effects of amputation and chemotherapy".

Hypothesis. That state is the one the biology papers say favours lung colonization (PMID 30135299, PMID 40882022). So the post-surgical window may be the wrong place to stimulate immunity and a reasonable place to try blocking IL-6 and IL-1. Three separate findings — the dog trial's failure, the lung cytokine circuit and the absence of any cytokine-blockade trial — point at the same unperformed experiment. The dog trial's own finding that plasma cytokines did not separate dogs who relapsed from those who did not cuts against the idea, and is the main reason to call this a lead rather than a conclusion.

What these single-arm results are judged against. The usual benchmark is COG AOST0221: 20% of 42 patients event-free 12 months after complete resection of a first lung relapse, with a 95% confidence interval of 10–34% (paper, full text; Lagmay 2016, PMID 27400942). A November 2026 COG update pooled 99 patients with resected lung-only relapse from three COG single-arm phase 2 trials: 12-month event-free survival 22% (95% CI 15–31%), 14% to 29% across the trials, and worse when relapse came within two years of diagnosis. Its authors conclude that single-arm trials can detect only large effects and that smaller effects "will require randomization" (preprint, abstract only; Chou et al., medRxiv, doi 10.64898/2026.11.13.26368045). The dinutuximab and denosumab cohorts above (28% and 26%) sit inside both intervals.

The randomized question has been attempted, not avoided. NCT00752206 randomized patients after complete resection of all lung nodules, and the binding constraint was accrual: nearly nine years for 38 patients, then termination. That is why a platform trial such as FOSTER (NCT07871864, 530 patients, randomized, recruiting since October 2026, registry) and pet-dog studies matter so much for this question.

A caution on the vaccine. The press-reported human OST-HER2 benefit against a historical control sits beside a larger, peer-reviewed, negative canine trial of the same vaccine construct in 118 dogs. Both are single-arm against historical controls; the bigger one found nothing.

Radiotherapy to Lung Metastases

SBRT controls what it irradiates. In 24 patients and 71 lesions, one-year local failure was 13.6% (95% CI 6.6–23.2) with no acute grade 3 toxicity, though only 17% of the lesions were in lung parenchyma (PMID 41531260, paper, abstract only). In a smaller series of 7 patients and 34 treatment courses, of which 29% were pulmonary or mediastinal, local control was 88% and all four failures had a biologically effective dose of 48 Gy or less — but median distant progression-free survival was 2.4 months (PMID 40509558, paper, abstract only). One registered SBRT trial in sarcoma and Wilms tumour lung metastases was terminated after 5 patients (NCT02581384, registry).

Two Delivery Routes Almost Nobody Discusses

Finding (from the trial-file scan): NCT02811523 "In Vivo Lung Perfusion for Pulmonary Metastases of Sarcoma" (phase 1, recruiting since 2016) and NCT03965234 "Pulmonary Suffusion in Controlling Minimal Residual Disease in Patients With Sarcoma" (phase 1/2, 99 patients, recruiting since 2020; early safety data in 31 patients with sarcoma and colorectal cancer, PMID 40940977, paper, abstract only; registry). Hypothesis: pulmonary suffusion is the only clinical programme we found that explicitly targets minimal residual disease in the lung — the dormancy state nothing else addresses — and it does not appear in the osteosarcoma reviews we read.

3. Detection: Does Finding Lung Relapse Earlier Help?

Finding: in osteosarcoma, there is no evidence that it does.

Question Evidence Answer
Does more intense surveillance improve survival? Randomized noninferiority trial, 500 non-metastatic extremity sarcoma patients at a single centre, 2x2 design: chest X-ray vs CT and 3-monthly vs 6-monthly (PMID 24249538, paper, abstract only) 3-year overall survival 67% X-ray vs 66% CT (HR 0.9, upper 90% CI 1.13), so X-ray was non-inferior to CT. On frequency, 64% 6-monthly vs 69% 3-monthly (HR 1.2, upper 90% CI 1.47): noninferiority of 6-monthly was not shown. About 90% of local recurrences were found by patients themselves
Do surveillance scans mostly find relapse? 69 patients (35 osteosarcoma), 1,279 images, 9 relapses (PMID 38345039, paper, abstract only) Only 2 of 9 relapses were symptomatic, so imaging found the rest — but positive predictive value was 0.5, and "abnormal findings on follow-up images rarely represent relapse"
Is there an osteosarcoma surveillance trial? ClinicalTrials.gov, 2026-11-14 (registry) None found. The two randomized surveillance trials are soft-tissue-sarcoma only: NCT04751409 (limited vs intense, 227 patients — primary outcome is fear of recurrence, not survival) and NCT06526897 (ECOG-ACRIN EA7201, phase 3 CT vs X-ray, 1,582 patients, overall survival, not yet recruiting, completion 2032)
Does ctDNA work? Prospective phase 2 of gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel in measurable relapsed disease, with serial liquid biopsy, 18 patients (PMID 39360936, paper, abstract only) Feasible, though this was not a surveillance study. All 18 had detectable circulating tumour cells, 10 had ctDNA, and all 8 patients with MYC amplification at entry progressed. Four-month PFS was 28% (95% CI 13–59%)
A mutation-free alternative? 5-hydroxymethylcytosine in plasma cell-free DNA, 262-gene signature (PMID 41673231, paper, abstract only) Sensitivity 65%, specificity 64% for active disease. Feasible, needs bigger cohorts
What does the field itself say is missing? 2026 review (PMID 42824543, paper, abstract only) Tumour-informed ctDNA is the strongest MRD signal and can precede imaging relapse; circulating cells, vesicles and microRNAs "remain exploratory signals without validated postoperative surveillance actions". Future studies "should test whether biomarker-triggered reassessment improves clinically meaningful outcomes ... rather than simply documenting recurrence earlier"

What does predict survival is the surgery, and the response before it. A 2026 meta-analysis of 21 retrospective studies found metastasectomy associated with better post-relapse survival than none (HR 0.29, 95% CI 0.18–0.46), complete resection strongly favourable (HR 0.21, 0.12–0.38), bilateral disease worse in primary metastatic osteosarcoma (HR 1.56, 1.27–1.93; not significant in post-relapse disease) and video-assisted surgery modestly worse than open (HR 1.69, 1.01–2.82) (PMID 42036655, paper, abstract only). A 2024 systematic review is blunt that these are all selection-biased, level IV evidence: the patients who did not get surgery were usually those who could not (PMID 38375639, paper, abstract only). And at the optimistic end, 463 consecutive children and young adults treated with repeated metastasectomies over 40 years had 5-year survival 34.0% and 20-year survival 29.7%, with 42 of 138 survivors (30.4%) cured by repeat operations (PMID 34674575, paper, abstract only).

The most actionable number in this section comes from a single-centre retrospective series: in 159 patients and 229 metastasectomies with a median 79 months of follow-up, response to pre-operative systemic therapy gave 5-year survival of 41.1% versus 0.0% in non-responders (HR 0.22, p<0.001). Palpation-assisted mini-thoracotomy found far more hidden nodules than VATS (43.4% vs 6.8%, p=0.004) with similar complications but no survival difference, and modelling put the best pre-operative watching window at 2–3 months (PMID 42723688, paper, abstract only).

The Platelet Axis: The Most Complete Untested Chain in the File

This did not appear in the project's earlier reports and may be the best genuinely new lead here.

  • Tissue factor was highly expressed in 73.6% of 53 pre-treatment osteosarcoma biopsies and associated with disease-free survival; knocking it down lowered IL-8, CXCL1, SNAI1 and MMP2, and blocking antibodies in mice reduced tumour growth, vessel density and significantly reduced lung metastasis (PMID 26763081, paper, abstract only).
  • Osteosarcoma cells activate platelets, which release lysophosphatidic acid; LPAR1 is upregulated in osteosarcoma; knocking out LPAR1 abolished early pulmonary metastatic foci; and the oral LPAR1 antagonist ONO-7300243 prevented pulmonary metastasis in mouse models (PMID 34302117, Oncogene 2021, paper, abstract only).
  • Podoplanin on tumour cells activates platelets through CLEC-2, and a humanized anti-podoplanin antibody (AP201) suppressed growth and pulmonary metastasis in vivo "without significant adverse events"; the mechanism runs through PDGFR activation by platelet releasate (PMID 35381070, Clin Cancer Res 2022, paper, abstract only).

And a natural experiment already under way: NCT03244020 randomizes aspirin 325 mg daily versus enoxaparin for clot prevention after orthopaedic oncology surgery in an estimated 2,868 patients with a dedicated primary-bone-tumour stratum, enrolling by invitation since February 2018 with completion in July 2028 (registry). Its primary outcome is venous thromboembolism, not cancer, and it enrols adults 18 and over, so most adolescents with osteosarcoma are not eligible.

5. The Top Gaps, as Hypotheses

Everything in this section is a hypothesis, not a finding. Each names its evidence, whether a drug exists, and the cheapest experiment that would test it.

Not a clean test (aspirin vs heparin rather than vs nothing, unpowered for cancer, and adults only, so few adolescents), but close to free
4 FAK is where several lung-niche signals converge, so defactinib might block more than one at once. FAK sits downstream of IL-8 (PMID 38302407), CXCL1 (PMID 32079335), integrins (PMID 36997926) and LOX (PMID 37013056), and a ZEB1/IQGAP3 axis runs through FAK, where pharmacologic FAK activation rescued the loss of migration and lung metastasis caused by IQGAP3 knockdown (PMID 42520958); 24 in vivo metastasis studies (our calculation). In the LOX study, defactinib itself reduced lung metastasis in an orthotopic model: 2 of 6 treated mice against 6 of 6 untreated (P = 0.03), using 143B cells engineered to overexpress LOX and injected into the shin bone; the dose is printed as 50 μg/kg twice daily, which is unusually low, and the study is small (paper, full text, read for the pet-dog brief; PMID 37013056) defactinib, approved 2025 for another indication, no osteosarcoma trial on ClinicalTrials.gov; a canine FAK-inhibitor study is planned under an NIH grant at Colorado State (R37CA303990, registry), and Colorado State lists a study giving defactinib to dogs with lung metastases (press release only: web listing; status and results unconfirmed) Pet-dog adjuvant trial — lead 4 of reports/Ranked leads and research partners.md; this report adds the mechanistic reason to prefer FAK over DKK-1
5 The resected-lung randomized question can be answered if enrolment is restricted to pre-operative non-responders. 5-year survival 41.1% in responders vs 0.0% in non-responders (HR 0.22, p<0.001) (PMID 42723688); the one randomized trial here needed 8.75 years for 38 patients and was stopped for accrual and futility (NCT00752206, PMID 32398945) any of the above An arm on the FOSTER platform (NCT07871864) restricted to non-responders, where the event rate approaches 100% and far fewer patients are needed
6 Earlier detection only helps if it buys a more complete operation, so the endpoint should be resection completeness, not lead time. Complete resection HR 0.21 for post-relapse survival (PMID 42036655); more intense imaging did not improve survival (PMID 24249538); the field's own review asks exactly this question (PMID 42824543) ctDNA assays exist and are feasible (PMID 39360936) A single-arm feasibility study of ctDNA-triggered surgery whose endpoint is the complete-resection rate against the published retrospective rate — no randomization needed to see a signal. Related to lead 8 of the ranked leads
7 Dogs can turn two-year survival endpoints into four-week biomarker endpoints for niche-directed drugs. The pre-metastatic niche is measurable in untreated dogs and recruitment appears transient (PMID 39526499); canine osteosarcoma was a more faithful cross-species model of tumour–host signalling than the alternatives compared (PMID 41648517, preprint); dogs die of metastasis at scale n/a — this is infrastructure Add lung-biopsy or lavage macrophage counts as a pharmacodynamic endpoint to a dog trial already running through the NCI Comparative Oncology Program, and first check the measurement works in living dogs
8 Resistance in lung metastases is born at the tumour–lung interface and can be suppressed by adding an FGFR inhibitor to the drug that provokes it. Drug-resistant ERK-high cells concentrated at the interface after MCL1 inhibition; tumour cell lysis amplified FGFR–ERK signalling in neighbours; fexagratinib or pazopanib suppressed that signalling and improved the MCL1 inhibitor's effect ex vivo and in vivo (PMID 42856637, preprint, not peer reviewed) fexagratinib, pazopanib, erdafitinib Repeat the combination in the ex vivo lung-metastasis culture that paper already built — the assay exists. Whether the same holds with a conventional cytotoxic rather than an MCL1 inhibitor has not been tested

Two further gaps worth stating plainly because they stop others wasting effort:

  • Ezrin is a chemistry gap, not an evidence gap. Seventeen in vivo metastasis studies over twenty years and still no clinical-grade inhibitor. "No trial" here does not mean "weak biology".
  • Glutamine metabolism in osteosarcoma lung metastasis has 5 in vivo metastasis papers out of 69 glutamine papers (our calculation), while telaglenastat and DRP-104 are clinical-stage. That is a cheap laboratory question, not a trial proposal.

6. Limits

  • Every mechanism count is a keyword match on title and abstract, not curation. "In vivo metastasis endpoint" means the abstract contains an animal term, metastasis wording and a metastasis-model phrase (tail vein, orthotopic, intratibial, lung nodules, metastatic burden and similar). It does not mean the mechanism was blocked and metastasis measured as the primary endpoint. Counts are a map of where effort went, not a verdict on quality.
  • Mechanism definitions overlap on purpose, so one paper can count under IL-6, STAT3 and macrophages. Columns do not sum, and nothing here should be treated as a partition of the literature.
  • Almost everything is abstract-only. Abstracts were retrieved through PubMed E-utilities and the Europe PMC REST API at www.ebi.ac.uk, both of which worked from this workspace; the europepmc.org website and PMC full-text HTML were not reachable through the proxy, so full text was read for nothing in the original search. Two later additions carry full-text labels from other reports' readings: the AOST0221 benchmark (November 19, from the pooled control dataset) and the defactinib mouse result in hypothesis 4 (November 20, from the pet-dog brief's full-text check). Each figure is labelled accordingly.
  • Two of the most interesting mechanistic papers are bioRxiv preprints (PMID 41648517, PMID 42856637) and have not been peer reviewed.
  • "No trial exists" rests on ClinicalTrials.gov only. EudraCT, ISRCTN, ChiCTR and jRCT were not searched, so a Chinese or Japanese trial of, say, tocilizumab in osteosarcoma would be missed. Given how much osteosarcoma trial activity in the earlier reports is Chinese, this is a real gap.
  • The randomized surveillance trial is sarcoma-wide, not osteosarcoma. PMID 24249538 studied 500 extremity sarcoma patients at a single centre and the abstract does not break down histology, so the osteosarcoma fraction is unverified.
  • The metastasectomy evidence is retrospective and selection-biased throughout, as its own systematic review says (PMID 38375639). Hazard ratios from that literature describe who gets operated on as much as what the operation does.
  • Dog trial results are single-arm against historical controls, with exactly the bias problem that makes the human single-arm results in this setting hard to read. The inhaled IL-15 phase 2 result (PMID 41209004) is the clearest case: it is a negative finding relative to a historical cohort, which is also how OST-HER2's positive figure is generated.
  • Hypothesis 1 is our inference, not anyone's published claim. No paper we found proposes IL-1 or IL-6 blockade in the post-surgical window; we are joining a dog trial's failure, its correlative cytokine data and two mouse mechanism papers, and that trial's plasma cytokines did not separate dogs who relapsed from those who did not. It should be read as a lead to check, not a conclusion.
  • The 33,000-record PubTator file is a snapshot and, as reports/Osteosarcoma literature map.md documents, a measurable slice of the osteosarcoma literature is unreliable. The ezrin and EMT counts here include that contamination.
  • One worked example of how the keyword method can mislead. Our count returns 37 GM-CSF papers and zero with an in vivo metastasis endpoint, which would suggest inhaled GM-CSF reached a 43-patient COG trial on no animal metastasis evidence at all. But the trial paper itself opens "based on preliminary data on the antitumor effects of GM-CSF in animal models" (PMID 20576718). The preclinical work exists; it is indexed under terms the regex does not catch, or sits outside an osteosarcoma-titled paper. Read every zero in this report as "our keywords found none", not "none exists".

  • November 14, 2026, after an independent audit that re-checked all 44 cited PMIDs and all 21 cited trial records:
  • Surveillance trial. The summary said cheap X-rays every six months "did no worse" than CT every three. The trial was a 2x2 design: X-ray was non-inferior to CT, but six-monthly was not shown non-inferior to three-monthly (HR 1.2, upper 90% CI 1.47). Corrected in both places.
