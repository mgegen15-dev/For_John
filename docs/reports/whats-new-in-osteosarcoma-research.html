What's New in Osteosarcoma Research

A scan of papers, preprints and trials from June 1, 2025 to November 19, 2026, checked against this project's reports and leads, plus a protein-level check of the new surface targets. Built November 19, 2026 from PubMed, Europe PMC, bioRxiv/medRxiv, ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 and the Human Protein Atlas. Working notes: research_notes/Whats new/scan_2025-06_to_now.md and research_notes/Surface targets/protein_check.md.

Summary

The biggest news is a randomised phase 3 trial in relapsed osteosarcoma in which a drug aimed at B7-H3 held off progression for a median 5.5 months against 1.4 with chemotherapy, at the cost of heavier side effects. A preprint (not yet peer-reviewed) from the Children's Oncology Group reports, from 99 patients, that only about one in five stays relapse-free a year after lung metastases are removed, and its authors conclude that only randomised trials can detect modest gains. LRRC15, the top hit in our surface-target screen, now has protein evidence from three independent 2026 studies (two in patient tumours, one in patient-derived xenografts), adding to a 2020 staining study of 69 tumours, so the trials already testing it look well aimed. The other RNA leads are weaker than they looked: PTK7 has been stained only in two patient-derived xenografts and two cell lines, and in no series of patient tumours, and we found no published measurement of IFITM5 or ADAM12 protein in human osteosarcoma tissue (PubMed, November 2026). New genetic work shows that MYC amplification predicts poor survival and, in a preprint, that cell lines from MYC-driven mouse tumours resist cabozantinib in the laboratory, which any test of our kinase-pill lead needs to account for.

How to read the labels. Key numbers carry one of these tags:

  • (registry): read directly from an official registry or database (ClinicalTrials.gov, PubMed counts, Human Protein Atlas).
  • (paper, full text): we read the full paper.
  • (paper, abstract only): we read only the abstract. Preprints are marked (preprint, abstract only) and are not peer-reviewed.
  • (press release only): the only source is a company or press page.
  • (our calculation): computed by us from the sources or this repository's data.

Part A: Do the Surface Targets Exist as Protein?

Our screen ranked targets from RNA. We checked each lead against the Human Protein Atlas and the published staining literature. HPA has no sarcoma tissue, so it speaks only to normal tissue, and it has no tissue staining at all for LRRC15 or IFITM5 (registry). (HPA does list LRRC15 as "tissue enriched: skin", apparently from non-staining data, so it has some protein-level annotation, but no staining.)

Two other proteins look better at protein level than our RNA list suggested. TMEM119 ranks 5th overall by RNA but was dropped because HPA gives it low surface confidence (our calculation); a 2026 study shows surface staining in osteosarcoma and little in 21 of 22 normal children's tissues (paper, full text). ROR2 (our shortlist rank 55) was nearly absent from 25 normal children's tissues (paper, full text). Five of our top 25 RNA candidates (MRC2, CD109, EPHB2, EPHA3, EMP3) were independently found enriched as protein in the PDX study (our calculation from the paper's text).

One tissue array would settle most of this: osteosarcoma primaries with paired lung metastases and relapses, plus normal growth plate, stained for LRRC15, PTK7, TMEM119 and CD109, with IFITM5 by RNA in-situ staining. COG's and St. Jude's osteosarcoma arrays already exist.

LRRC15 trials (registry, November 19, 2026). Lu-TARGO (LNTH-2403, NCT07357519) recruiting at 2 US sites; ZL-6201 (NCT07374848) recruiting at 12 sites in China and the US, record updated November 18; SOT106 (NCT07801222) still "not yet recruiting", its planned September 30 start not yet updated; NCT07272642 not yet recruiting. That last record now names its drug as "LNTH-2403 (177Lu-DOTA-DUNP19)", so it is the same antibody as Lu-TARGO. None has posted results. Company news about SOT106 designations and posters was seen only in search summaries, because the pages were blocked here, so we do not quote it. Two PTK7 drugs were missing from our surface report: IDE034, a B7-H3 × PTK7 ADC (NCT07503808, recruiting), and Lilly's LY4175408 (NCT07046923), which we take to be a PTK7 drug because its exclusion criteria bar earlier PTK7 ADCs with the same payload class (registry; the target is our inference).

What Should Change in Existing Reports

These are the corrections and updates we found. They were applied to the named reports on November 19, 2026; each report's change log records what changed.

  1. Osteosarcoma research landscape and gaps. ARTEMIS-011 is now a journal paper (Ann Oncol 2026, PMID 42933637); replace "no journal paper yet (press release only)". Corrected November 19: there is no CI conflict. The abstract gives the progression hazard ratio as 0.33 (95% CI 0.20–0.55), matching data/osteosarcoma_relapse_benchmarks_published.csv; the 0.21–1.23 interval belongs to the immature overall-survival hazard ratio (0.51).
  2. Same report. The PEPN1924 result is cited to PMID 40911899, a preclinical paper. Cite the trial paper, PMID 42131734 (1 of 9 with stable disease past 24 weeks).
  3. Same report and the pooled control dataset. The resected lung-relapse benchmark (20%, 95% CI 10–34%, n = 42, AOST0221) has a larger update: 22% (15–31%), n = 99 (preprint, abstract only).
  4. New surface targets and Ranked leads. NCT07272642 is LNTH-2403, the same antibody as Lu-TARGO; drop "could not confirm". Add the LRRC15 protein data; state that IFITM5 and ADAM12 have no human protein data; note that ADAM12's "Enhanced" HPA grade rests on antibodies that disagree; add IDE034 and LY4175408; note cofetuzumab's NCT04189614 was terminated. The "6 osteosarcoma papers name LRRC15" figure is out of date: PubMed returns 11 for "LRRC15 AND osteosarcoma", 7 of them from 2025–26 (registry).
  5. New surface targets. The HPA surface filter excluded TMEM119, which now has surface staining in patient tumours. Consider re-running the shortlist with TMEM119 admitted.
  6. Why kinase-inhibitor pills work. Add MYC status to the proposed amplification test (lead 2): MYC amplification predicts poor survival and, in cell lines from MYC-driven mouse tumours, cabozantinib resistance (in the mice themselves, cabozantinib-containing regimens still delayed progression). CabOSTar's posted results contain no efficacy data.
  7. Osteosarcoma clinical trials landscape. JCOG0905 (randomised, negative) is one more osteosarcoma-only randomised trial to check against the count of 27. Checked November 19: it is registered only in jRCT (jRCTs031180126), so it is outside ClinicalTrials.gov and not among the 27.

Limits

  • Most items were read as abstracts only. We read the full text of four papers: Mooney 2026, O'Reilly 2026, Moquin-Beaudry 2026 and Lee 2023.
  • About 450 PubMed titles, 100 preprint titles and 63 trial records were screened, out of 2,665 PubMed osteosarcoma records in the window (query osteosarcoma[tiab], publication dates June 1, 2025 to November 19, 2026; the same query returned 2,670 on November 20). Single-gene laboratory papers and herbal-compound studies were skipped on purpose, so a relevant laboratory paper could have been missed.
  • We did not search conference abstract books (ASCO, ESMO, CTOS) directly. Company and trade-press pages were blocked from this environment, so the SOT106, ZL-6201 and LNTH-2403 company updates were not read.
  • The Human Protein Atlas has no sarcoma or bone tissue, and no tissue staining for LRRC15 or IFITM5. Its normal-tissue maps are from adults.
  • Preprints are not peer-reviewed.
  • Counts of "new" vs "in repo" depend on PMID and NCT matching against this repository's files.
