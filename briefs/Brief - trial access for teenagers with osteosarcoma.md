# Research brief: new targeted-drug trials mostly leave out teenagers with osteosarcoma

*For John project · November 2026 · Based on public data and ClinicalTrials.gov records read on November 24, 2026 (engine cycles 3, 4 and 7). Written by a family member working with AI tools, and checked line by line against its sources by a separate review; not peer-reviewed. Not medical advice, and not evidence that any of these drugs works in osteosarcoma.*

## Summary

Two gene changes found in relapsed osteosarcoma now have drugs in clinical trials that pick patients by that change, whatever their cancer. We counted the trials and read their entry rules. Adults with these changes have trials to ask about. Teenagers, the age group in which osteosarcoma is most often diagnosed, have almost none: of 34 trials an osteosarcoma patient with one of these changes could join on paper, only two accepted anyone under 18 when we read the registry, and only one of those enrols in the United States or Europe. Age limits in early trials have real reasons, set out below, but US regulators have recommended since 2019 that adolescents be included once those reasons are met, and a few trials of these drugs already do.

**How to read the labels.** **(our calculation)**: counted by this project from open data. **(registry)**: a ClinicalTrials.gov record. **(FDA guidance)**: a published US Food and Drug Administration guidance document.

## The two gene changes

| Gene change | Relapsed osteosarcomas with it | Drug type being tested | Trials an osteosarcoma patient could join on paper | Of those, open under 18 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MTAP loss** (part of a 9p21 deletion) | About 1 in 7 (an estimate; about 1 in 5 have the wider 9p21 deletion) | Mostly PRMT5 or MAT2A inhibitors | 25 recruiting or about to; 12 with a US site | **1**, in China only |
| **CCNE1 amplification** | About 1 in 10 on clinical gene tests | PKMYT1, CDK2 or WEE1 inhibitors | 9 recruiting or about to; 6 with a US site | **1**: lunresertib, from age 12 |

Frequencies (our calculation) from 341 patients in two clinical sequencing cohorts, plus 49 from a European relapse trial; trial counts (registry). In 30 patients tested twice, the result usually matched between the first tumour and a later sample, but few of them had either change (7 with the 9p21 deletion, 3 with CCNE1 amplification), so a test at diagnosis is a guide, not a guarantee. A trial may accept an earlier report or ask for a new test.

## What is open now

- **Lunresertib** (NCT04855656, Debiopharm): any advanced solid tumour with CCNE1 amplification (or certain FBXW7 or PPP2R1A mutations), **from age 12** (under-18s must weigh at least 40 kg), with sites in the United States, Canada, Denmark, Spain and the United Kingdom (registry).
- **TNG462, also called vopimetostat** (NCT05732831, Tango Therapeutics): has a group for **MTAP-deleted sarcoma, soft tissue or bone**, from age 18, in the United States, France and Spain (registry).
- **GH56** (NCT06796699): any advanced solid tumour with MTAP loss, from age 12, sites in China only (registry).
- The other 31 trials start at 18 or older (one at 19). Full lists with registry numbers: [engine cycle 4](../reports/engine-cycle-4-mtap-selected-trials-open-to-osteosarcoma.html) and [engine cycle 7](../reports/engine-cycle-7-ccne1-amplification-at-relapse-and-the-trials-open-to-it.html).

## Why age limits exist, and what regulators recommend

- **The reasons are real.** The first trials of a new drug look for a safe dose and its side effects, and there is no dosing information for children yet. Thirty-two of the 34 trials counted here are at that early stage (phase 1 or 1/2), so starting in adults is a normal, cautious choice (our calculation).
- **Regulators have addressed it.** In 2019 the US Food and Drug Administration issued guidance, *Considerations for the Inclusion of Adolescent Patients in Adult Oncology Clinical Trials*, recommending that patients aged 12 to 17 be eligible for adult cancer trials relevant to their disease or the drug's target. For first-in-human and dose-finding trials it suggests including adolescents once some adult dosing and side-effect data exist; for trials that measure whether a drug works, at the same time as adults. It is guidance, not a requirement (FDA guidance).
- **The pattern is wider.** Of 1,434 early-phase "solid tumour" drug trials recruiting on November 26, 2026, 78 (5%) took anyone under 18; of 57 relapse trials designed for osteosarcoma, 43 do ([engine cycle 12](../reports/engine-cycle-12-how-often-can-a-teenager-with-osteosarcoma-get-into-a-drug-trial.html); our calculation).

## Some trials of these drugs already include adolescents

- **Lunresertib**, above, from age 12 in five countries (registry).
- **An investigator-led, single-centre US trial of navlimetostat** for malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, another sarcoma, takes patients with MTAP loss from age 12 (NCT07549022; registry).
- **AstraZeneca's AZD3470 trial in lymphoma** admits patients from age 12 (at least 40 kg) in one Hodgkin lymphoma part (NCT06137144; registry).
- **The opposite also happens:** one closed CDK2-inhibitor study had a group for "Other tumor indications excluding bone cancers" (NCT05238922), with no reason given (registry).

## What we are asking

1. **Open the tumour-agnostic MTAP and CCNE1 trials to patients aged 12 and over with sarcoma** once adult dosing data allow, in line with the FDA guidance and as the lunresertib and nerve-sheath-tumour trials already do. The sponsors are listed in engine cycles 4 and 7.
2. **Report osteosarcoma separately** within the TNG462 sarcoma group, so that the first osteosarcoma result is not lost in a pooled sarcoma figure.
3. **Measure MTAP loss in relapsed osteosarcoma directly.** Our one-in-seven figure is an estimate; only 15 relapsed patients in the data had MTAP measured directly, 2 of whom had lost it. A routine MTAP stain on stored samples would settle it. A hospital in Beijing has registered such a study (NCT07593573); a US sarcoma centre could do the same.
4. **For clinicians:** many clinical gene panels report CDKN2A deletion and CCNE1 amplification; fewer report MTAP, which may need a newer panel or the stain. MTAP trials usually require both copies to be lost. Whether any trial suits a particular patient is a question for that patient's oncologist.

## Limits

These are registry records on one day; trials open and close, and "any solid tumour" can hide limits. Most of these trials also require disease that can be measured on scans, which rules out patients whose tumours have all been removed. No osteosarcoma result has been reported for any of these drugs that we found, and our laboratory-data checks (cycles 5 and 6) could not confirm in osteosarcoma cell lines the weaknesses the drugs aim at, because too few lines carry the changes. This brief argues for access to trials, not for any treatment. Corrections are welcome and will be published.
