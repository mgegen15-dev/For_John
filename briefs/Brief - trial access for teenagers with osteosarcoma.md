# Research brief: new targeted-drug trials mostly leave out teenagers with osteosarcoma

*For John project · November 2026 · Based on public data and ClinicalTrials.gov records read on November 24, 2026 (engine cycles 3, 4 and 7). Written by a family member working with AI tools; not peer-reviewed. Not medical advice, and not evidence that any of these drugs works in osteosarcoma.*

## Summary

Two gene changes found in relapsed osteosarcoma now have drugs in clinical trials that pick patients by that change, whatever their cancer. We counted the trials and read their entry rules. Adults with these changes have options to ask about. Teenagers, the age group in which osteosarcoma is most often diagnosed, have almost none: of 34 trials that would take an osteosarcoma patient on paper, only two accepted anyone under 18 when we read the registry, and only one of those enrols in the United States or Europe. Age limits in early trials have real reasons, set out below, but US regulators have recommended for several years that adolescents be included once those reasons are met, and some sponsors have already done so for other rare cancers.

**How to read the labels.** **(our calculation)**: counted by this project from open data. **(registry)**: a ClinicalTrials.gov record. **(FDA guidance)**: a published US Food and Drug Administration guidance document.

## The two gene changes

| Gene change | Relapsed osteosarcomas with it | Drug type being tested | Trials an osteosarcoma patient could join on paper | Of those, open under 18 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MTAP loss** (part of a 9p21 deletion) | About 1 in 7 (estimate; about 1 in 5 have the wider 9p21 deletion) | PRMT5 or MAT2A inhibitors | 25 recruiting or about to; 12 with a US site | **1**, in China only |
| **CCNE1 amplification** | About 1 in 10 on hospital tests | PKMYT1, CDK2 or WEE1 inhibitors | 9 recruiting or about to; 6 with a US site | **1**: lunresertib, from age 12 |

Figures (our calculation) from 341 osteosarcoma patients in two hospital sequencing cohorts and a European relapse trial; trial counts (registry). Both changes were nearly always the same in a patient's primary tumour and later relapse, so the test done at diagnosis is a fair guide.

## What is open now

- **Lunresertib** (NCT04855656, Debiopharm): any advanced solid tumour with CCNE1 amplification, **from age 12** (at least 40 kg), with sites in the United States, Canada, Denmark, Spain and the United Kingdom (registry).
- **Vopimetostat** (NCT05732831, Tango Therapeutics): has a group for **MTAP-deleted sarcoma, soft tissue or bone**, from age 18, in the United States, France and Spain (registry).
- **GH56** (NCT06796699): any advanced solid tumour with MTAP loss, from age 12, sites in China only (registry).
- The other 31 trials start at 18. Lists with registry numbers: `reports/Engine cycle 4 - MTAP-selected trials open to osteosarcoma.md` and `reports/Engine cycle 7 - CCNE1 amplification at relapse and the trials open to it.md`.

## Why age limits exist, and what regulators recommend

- **The reasons are real.** The first trials of a new drug look for a safe dose and its side effects, and there is no dosing information for children yet. Many of the trials counted here are at that stage, so starting in adults is a normal, cautious choice.
- **Regulators have addressed it.** In 2019 the US Food and Drug Administration issued guidance, *Considerations for the Inclusion of Adolescent Patients in Adult Oncology Clinical Trials*, recommending that patients aged 12 to 17 be eligible for adult cancer trials relevant to their disease or the drug's target. For first-in-human and dose-finding trials it suggests including adolescents once some adult dosing and side-effect data exist; for trials that measure whether a drug works, at the same time as adults. It is guidance, not a requirement (FDA guidance).

## Sponsors have already done this for other rare cancers

- A navlimetostat trial for malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour, another sarcoma, takes patients **from age 12** in the United States (NCT07549022; registry).
- A mesothelioma trial in the United Kingdom takes patients from 16 (NCT07602946), and a lymphoma trial of AZD3470 takes patients from 12 in one part (NCT06137144) (registry).
- One closed CDK2-inhibitor study had a group for "other tumour indications **excluding bone cancers**" (NCT05238922), with no reason given (registry).

## What we are asking

1. **Open the tumour-agnostic MTAP and CCNE1 trials to patients aged 12 and over with sarcoma** once adult dosing data allow, in line with the FDA guidance and as the lunresertib and nerve-sheath-tumour trials already do. The relevant sponsors are listed in engine cycles 4 and 7.
2. **Report osteosarcoma separately** within the vopimetostat sarcoma group, so that the first osteosarcoma result is not lost in a pooled sarcoma figure.
3. **Measure MTAP loss in relapsed osteosarcoma directly.** Our one-in-seven figure rests on 15 directly tested patients. A routine MTAP stain on stored samples would settle it. A hospital in Beijing has registered such a study (NCT07593573); a US sarcoma centre could do the same.
4. **For clinicians:** many hospital gene panels report CDKN2A deletion and CCNE1 amplification; fewer report MTAP, which may need a newer panel or the stain. Whether any trial suits a particular patient is a question for that patient's oncologist.

## Limits

These are registry records on one day; trials open and close, and "any solid tumour" can hide limits. No osteosarcoma result has been reported for any of these drugs that we found, and our laboratory-data checks (cycles 5 and 6) could not confirm in osteosarcoma cell lines the weaknesses the drugs aim at, because too few lines carry the changes. This brief argues for access to trials, not for any treatment. Corrections are welcome and will be published.
