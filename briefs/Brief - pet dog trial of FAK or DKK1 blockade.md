# Research brief: a pet-dog trial of FAK or DKK1 blockade in osteosarcoma

*For John project · November 2026 · Supporting data and methods: this repository. Not medical advice.*

## The question

Can blocking FAK or DKK1, with drugs that already exist, reduce lung metastasis after amputation in dogs with naturally occurring osteosarcoma?

## Why these two targets

We scored all 1,494 genes named in at least five of the 33,242 osteosarcoma papers in PubMed. We linked each to the 603 registered osteosarcoma treatment trials and to NIH grants, then read the abstracts for the strongest untested candidates.

**FAK (PTK2).**
- **Mouse evidence:** a clinical-stage FAK inhibitor given by mouth reduced osteosarcoma xenograft growth and blood-vessel formation (PMID 28406574).
- **Patient evidence:** activated FAK (pY397) is high in about 62% of tumours (PMID 28846700), and a FAK-pathway panel separated chemotherapy responders from non-responders (PMID 23845472).
- **Literature quality:** clean, with 1 retraction in 95 papers since 2010.
- **Drug:** defactinib, FDA-approved in 2025 with avutometinib for KRAS-mutant low-grade serous ovarian cancer.
- **Trials:** none in osteosarcoma.

**DKK1.**
- **Mouse evidence:** three different ways of blocking DKK1 reduced metastasis and preserved bone in mice. These were an anti-DKK1 antibody close to DKN-01 in patient-derived orthotopic models (PMID 27049730), a morpholino (PMIDs 35277659, 39805917) and a small molecule (PMIDs 39781890, 39713389).
- **Drug:** DKN-01 (sirexatamab), in clinical trials for gastric and other cancers.
- **Trials:** none in osteosarcoma.
- **Caution:** the work comes largely from one research lineage, a few papers report the opposite direction, and two of 28 papers since 2010 are retracted. Independent replication is the priority.

**What neither target has:** any dog data, any test of the actual clinical drug in osteosarcoma, or a trial.

## Why dogs

Pet dogs develop osteosarcoma about ten times as often as people. The disease is biologically similar, and over 90% of dogs die of lung metastases within two years of amputation. A post-amputation study can test anti-metastatic drugs, the gap that matters most for children, within one to two years. NIH already funds canine osteosarcoma studies through the NCI Comparative Oncology Program and cooperative agreements at UC Davis and Tufts.

## Why it is timely

- NIH already funds osteosarcoma work on both targets: FAK inhibition at Colorado State (a veterinary school, R37) and DKK-1 at Albert Einstein College of Medicine (R01) and Texas A&M (R21).
- Kinase-inhibitor pills are the only drug class clearly above the historical benchmark in relapsed osteosarcoma (`reports/Osteosarcoma pooled control dataset.md`). But the US first-line cabozantinib trial is suspended and an industry maintenance trial closed after 10 patients, so new candidates are needed.
- MDM2, the most-studied untested target, does not hold up on close reading, which narrows the field to these two.

## A possible design, for discussion

- **Population:** client-owned dogs with appendicular osteosarcoma, after amputation and standard carboplatin.
- **Arms:** standard care with or without defactinib (or DKN-01), randomized, or single-arm against the large historical canine control data.
- **Endpoints:** time to lung metastasis, plus activated FAK or serum DKK1 measured at baseline and during treatment, to define who benefits.
- **Before the dog study:** confirm drug activity in canine osteosarcoma cell lines, and check how much FAK and DKK1 dog tumours express. A small, cheap step.

## What we are asking

1. Is this question already being pursued, or has it been tried and abandoned?
2. Would a pet-dog study be feasible through an existing consortium, and roughly what would it cost?
3. What evidence would you need first?

## Caveats

- These findings come from automated analysis of abstracts plus a close reading, not from new experiments.
- Mouse results were often from single laboratories.
- Full texts were not read for every paper.
- Drug availability for veterinary studies would need to be confirmed with the manufacturers.
