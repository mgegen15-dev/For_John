# Neuroblastoma Research & Treatment Landscape: Lessons for Osteosarcoma (notes current to Nov 2026)

Note on method: only a few web searches were run (DFMO approval, GD2-CART01, AOST1421, ANBL1531, OS CAR-T). Many citations below point to landmark primary papers by DOI/PubMed link from well-established literature, and they were not re-fetched this session. Any number marked "(verify)" should be checked before it is quoted.

## Biology of high-risk neuroblastoma (and shared biology with OS)

### Takeaway
High-risk NB (HR-NB) has few somatic mutations and is driven mainly by copy-number changes: MYCN amplification, 11q loss, 1p loss and 17q gain. Three mutually exclusive ways of maintaining telomeres (MYCN-amp, TERT rearrangement, ATRX loss/ALT) define its prognosis, and ALK is the main actionable kinase. Osteosarcoma (OS) is also copy-number driven with low SNV burden, and it shares the ALT/ATRX axis and the immunotherapy surface targets GD2 and B7-H3. OS is driven mainly by TP53/RB1 loss and chromothripsis rather than by a single oncogene.

### Cited Findings
- MYCN amplification occurs in about 20% of NB overall and about 40-50% of high-risk cases. It is the strongest adverse genomic marker and is used in INRG staging — [Matthay et al., Nat Rev Dis Primers 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrdp.2016.78)
- ALK activating mutations (hotspots F1174, R1275, F1245) occur in about 8-10% of tumors and cause most familial NB. About 14% of high-risk tumors have an ALK aberration (mutation or amplification), which is linked to inferior survival — [FDA document on ANBL1531](https://fda.gov/media/158240/download); [Mossé et al., Nature 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07261)
- In ANBL0532 (JCO 2025), activating ALK mutations at a variant allele frequency of 5% or more gave 5-year OS of 37.7%, against 66.3% for ALK wild-type tumors — [Bresler/ COG, JCO 2025 via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12058367)
- Telomere maintenance defines prognosis. Tumors use one of three mutually exclusive mechanisms: TERT rearrangement, MYCN amplification (both TERT-high), or ALT associated with ATRX alterations. Tumors with no telomere-maintenance mechanism do very well. Adding RAS/p53-pathway mutations identifies the worst outcomes — [Ackermann et al., Science 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat6768); [Peifer et al., Nature 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14980)
- ATRX alterations are age-linked: they are enriched in adolescents/young adults with chronic, indolent but ultimately lethal disease — [Cheung et al., JAMA 2012](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.228)
- The adrenergic (ADRN) vs mesenchymal (MES) cell states are defined by distinct core regulatory circuits (PHOX2B/HAND2/GATA3 vs PRRX1/AP-1/RUNX). Cells can switch between them. MES cells are more chemoresistant and lose GD2 and the MIBG transporter (NET) — [van Groningen et al., Nat Genet 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3899); [Boeva et al., Nat Genet 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3921)
- Spontaneous regression in stage 4S/MS infants (under 18 months, with metastases limited to skin, liver and marrow) is thought to involve delayed neurotrophin (TrkA/NGF) dependence and developmental apoptosis, and possibly a lack of telomere maintenance. Many of these infants survive with observation or minimal therapy — [Brodeur, Nat Rev Cancer 2003 / 2018 review](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41568-018-0003-x)
- OS frequently has ALT/ATRX loss, with ALT reported in roughly half of OS (verify). OS has near-universal TP53 pathway disruption, chromothripsis/kataegis, and MDM2/CDK4 amplification in low-grade parosteal/central OS — [Chen et al., Cell Rep 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2014.03.003); [Behjati et al., Nat Commun 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-01461-6)

### Inferences
- In both diseases, chromosomal instability and ALT drive the disease rather than one targetable oncogene, apart from MYCN/ALK in NB. NB data suggest that the telomere-maintenance mechanism is a prognostic axis, and this has not been systematically applied to OS risk stratification. This is a candidate gap.
- In NB, TP53 mutation is rare at diagnosis and enriched at relapse, while in OS it is near-universal. MDM2 inhibitors (idasanutlin trials in NB) therefore transfer poorly to most OS, though they may fit MDM2-amplified OS subtypes.
- Cell-state plasticity (ADRN to MES) as a way to escape GD2 or MIBG therapy has a likely OS analogue in antigen-low or heterogeneous GD2 expression. The ADRN/MES framework could inform OS immunotherapy failure analysis.

### Gaps
- Exact ALT prevalence in OS and its prognostic value were not verified this session.
- No OS equivalent of the "no telomere maintenance means favorable" finding was found.

## Treatment evolution and survival gains for HR-NB

### Takeaway
HR-NB survival rose from under 15% (1980s) to about 50-60% long-term EFS and roughly 60-70% OS in recent COG cohorts (verify the latest numbers). Each gain came from a sequential, randomized COG/SIOPEN trial: myeloablative ASCT with 13-cis-RA (CCG-3891), tandem transplant (ANBL0532), and anti-GD2 plus cytokines (ANBL0032).

### Cited Findings
- CCG-3891 (Matthay 1999) showed that myeloablative chemotherapy with purged ASCT was better than consolidation chemotherapy, with 3-year EFS of 34% vs 22%. 13-cis-retinoic acid then improved EFS further (46% vs 29%) — [Matthay et al., NEJM 1999](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199910143411601)
- ANBL0032 (Yu 2010) found that dinutuximab + GM-CSF + IL-2 + isotretinoin gave 2-year EFS of 66% vs 46% with isotretinoin alone, and OS of 86% vs 75%. The randomization was stopped early for efficacy. Long-term follow-up (Yu 2021, JCO) confirmed the 5-year EFS benefit, with a narrowed absolute difference of about 57% vs 46% (verify) — [Yu et al., NEJM 2010](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0911123)
- ANBL0532 (Park 2019) compared tandem with single transplant. Among patients who received immunotherapy, 3-year EFS was about 73.7% vs 56.5% (verify), and tandem transplant became the COG standard — [Park et al., JAMA 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2019.11642)
- SIOPEN HR-NBL1 established busulfan-melphalan as superior to CEM conditioning [Ladenstein, Lancet Oncol 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(17)30070-0). It also found that IL-2 added toxicity without benefit to dinutuximab beta [Ladenstein, Lancet Oncol 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(18)30578-3). The two cooperative groups therefore reached divergent standards.
- In the DFMO approval analysis, the external control (ANBL0032 cohort) had 4-year EFS of 72% in patients who had completed immunotherapy. This shows how strongly survivors after GD2 immunotherapy are pre-selected — [FDA/TargetedOnc summary](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/fda-approves-eflornithine-to-reduce-risk-of-relapse-in-high-risk-neuroblastoma)

### Inferences
- The largest single increment was anti-GD2 immunotherapy, at about +20 points of EFS at 2 years and about +11 points long term. OS has no post-1980s increment of that size: MAP chemotherapy has been standard since the 1980s, and EURAMOS-1 (MAPIE, MAP+IFN) added nothing.
- The NB model has three parts: a randomized question every 5-8 years, with a biology-defined population and a backbone kept constant. OS has had fewer, larger, mostly negative randomized trials.

### Gaps
- The most current (2024-2026) pooled 5-year EFS/OS for HR-NB on modern therapy could not be verified this session.

## Recent advances (2020-2026)

### Takeaway
New in this period: DFMO maintenance (FDA-approved, controversially, on an external control), naxitamab (FDA 2020), GD2-CART01 (an academic CAR-T with durable responses), and ALK/MIBG questions in front-line therapy (ANBL1531 results still pending). Relapse therapy now uses chemoimmunotherapy as standard.

### Cited Findings
- Eflornithine (Iwilfin) was approved by the FDA in December 2023 for HR-NB after at least a partial response to multimodal therapy including anti-GD2. The evidence came from single-arm NMTRC003B (n=92) against an ANBL0032 external control (n=852): 4-year EFS 84% vs 72%, HR 0.50. ODAC voted 14-6 in favor. FDA reviewers said they had never before relied on a single externally controlled trial for an oncology approval, and some panelists warned of a "slippery slope" and possible overestimation of benefit — [TargetedOnc](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/fda-approves-eflornithine-to-reduce-risk-of-relapse-in-high-risk-neuroblastoma); [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/fda-adcomm-backs-us-worldmeds-neuroblastoma-treatment-even-without-rct-data); [Pink Sheet](https://pink.citeline.com/PS148995/US-WorldMeds-Neuroblastoma-Drug-External-Controls-Confirmatory-Evidence-And-A-Concern-About-Precedent); [FDA ODAC briefing](https://fda.gov/media/173794/download)
- Naxitamab (Danyelza) received accelerated approval in November 2020 with GM-CSF for relapsed/refractory HR-NB with bone/marrow disease (Study 201 / MSKCC 12-230; ORR about 45% (verify)) — [FDA approval notice](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-naxitamab-high-risk-neuroblastoma-bone-or-bone-marrow)
- GD2-CART01 (Del Bufalo, NEJM 2023; 388:1284-95; n=27 R/R HR-NB). It included an iCasp9 suicide switch and the costimulatory domains CD28 and 4-1BB. ORR was 63% (9 CR, 8 PR). At the recommended dose, 3-year OS was 60% and EFS 36%. CRS occurred in 74% and was mostly mild. CAR-T cells persisted for up to 30 months — [Healio](https://cm.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20230405/car-t-cells-can-eradicate-solid-tumors-study-in-advanced-neuroblastoma-shows); [ESMO](https://esmo.org/oncology-news/gd2-cart01-may-induce-sustained-antitumour-effect-in-a-proportion-of-patients-with-relapsed-or-refractory-neuroblastoma)
- ANBL1531 (NCT03126916) has two questions. The first randomizes MIBG-avid, ALK-wild-type patients to standard therapy, standard therapy with 131I-MIBG in induction, or 131I-MIBG with single BuMel ASCT; that randomization closed on 28 September 2023, with no results reported as of the sources found. The second, Arm E (lorlatinib for ALK-aberrant tumors), remains open — [ASTRO abstract](https://amportal.astro.org/sessions/pqa-02-22836/a-phase-iii-study-of-131i-metaiodobenzylguanidine-131i-mibg-or-lorlatinib-added-to-intensive-110824); [St. Jude](https://www.stjude.org/care-treatment/clinical-trials/anbl1531-neuroblastoma.html)
- Lorlatinib given with anti-GD2 antibody may be associated with severe pulmonary toxicity, which appeared to be mitigated by holding lorlatinib during immunotherapy (Pediatric Blood & Cancer 2025, retrospective). An ALK-driven cohort (npj Precis Oncol 2026, n=25) found responses were best with hotspot mutations and worse with co-occurring MYCN amplification or ALK amplification — [search summary of PMC/EMJ sources](https://www.emjreviews.com/?p=209006)
- In relapse, ANBL1221 tested irinotecan/temozolomide with dinutuximab/GM-CSF and found an ORR of about 53% vs 6% with temsirolimus (verify), making chemoimmunotherapy the de facto relapse standard — [Mody et al., Lancet Oncol 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(17)30355-8)
- Front-line chemoimmunotherapy: the St. Jude pilot (Furman, JCO 2022) added hu14.18K322A to induction chemotherapy and reported a 3-year EFS of about 74% (verify). COG ANBL1232/ANBL2131-type studies are testing this approach — [Furman et al., JCO 2022](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.21.01375)

### Inferences
- The DFMO approval created a regulatory precedent: an externally controlled single trial, supported by mechanistic and animal data, using a high-quality cooperative-group control. Rare-disease OS sponsors could follow the same path, and OS has strong historical controls such as AOST0331 and the INT-0133 and EURAMOS datasets. A caution: OS outcomes have stagnated for decades, which makes historical controls cleaner for OS than for NB, where outcomes keep shifting.
- Moving anti-GD2 into induction (St. Jude) is the NB lesson OS has not tested. OS tested GD2 only in minimal residual disease after relapse.

### Gaps
- ANBL1531 EFS results were not found. They may have been reported at ASCO/SIOP 2025-2026 after the indexed sources.
- B7-H3 clinical data in NB (e.g., the omburtamab 131I-intrathecal program, which received an FDA CRL in 2022, and Seattle Children's B7-H3 CAR-T) were not re-verified this session.

## Risk stratification, de-escalation and data sharing

### Takeaway
INRG (2009) combined about 8,800 patients (verify) to define 16 pretreatment groups, using age, image-defined risk factor (IDRF)-based stage, histology, MYCN, 11q and ploidy. That system let cooperative groups safely cut therapy for low- and intermediate-risk patients and focus intensification on HR-NB. OS has no biology-based pretreatment classifier comparable to INRG.

### Cited Findings
- The INRG classification system was built from 8,800 patients in COG, GPOH, JNBSG and SIOPEN, and defines very-low, low, intermediate and high pretreatment risk — [Cohn et al., JCO 2009](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2008.16.6785)
- De-escalation in COG ANBL0531 reduced chemotherapy for intermediate-risk patients while keeping 3-year OS around 95% or higher (verify) — [Twist et al., JCO 2019](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.18.00419)
- The Pediatric Cancer Data Commons (University of Chicago) grew out of the INRG Data Commons and now includes soft-tissue sarcoma, germ cell and other disease consortia — [PCDC](https://commons.cri.uchicago.edu/)

### Inferences
- OS risk is still assigned mainly after treatment, from metastatic status and histologic necrosis. Randomizing on necrosis (intensifying poor responders in EURAMOS-1 with MAPIE) failed. OS lacks a prospectively validated genomic classifier. Candidate classifier features include ALT status, MYC/8q gain, CDKN2A loss and the burden of the chromothripsis signature. Assembling one via a PCDC OS consortium would be a direct transfer of the INRG model (verify whether an OS PCDC/"International OS Data Commons" effort exists).
- Infant 4S regression shows that some NB biology is self-limiting. OS has no comparable favorable subgroup, so OS cannot copy the de-escalation half of the NB model. OS can copy the intensification half: a pretreatment biological high-risk definition.

### Gaps
- Whether a PCDC osteosarcoma module or an equivalent OS genomic risk group exists as of 2026 was not verified.

## What remains unsolved in neuroblastoma

### Takeaway
Relapsed HR-NB remains largely fatal: long-term survival is under 10-20% historically. MYCN is still undruggable directly, late effects are severe, and resistance to anti-GD2 (GD2-low or MES cells, immunosuppressive microenvironment) limits cure.

### Cited Findings
- Indirect approaches to MYCN include Aurora A inhibition (alisertib, which stabilizes and degrades N-Myc), BET inhibitors, CDK7 (THZ1) and CDK9, and ODC1/polyamine blockade (DFMO). ODC1 is a MYCN transcriptional target — [Chipumuro et al., Cell 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2014.10.024); [Brockmann et al., Cancer Cell 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccr.2013.05.005)
- MES-state cells have lower GD2 synthase expression, are less MIBG-avid, and are enriched after therapy, which links plasticity to immunotherapy escape — [van Groningen 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3899)
- Late effects in survivors include hearing loss, endocrine dysfunction, secondary malignancies and growth failure, and they are common after tandem ASCT, MIBG and radiation — [Matthay, Nat Rev Dis Primers 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrdp.2016.78)

### Inferences
- The NB problems that map onto OS are antigen escape (heterogeneous GD2 and B7-H3), a myeloid-rich immunosuppressive microenvironment, and late effects (cardiotoxicity from doxorubicin and ototoxicity from cisplatin in OS).

### Gaps
- Current published survival after first relapse on chemoimmunotherapy-era therapy was not verified.

## Which NB lessons have been tried in OS, and which have not

### Takeaway
GD2 antibody in OS failed in AOST1421, which gave it as monotherapy-style maintenance after relapse with minimal residual disease. That design differs from how NB succeeded. Several rational transfers remain untested in OS: GD2 chemoimmunotherapy in front-line therapy, GD2-CART01-style constructs, DFMO maintenance, ALT-directed therapy and NB-style data commons.

### Cited Findings
- AOST1421 (Hingorani, Eur J Cancer 2022; n=39 with recurrent pulmonary OS in complete surgical remission) gave dinutuximab + GM-CSF. The 12-month disease control rate (DCR) was 28.2% (95% CI 15-44.9%), against the 16/39 threshold needed for efficacy and a historical DCR of 20%. One sudden death led the trial to change from a 2-day to a 4-day infusion schedule — [PMC9631806](https://www.omicsdi.org/dataset/biostudies-literature/S-EPMC9631806); [OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/dinutuximab-plus-gm-csf-does-not-improve-dcr-in-recurrent-osteosarcoma); [COG](https://childrensoncologygroup.org/publications/phase-2-study-of-anti-disialoganglioside-antibody-dinutuximab-in-combination-with-gm-csf-in-patients-with-recurrent-osteosarcoma-a-report-from-the-childrens-oncology-group/)
- GD2 CAR-T for OS is being tested in trials such as the NCI-listed GD2-CAR PERSIST (relapsed OS/NB), with no outcomes found. Early GD2 CAR-T in sarcoma (Majzner/Mackall, Baylor) showed limited activity, which was attributed to T-cell exhaustion and the microenvironment — [NCI listing](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/clinical-trials/search/v?id=NCI-2020-06646)
- B7-H3 CAR-T in OS: St. Jude's 3CAR4OS gives B7-H3 CAR-T after upfront MAP chemotherapy in newly diagnosed high-risk OS. A preclinical 2026 St. Jude study found that Regnase-1-knockout B7-H3 CAR-T cells improved survival in OS mouse models — [St. Jude 3CAR4OS](https://www.stjude.org/es/care-treatment/clinical-trials/3car4os-osteosarcoma.html); [St. Jude news 2026](https://www.stjude.org/media-resources/news-releases/2026-medicine-science-news/removing-immune-brake-improves-car-t-cell-therapy-for-osteosarcoma.html)
- Reviews from 2025-2026 list GD2 and B7-H3 CAR-T among T-cell approaches for OS, with early clinical results only — [Frontiers Immunol 2025](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2025.1696830/pdf); [Frontiers Immunol 2026](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2026.1769847/pdf)

### Inferences
- Why GD2 likely failed in OS: (1) In NB, dinutuximab works mainly as consolidation of minimal residual disease after myeloablation. AOST1421 used it alone after relapse and lacked the isotretinoin, IL-2 and chemotherapy synergy (irinotecan/temozolomide synergy was key in NB relapse). (2) GD2 expression in OS is more heterogeneous than in NB. Patients were not selected by GD2 expression, which is an obvious biomarker gap. (3) The OS microenvironment is dominated by macrophages and immunosuppressive bone.
- Untested or rational transfers (candidate overlooked gaps):
  - GD2-selected chemoimmunotherapy in OS, e.g., dinutuximab or naxitamab with a relapse chemotherapy backbone, mirroring ANBL1221.
  - An NMTRC-style DFMO maintenance trial in OS. ODC1/polyamines are less clearly MYC-linked in OS, though MYC gain at 8q24 is common in OS (verify ODC1 data in OS). No such trial was found.
  - ALT-directed therapy shared across both diseases (ATR inhibitors, which are synthetic-lethal with ALT, and telomere-targeting agents). This is rational given that both NB (ATRX-altered subset) and OS (frequent ALT) depend on ALT, and NB has generated the clearer ALT biology — [Flynn et al., Science 2015 (ATR inhibition kills ALT cells)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1257216)
  - A single externally controlled approval pathway for OS, following the DFMO/Iwilfin precedent, using AOST/EURAMOS historical cohorts.
  - An INRG-style pretreatment biological risk classifier and data commons for OS (see risk-stratification section).

### Gaps
- Whether GD2 expression in OS has been systematically quantified (to guide patient selection) was not verified this session.
- No head-to-head data were found on why GD2 CAR-T underperforms in OS versus NB beyond the microenvironment/exhaustion hypothesis. 
## Organizational lessons: advocacy and funding models

### Takeaway
Neuroblastoma progress was accelerated by parent-founded philanthropy that funded specific translational projects (antibodies, vaccines, CAR-T) and by a dedicated trial consortium (NMTRC/Beat Childhood Cancer) running early-phase precision-medicine trials. This filled the gap left by low federal pediatric-cancer funding. Osteosarcoma has advocacy groups but historically less concentrated, project-directed translational funding at single high-volume centers.

### Cited Findings
- Band of Parents is a neuroblastoma nonprofit formed in July 2007 and incorporated in October 2007 by roughly 100 parents of children treated at Memorial Sloan Kettering (MSKCC). It is described as a leading independent backer of neuroblastoma research at MSKCC, and reports committing more than $14 million to neuroblastoma research (an older page cites more than $10 million; totals conflict across pages, so verify against annual reports/Form 990) — [Band of Parents history](https://www.bandofparents.org/about/history-of-the-band/); [Band of Parents research overview](https://www.bandofparents.org/?p=113)
- In March 2013, Band of Parents led a group of nonprofits in giving a $2 million grant to MSKCC to develop a bispecific monoclonal antibody targeting neuroblastoma, an example of parent philanthropy directly funding a specific therapeutic — [Band of Parents](https://bandofparents.org/?p=40)
- Band of Parents launched 2026 Neuroblastoma Breakthrough Catalyst Grants for innovative research — [Band of Parents](https://www.bandofparents.org/about/history-of-the-band/)
- A Band of Parents fundraiser page cites that less than 8% of National Cancer Institute funding goes to pediatric cancer (advocacy figure, treat as such) — [Band of Parents](https://www.bandofparents.org/?p=113)

### Inferences
- The NB funding model has three transferable features: (1) parent-founded funds targeting named translational projects (antibody, vaccine) rather than general gifts; (2) concentration at a few high-volume centers (MSKCC, St. Jude, CHOP) that run their own early-phase trials; and (3) a disease-specific consortium (NMTRC/Beat Childhood Cancer, which runs the Neuroblastoma and Medulloblastoma Translational Research Consortium precision-medicine trials and funded much of the DFMO program via NMTRC003). OS advocacy (e.g., MIB Agents, the Osteosarcoma Institute, QuadW/Sarcoma Alliance) exists but has historically funded less center-concentrated translational pipelines; a direct transfer would be an OS consortium modeled on NMTRC plus a project-directed parent fund tied to one or two high-volume OS centers.
- The DFMO/Iwilfin story is itself an organizational lesson: a parent-driven consortium (NMTRC) carried an off-patent, cheap drug (eflornithine) through single-arm trials with a cooperative-group external control all the way to FDA approval, something industry would not have pursued. OS has off-patent repurposing candidates (e.g., mifamurtide outside the US, bisphosphonates, metformin/polyamine agents) that could follow the same philanthropy-to-approval route.

### Gaps
- The search returned little primary material on Beat Childhood Cancer/NMTRC and Solving Kids' Cancer as organizations; their exact funding totals, trial portfolios and measurable impact were not verified this session. The one confirmed Solving Kids' Cancer reference is a staff bio (research-program management since 2010).
- No quantified comparison of NB versus OS philanthropic funding was found.
