# Osteosarcoma: Treatment History and Four Decades of Survival Stagnation

Notes compiled 2026-11-13. Scope: pediatric/AYA high-grade osteosarcoma. Items tagged [UNVERIFIED-BACKGROUND] come from prior domain knowledge and were not confirmed against a fetched source in this session. The report writer should treat them as leads to verify, not as citable facts.

## 1. History and current survival (MIOS, T-protocols, MAP, limb salvage, survival rates)

### Takeaway
Adjuvant chemotherapy raised relapse-free survival from about 11–17% (surgery alone) to about 60–66% in the early 1980s. Survival has been essentially flat since the mid-1980s: about 70% 5-year OS for localized disease, about 20–30% for metastatic disease at diagnosis, and under 30% after relapse.

### Cited Findings
- MIOS (Link et al., NEJM 1986;314:1600–1606) randomized 36 post-surgical patients to adjuvant chemotherapy or observation. 2-year relapse-free survival was 17% in controls ("similar to that found in studies before 1970") vs 66% with chemotherapy (P<0.001). Another 77 patients who declined randomization had similar results. — [QxMD/PubMed 3520317](https://www.qxmd.com/r/3520317)
- MIOS update: projected 6-year EFS was 11% for controls vs 61% with chemotherapy. — [BJC 1986 PDF](https://www.nature.com/articles/bjc198681.pdf)
- Note the trial size: the evidence that established adjuvant chemotherapy came from a randomized cohort of only 36 patients. — same source as above
- SEER-based NCI data: 5-year survival rose from 40% to 76% in children under 15, and from 56% to about 66% in adolescents aged 15–19, between 1975 and later years. "No substantial improvement since the 1980s." — [NCBI/PDQ](https://https.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK65736.27)
- A SEER 1975–2017 analysis found OS "relatively unchanged over recent decades." Survival was worse in males, American Indian/Alaska Native patients, older patients and those with metastatic disease. — [PMC11647566](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11647566)
- SEER-based estimates of 5-year cause-specific survival: 71.8% without metastasis vs 30.4% with metastasis at diagnosis. A 2019 SEER metastatic cohort reported 24.4%, and a Canadian series reported 37.4% for metastatic presentation. — [search summary of SEER studies, PMC8534404](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8534404); [Can J Surg 2022](https://www.canjsurg.ca/content/65/4/E527.full.pdf)
- EURAMOS-1 cohort (2,260 registered patients, April 2005–June 2011; Smeland et al., EJC 2019;109:36–50): 3-year EFS 59%, 5-year EFS 54% (95% CI 52–56%), 5-year OS from biopsy 71% (68–73%). The most adverse factors at diagnosis were lung metastases, non-pulmonary metastases and an axial site. — [Lund record](https://lup.lub.lu.se/record/2f6c7362-c1f1-450a-8937-8b3ee3b5c107); [UCL Discovery](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10065960)
- "Patients who develop osteosarcoma in 2017 receive treatment that remains essentially unchanged since the 1970s"; 5-year OS has stayed about 60% across five decades. — [Frontiers in Oncology 2018 mini-review](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fonc.2018.00004/full)
- After relapse, 5-year OS is under 30% (FOSTER WP3 meta-analysis). — [UCL Discovery, Wilson et al.](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10220907/1/Wilson_Relapsed%20OS%20prognostic%20factors%20accepted.pdf)
- [UNVERIFIED-BACKGROUND] Rosen's T-protocols at Memorial Sloan Kettering (T-7, T-10, late 1970s) introduced preoperative ("neoadjuvant") chemotherapy. The original reason was practical: it bought time to manufacture custom endoprostheses. It also made histologic response grading possible. MAP (high-dose methotrexate 12 g/m², doxorubicin, cisplatin) became the de facto backbone through COG, EOI and COSS trials, and was formalized as the EURAMOS-1 control arm.
- [UNVERIFIED-BACKGROUND] Limb salvage now replaces amputation in roughly 85–90% of extremity cases at experienced centers, with no clear survival penalty provided margins are adequate. Rotationplasty remains an option for young children.

### Inferences
- The 1986 survival jump appears to be the only step-change in outcomes. Every later front-line modification has failed to move OS, so the field has sat on a plateau of about 70% (localized) for about 40 years.
- MIOS was tiny by today's standards, which shows how large the first effect was. Later trials chased much smaller increments.

## 2. EURAMOS-1, INT-0133 and mifamurtide

### Takeaway
EURAMOS-1 was the largest osteosarcoma trial ever run. Both of its randomized questions were negative: intensifying therapy for poor responders (MAPIE) did not help, and adding interferon for good responders did not help. Response-adapted therapy built on the existing cytotoxic toolkit did not improve outcomes. Mifamurtide (INT-0133) is still disputed because of factorial-design interaction and post hoc analysis issues. It is approved in Europe but not in the US.

### Cited Findings
- Poor responders (≥10% viable tumor), n=618: MAPIE (n=308) vs MAP (n=310). EFS HR was 0.98 (95% CI 0.78–1.23), with 307 events (153 MAP, 154 MAPIE) and non-proportional hazards. Grade 4 non-hematologic toxicity was 24% with MAPIE vs 12% with MAP. Authors' conclusion: the results "do not support the addition of ifosfamide and etoposide." (Marina et al., Lancet Oncol 2016;17:1396–1408) — [ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/News/43899); [EU CTR results](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/trial/2004-000242-20/results); [PMC6822952](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6822952)
- Good responders: 1,041 of 2,260 had a good response, but only 716 consented to randomization (MAP 359; MAP + pegylated IFN-α-2b 357). Adjusted EFS HR was 0.83 (0.61–1.12; P=0.214), with 3-year EFS 76% overall and 174 events. Of the 357 allocated to IFN, 271 started it and 105 stopped early. Median IFN duration, when started, was 67 weeks. (Bielack et al., JCO 2015, doi:10.1200/JCO.2014.60.0734) — [PubMed 26033801](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26033801/)
- INT-0133 (CCG/POG, 678 patients, mostly US) used a 2×2 factorial design: ± ifosfamide and ± mifamurtide (L-MTP-PE). Discontinuation was higher in the mifamurtide arms (22%, 30%) than in the non-mifamurtide arms (13%, 17%). After a median follow-up of 7.9 years, the manufacturer reported an OS HR of 0.63 with mifamurtide. NICE accepted the combined analysis over post hoc subgroup comparisons. — [NICE TA235 manufacturer's submission](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ta235/chapter/the-manufacturers-submission); [NICE TA235 evidence considerations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ta235/chapter/4-Consideration-of-the-evidence)
- In 2007 the FDA ODAC voted 12–2 that INT-0133 did not demonstrate efficacy. The 6-year relapse-free survival figures (66% with mifamurtide vs 57% without) were not supported by the FDA's own analysis. — [Healio, June 2007](https://cm.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20120325/fda-panel-rejects-mifamurtide-for-osteosarcoma-in-children)
- [UNVERIFIED-BACKGROUND] The core interpretive problems with INT-0133 were these. A possible interaction between the two factors appeared: mifamurtide looked better with ifosfamide, and the first report claimed an interaction, which invalidates the usual factorial analysis. The primary EFS benefit was not statistically significant (p≈0.08). The OS benefit emerged only with later follow-up. The trial was open-label with no placebo. Methotrexate dosing also differed from European MAP. EMA approved Mepact in 2009. The FDA never approved it. It has never been tested in a confirmatory randomized trial.

### Inferences
- **Flag, possibly abandoned too early: immunomodulation in front-line therapy.** Both INT-0133 (OS HR about 0.63–0.72) and EURAMOS-1 IFN (EFS HR 0.83, CI crossing 1) point in a favorable direction. Neither was conclusive. The IFN arm was weakened by poor uptake and adherence, with only 271/357 starting and 105 of those stopping early. That is close to an underpowered per-protocol test. No confirmatory mifamurtide RCT has ever been run, which leaves a roughly 15-year-old open question.
- **MAPIE failure:** adding more of the same class of cytotoxics to biologically chemo-resistant tumors (poor responders) raised toxicity without benefit. The implication is that poor response is an intrinsic biological phenotype that dose intensification cannot overcome, not simple under-treatment.
- About 31% of eligible good responders and many poor responders were never randomized. That reduced power and may have biased the randomized populations.

- I did not fetch the latest long-term EURAMOS-1 follow-up for the MAPIE and IFN comparisons. The report writer should check for an updated publication (about 2019–2024).
- I did not confirm the current US regulatory status of mifamurtide (as of 2026, believed still unapproved; verify). I also did not confirm the exact INT-0133 interaction p-values (Meyers et al., JCO 2005 and 2008).

## 3. Histologic necrosis (Huvos) as a prognostic marker but failed basis for adaptation

### Takeaway
Necrosis of 90% or more after preoperative chemotherapy remains one of the strongest prognostic factors. Every attempt to change postoperative chemotherapy according to necrosis has failed, both MSKCC T-10 salvage and EURAMOS-1 MAPIE. The marker is prognostic but not predictive.

### Cited Findings
- EURAMOS-1 used <10% viable tumor (≥90% necrosis) to define good response. Changing therapy for poor responders did not improve EFS (HR 0.98). — [ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/News/43899)
- 3-year EFS in good responders was 76%. — [PubMed 26033801](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26033801/)
- [UNVERIFIED-BACKGROUND] Rosen's T-10 originally reported benefit from switching poor responders to cisplatin and doxorubicin. Later MSKCC follow-up and other groups (e.g. COSS-82) did not reproduce that benefit.

### Inferences
- Possible reasons: (a) necrosis is measured once, late, on resected tissue, and reflects biology rather than drug sensitivity; (b) the salvage agents (ifosfamide/etoposide) are not non-cross-resistant enough; (c) micrometastatic clones may differ from the primary tumor.
- **Unasked question:** no one has run a trial adapting therapy on an early, dynamic biomarker such as ctDNA kinetics, FDG-PET SUV change or DCE-MRI during induction. Adaptation on necrosis also arrives after weeks 10–12, possibly too late. This is a plausible gap.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve primary data on necrosis-grading reproducibility or inter-observer variability.

## 4. Relapsed and metastatic disease: surgery, outcomes, systemic options

### Takeaway
Complete surgical remission is the main determinant of survival after relapse. Second-line chemotherapy shows little or no added benefit in retrospective series. TKIs such as regorafenib give a modest progression-free survival (PFS) benefit but no proven survival gain.

### Cited Findings
- COSS (Kempf-Bielack et al., JCO 2005), 576 relapsed patients: post-relapse OS was 38% at 2 years, 23% at 5 years and 18% at 10 years. 5-year OS was 39% with a second surgical complete remission (n=339) vs 0% without (n=229). Chemotherapy "may contribute to limited improvements." — [PubMed 12586810 / COSS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12586810/); [UKE record](https://fis.uke.de/portal/en/publications/osteosarcoma-relapse-after-combined-modality-therapy-an-analysis-of-unselected-patients-in-the-cooperative-osteosarcoma-study-group-coss(091a5e71-01f4-4eaf-a4b8-b355f2b3214a)/export.html)
- COSS solitary lung relapse (Mettmann et al., Cancer Med 2023), n=219: 94.9% achieved a second complete remission. Post-relapse OS was 72.0% at 2 years and 51.2% at 5 years. Relapse chemotherapy "does not seem to improve survival." — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/7c87cf91104a4367a88394805f691d43)
- Italian extremity series (n=162): 5-year post-relapse survival was 39% with complete surgery vs 0% at 3 years without. Second-line chemotherapy did not influence outcome. — [Univ. Torino PDF](https://iris.unito.it/bitstream/2318/1690902/2/Postrelapse_Survival_in_Osteosarcoma_of_the_Extrem%20%281%29.pdf)
- SARC024 regorafenib (randomized, double-blind phase 2 with crossover): median PFS 3.6 vs 1.7 months (HR 0.42, P=0.017). No OS difference, attributed to crossover. Most patients had grade 3–4 events. (Davis et al., JCO 2019;37:1424) — [PubMed 31013172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31013172/)
- [UNVERIFIED-BACKGROUND] REGOBONE (French, Duffaud et al., Lancet Oncol 2019) also favored regorafenib. Cabozantinib (CABONE), sorafenib ± everolimus (Italian Sarcoma Group) and lenvatinib + ifosfamide/etoposide (the OLIE randomized trial) all report modest PFS benefits.

### Inferences
- **Never asked in a randomized way:** does systemic therapy after complete metastasectomy add anything? The only evidence is retrospective, and it says no. A randomized adjuvant trial in the post-metastasectomy setting is both feasible and badly needed. The 2023 COSS data also show a 51% 5-year OS in solitary lung relapse, which gives a workable resected-disease population.
- Selection bias in the surgical series is large: resectable patients have less disease. "Surgery works" is partly confounded.

### Gaps
- I did not verify outcomes for CABONE, OLIE or other recent TKI trials, or results of current resected-relapse trials (e.g. COG AOST2032 cabozantinib front-line, or the denosumab trial AOST1321).

## 5. Trial-design problems and proposed better designs

### Takeaway
Osteosarcoma drug development has relied on small single-arm phase 2 trials with RECIST response endpoints, which are poorly suited to a calcified tumor. COG moved to a 4-month EFS benchmark (12% historical), but that benchmark has held at 7–12% across 10 consecutive trials, so almost nothing has advanced. Large randomized phase 3 trials (EURAMOS-1) tested modest increments of old drugs.

### Cited Findings
- Lagmay et al., JCO 2016;34(25):3031–3038 pooled 96 patients with measurable relapsed disease from 7 COG/predecessor phase 2 trials (1997–2007). 4-month EFS was 12% (95% CI 6–19%). Radiographic response "may limit optimal detection of treatment response because of the calcified tumor matrix." — [PMC5012712](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5012712); [Healio 2016](https://cm.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20160715/alternative-endpoints-needed-for-osteosarcoma-trials)
- Proposed two-stage design: stop after 19 patients if 3 or fewer reach 4-month EFS; at 36 patients, 10 or fewer means ineffective. — [PMC5012712 Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5012712/table/T3)
- COG follow-up of AOST1321, AOST1322 and AOST1521 (57 evaluable patients): 4-month EFS was 7% (95% CI 2–16%), confirming that the benchmark still holds and that those agents did not work. — [ASCO abstract](https://ace.asco.org/content/344819/abstract/197938)
- A FOSTER-linked review identified 98 phase 2 trials in refractory/recurrent osteosarcoma from 2003–2016 and examined how design could improve. — [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10178055/1/1-s2.0-S0305737223001184-main.pdf)
- "Repeated attempts by large international cooperative groups to improve outcomes through randomized clinical trials have not led to survival improvement in osteosarcoma." — [FOSTER/Euro Ewing statement, PMC7829872](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7829872)
- Most patients have only minimal tissue sampled at diagnosis, enough for diagnosis but not for molecular analysis. This is a structural barrier to biomarker-driven trials. — [PMC7829872](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7829872)

### Inferences
- **Weakness of the historical-control design:** the 12% benchmark comes from measurable, unresectable patients with heterogeneous prior therapy. Cytostatic agents, immunotherapies and agents that act on micrometastases may never cross it, even if they would help in minimal residual disease. Promising mechanisms may therefore be screened out (false negatives), for example the denosumab and glembatumumab trials (AOST1321, AOST1322).
- Proposed alternatives: (1) resected-disease designs, where patients are rendered disease-free and EFS is measured, which mirrors where cures actually happen; (2) randomized phase 2 with PFS, as in SARC024, which succeeded where single-arm trials gave ambiguous answers; (3) Bayesian or multi-arm platform trials (MAMS designs, as in FOSTER and UK proposals); (4) ctDNA or MRD endpoints.
- Splitting AYA patients between pediatric and adult systems also fragments an already rare disease. Adult sarcoma trials often exclude patients under 18, and pediatric trials often cap age at 30–40.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch specific platform-trial protocols (e.g. a FOSTER MAMS design) or the current ClinicalTrials.gov front-line status. COG AOST2031/AOST2032 (MAP ± cabozantinib) need checking.

## 6. Toxicity and survivorship burden of MAP; sodium thiosulfate

### Takeaway
MAP carries substantial lifelong toxicity: doxorubicin cardiotoxicity, cisplatin ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity, infertility and second cancers. Sodium thiosulfate (Pedmark) was FDA-approved in 2022 for localized solid tumors and reduces hearing loss. Its effect on survival is not established in osteosarcoma, where cisplatin is a core curative drug.

### Cited Findings
- FDA approved Pedmark on 2022-09-20 for patients aged 1 month and older with localized, non-metastatic solid tumors receiving cisplatin. — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-sodium-thiosulfate-reduce-risk-ototoxicity-associated-cisplatin-pediatric-patients)
- COG ACCL0431 included osteosarcoma, which made up 26% of the localized subset. The efficacy-relevant localized subset was small (77 patients). There were "questions about its effects on survival." STS inactivates cisplatin, which is why the label requires dosing 6 hours after each cisplatin infusion. Safety and efficacy are not established after infusions longer than 6 hours. — [OncLive](https://onclive.com/view/pedmark-reduces-ototoxicity-risk-in-cisplatin-treated-pediatric-solid-tumors); [FDA multidisciplinary review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2023/212937Orig1s000MultidisciplineR.pdf)
- SIOPEL-6 (hepatoblastoma): 3-year OS 98% with STS vs 92% without; EFS 82% vs 79%. — [ASCO Post 2022](https://ascopost.com/news/november-2022/sodium-thiosulfate-to-reduce-the-risk-of-cisplatin-related-ototoxicity-in-pediatric-patients-with-localized-solid-tumors)
- Scotland's SMC assessed STS in 2025. — [SMC](https://scottishmedicines.org.uk/media/9137/sodium-thiosulfate-pedmarqsi-final-april-2025-for-website.pdf)
- MAPIE raised grade 4 non-hematologic toxicity from 12% to 24%. — [ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/News/43899)

### Inferences
- In osteosarcoma, cisplatin is often given as multi-day or long infusions, and AYA patients over 18 fall outside the age framing used in the pediatric studies. Uptake of STS in osteosarcoma is therefore probably limited and controversial. No osteosarcoma-specific randomized trial exists.
- **Rarely asked:** can any MAP component be de-escalated in good responders? Cisplatin and doxorubicin cause most of the late effects, and nobody has randomized their omission.

- I did not retrieve quantitative survivorship data for osteosarcoma (cardiomyopathy incidence, hearing-loss rates, second-malignancy rates, CCSS or St. Jude SJLIFE). I also did not retrieve limb-function outcomes (MSTS scores) or quantitative endoprosthesis failure rates.

## 7. Disparities (AYA, socioeconomic, LMIC)

### Takeaway
Older age, male sex, American Indian/Alaska Native background and metastatic disease predict worse SEER survival. AYA patients do worse than children. LMIC data were not retrieved in this session.

### Cited Findings
- SEER 1975–2017: worse survival in males, AI/AN patients and older patients. — [PMC11647566](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11647566)
- Gains in adolescents aged 15–19 (56% to about 66%) were smaller than in children under 15 (40% to 76%). — [NCBI/PDQ](https://https.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK65736.27)
- Korean registry: AYA (15–39) 5-year OS was 68%, vs 47% in adults. — [PMC9377546](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9377546)

### Inferences
- The AYA gap probably combines biology, lower trial enrollment, treatment outside pediatric centers, and adult-oncology dose modifications of high-dose methotrexate. The relative weight of each is unresolved.

### Gaps
- No sourced data were obtained on insurance or socioeconomic status, rural vs urban outcomes, or LMIC survival (reports from parts of Africa and South Asia often cite 5-year OS of 20–40% with high abandonment rates; [UNVERIFIED-BACKGROUND]).

## 8. Expert views on reasons for stagnation and top priorities

### Takeaway
Cooperative-group experts (FOSTER, EURAMOS, COG) agree on several points: incremental cytotoxic RCTs have failed, the biology is genomically chaotic with few actionable drivers, tissue and biobanking are inadequate, and phase 2 endpoints and designs are poor. They prioritize international collaboration, biology-driven trials and new designs.

### Cited Findings
- FOSTER was formed by two international panels of scientists, clinicians and patient/parent advocates to promote European collaboration. It states that RCTs by large cooperative groups have failed to improve survival. — [PMC7829872](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7829872)
- European guidance with FOSTER experts concludes that primary metastatic and recurrent disease still have a poor prognosis, and calls for intensified collaborative research. — [PMC11334773](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11334773)
- FOSTER WP4 systematic review on prognostic factors at diagnosis (Cancer Medicine 2025). — [record](https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-5629a5d7a9414cc38ed5cbb9162fec84)
- FOSTER WP3 relapsed prognostic-factor meta-analysis. — [UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10220907/1/Wilson_Relapsed%20OS%20prognostic%20factors%20accepted.pdf)
- [UNVERIFIED-BACKGROUND] Other relevant voices: the "Osteosarcoma: a call to arms"-style commentaries; the COG Bone Tumor Committee's 2020s strategy (Janeway/Gorlick/Reed et al.); ICONIC (UK, ctDNA/biology cohort); the Osteosarcoma Institute; and MIB Agents. None were fetched.

### Inferences
- Synthesis of overlooked or prematurely abandoned questions:
  1. Front-line immunomodulation (mifamurtide/IFN) was never given a clean confirmatory trial.
  2. No randomized adjuvant trial exists after metastasectomy.
  3. No trial has adapted therapy on early dynamic biomarkers (ctDNA, PET) instead of late necrosis.
  4. The 4-month EFS screen may discard agents that work against minimal residual disease.
  5. MAP de-escalation in good responders has never been randomized.
  6. STS's survival safety in osteosarcoma is unproven.
  7. AYA-specific dosing and delivery questions remain open.
  8. Tissue collection has been too thin for biomarker science.
