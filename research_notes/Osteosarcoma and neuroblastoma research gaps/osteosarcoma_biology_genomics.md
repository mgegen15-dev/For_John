# Osteosarcoma (pediatric/AYA): biology, genomics, and disease models

Notes compiled 2026-11-13. Sources below are abstracts, publisher pages, institutional press releases and PMC records retrieved in this session; where a number could not be confirmed against a retrieved source it is listed under Gaps rather than asserted.

## Cell of origin, and why incidence peaks at the adolescent growth spurt

### Takeaway
Mouse genetics place the initiating cell somewhere along the mesenchymal-to-osteoblast axis, with committed osteoblast-lineage cells the most consistent producers of osteosarcoma (OS) and uncommitted MSCs producing a broader tumor spectrum; human transformation experiments favor an intermediate, partly osteoblast-committed cell. Growth-related risk is supported genetically (adult and childhood height polygenic scores) and epidemiologically (high birth weight), but direct GH/IGF-1 causal evidence in humans is thin.

### Cited findings
- Crossing conditional *Rb* and *p53* mice with Prx1-Cre (uncommitted mesenchyme) showed Rb acts in a dose-dependent manner to modulate the tumour spectrum arising from p53-deficient uncommitted MSCs: OS dominated when Rb was intact, Rb loss favored hibernoma — [PMC4422092](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4422092)
- In the bone-committed Osx-Cre model, Osx-Cre;p53fl/fl mice develop osteosarcoma with ~100% penetrance; adding Rb loss yields a mixture of OS, hibernomas and other sarcomas — [PMC4422092](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4422092)
- Loss of Rb and p53 in Sca-1+ mesenchymal stem/progenitor cells is sufficient to generate transformed cells that initiate OS in vivo — [PMC4422092](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4422092)
- A head-to-head comparison of osteoblast-lineage Cre drivers at different differentiation stages raised the concern that an earlier, widely used OS model may have derived from leaky or pre-osteoblast Cre expression causing Trp53 disruption — i.e. published "cell of origin" claims are partly an artifact of Cre specificity — [PMC searchresult, promoter comparison study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmid/25347737)
- Stepwise transformation of human osteoblasts/MSCs indicates OS derives from a cell intermediate in differentiation between an MSC and a pre-osteoblast, with partial osteoblastic commitment — [PMC5396624](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5396624/)
- Polygenic score analysis in 864 cases and 1,879 European-ancestry controls: genetic propensity to greater adult height associated with 1.10-fold increased OS risk per SD; childhood height score also associated and independent of the adult score in a joint model; birth-length score not associated (P = 0.11) — Zhang et al., *Cancer* 2018 — [PubMed 30311632](https://www.pubmed.gov/30311632/); [PMC6214707](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6214707)
- Pooled individual-patient analysis: high birth weight (>=4046 g) associated with increased OS risk, OR 1.35 (95% CI 1.01-1.79) vs average birth weight — Mirabello et al. 2011 — [PubMed 21465145](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21465145/); [BMC Cancer 11:209](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2407-11-209)
- The mechanistic rationale invoked in this literature is that peak endogenous sex hormone, growth hormone and IGF-I levels occur during puberty, coinciding with peak bone growth rates — [PMC3494416](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3494416)
- A further genetic-risk-score study of birthweight, height and puberty timing in OS exists (Gianferante et al., *Cancer Epidemiology* 2024) — [UMN record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/genetically-inferred-birthweight-height-and-puberty-timing-and-ri/); results text not retrieved
- Caution for model-based claims: a JCI Insight paper on Lkb1 deletion in Ctsk-Cre periosteal cells, which discussed osteogenic tumor origin, is marked RETRACTED (Feb 2026) — [jci.org/articles/view/124590](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/124590/version/3/pdf/render.pdf)

### Inferences
- The "MSC vs osteoblast precursor" debate is partly a false dichotomy created by Cre-driver leakiness; the testable question is which differentiation state is permissive for the specific chromosomal catastrophe (see chromothripsis section), not merely which cell can be transformed.
- No retrieved study links a growth-plate-specific cellular state (e.g. proliferating chondrocyte-adjacent periosteal/metaphyseal progenitors during the pubertal growth spurt) to the genomic mechanism of OS initiation. That junction — pubertal proliferative burst as the permissive window for loss-translocation-amplification chromothripsis — appears to be an open, under-tested hypothesis.

### Gaps
- No human Mendelian randomization result for circulating IGF-1 and OS risk was found; the only IGF-1 statements retrieved are background/temporal-coincidence arguments.
- No retrieved source quantifies OS incidence against growth-velocity curves (e.g. age at peak height velocity in cases vs population), nor tests whether GH-treated children have excess OS risk.
- The identity of the growth-plate/periosteal progenitor population at risk, and whether dogs (which also develop OS in metaphyses of long bones) share that population, was not resolvable from retrieved sources.

## The osteosarcoma genome: structural catastrophe, TP53 disruption, BRCAness

### Takeaway
OS has few recurrent point-mutation drivers; near-universal TP53 inactivation is often achieved by structural variation (notably intron-1 rearrangements), and the genome is dominated by chromothripsis, kataegis, and copy-number chaos with HR-deficiency-like ("BRCAness") mutation signatures. A 2025 *Cell* study defines a specific mechanism, loss-translocation-amplification (LTA) chromothripsis, in ~50% of high-grade cases.

### Cited findings
- Chen et al. 2014 (St. Jude/WashU PCGP, *Cell Reports*): WGS of 34 tumors from 32 pediatric OS patients; 55% of TP53 mutations were caused by structural variations; localized hypermutation (kataegis) in 50% of tumors — [Cell Reports record](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/recurrent-somatic-structural-variations-contribute-to-tumorigenes/); [PMC4096827](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/4096827)
- Ribi et al. 2015 (*Oncotarget*): screening 288 OS and 1,090 tumors of other types found TP53 intron-1 rearrangements in 46/288 (16%) of OS and none in other tumor types; a germline 445 kb inversion from TP53 intron 1 toward the centromere segregated with tumors in a Li-Fraumeni family; one tumor had a balanced translocation between TP53 intron 1 and chromosome 1; authors proposed open chromatin/active transcription of TP53 as the mechanistic predisposition — [PMC4480712](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4480712); [Oncotarget](https://oncotarget.com/article/3115/)
- Perry et al. 2014 (*PNAS*): 59 tumor/normal pairs by WES, WGS and RNA-seq; only TP53 was mutated at significant recurrent frequency; convergence of pathway analysis, comparative (canine) oncology and an shRNA screen on PI3K/mTOR as a central vulnerability; accompanying PNAS commentary states 24% of patients harbored PI3K/mTOR pathway alterations — [PMC4280583](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4280583); [Reed & Shokat commentary PDF](https://shokatlab.ucsf.edu/pdfs/25512494.pdf)
- Kovac et al. 2015 (*Nat Commun*): "Exome sequencing of osteosarcoma reveals mutation signatures reminiscent of BRCA deficiency"; multiple oncogenic pathways drive chromosomal instability producing acquired BRCA-like traits proposed as therapeutically exploitable; a later review reports COSMIC signatures 3 and 5 in that WES data — [UCL Discovery / ncomms15936-adjacent record](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/154710/1/154710.pdf); [review PMC8361660](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8361660)
- Behjati et al. 2017 (*Nat Commun*, "Recurrent mutation of IGF signalling genes and distinct patterns of genomic rearrangement in osteosarcoma"): IGF signalling gene mutations in 8/112 cases (7%); FISH in a further 87 OS found IGF1R amplification in 14%; describes a rearrangement process termed chromothripsis-amplification generating drivers genome-wide — [UCL Discovery 1562188](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1562188/); [UCL news](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/cancer/news/existing-drugs-could-benefit-patients-bone-cancer-genetic-study-suggests)
- Espejo Valle-Inclan, Cortés-Ciriano et al. 2025 (*Cell*, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2024.12.005): multi-region WGS of the largest OS WGS collection; chromothripsis is an ongoing mutational process occurring subclonally in 74% of osteosarcomas; LTA chromothripsis — initiated by a double-strand break causing TP53 loss, then translocation and amplification — mediates punctuated evolution in ~50% of pediatric and adult high-grade OS; genome-wide LOH burden is prognostic, with high LOH predicting lower survival — [EMBL news](https://www.embl.org/news/science-technology/researchers-uncover-what-drives-aggressive-bone-cancer); [Máxima Centrum record](https://pure.prinsesmaximacentrum.nl/en/publications/ongoing-chromothripsis-underpins-osteosarcoma-genome-complexity-a/); [preprint full text](https://storage.prod.researchhub.com/uploads/papers/2025/11/21/2023.12.29.573403v1.full.pdf)
- Cross-species aCGH found copy-number deletions in the tumor suppressor DLG2 in 42% of human and 55.6% of canine OS; shared CNAs included MYC, CDKN2A/B, RB1 and PTEN — [PMC6642146](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6642146)
- ecDNA: the 2025 *Cell* chromothripsis paper is indexed with extrachromosomal DNA keywords and reports ecDNA and segmental amplifications co-localizing with chromothripsis-linked events — [preprint](https://storage.prod.researchhub.com/uploads/papers/2025/11/21/2023.12.29.573403v1.full.pdf). An AACR 2024 abstract (Clugston, Leung, Lopez Fuentes) combined optical genome mapping with WGS across 11 OS cell lines and reported dozens of candidate ecDNA amplicons, one potentially explaining exceptionally high expression of its cargo gene — [abstract index](https://synapsesocial.com/papers/W4393085976). A pan-cancer review cites Bailey et al.: 17.1% of 14,778 tumors across 39 types contain ecDNA, with figures including primary conventional OS — [Front Oncol 2024 review](https://frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2024.1382276/epub)

### Inferences
- The LTA model reframes TP53 loss not as one of many drivers but as the initiating event of a single catastrophic mechanism, which would predict that the TP53 intron-1 hotspot (16% of OS, zero in other tumor types) is where that double-strand break often lands. No retrieved source explicitly joins Ribi 2015 to the LTA mechanism — this is an explicit, testable and apparently untested link.
- "BRCAness" has been claimed since 2015 but retrieved sources show no completed OS-specific trial reading out on an HRD biomarker; the signature-3 claim rests on exome data, which is weak for HRD inference relative to WGS-based HRDetect-type classifiers. Re-deriving HRD calls on the 2025 WGS cohort is an obvious, unexploited analysis.
- LOH burden as a prognostic marker (2025) has not, in retrieved sources, been translated into any risk-stratified trial design.

### Gaps
- Frequencies for RB1, CDKN2A, MYC, CCNE1, VEGFA/CDK4/MDM2 amplification and ATRX in large OS cohorts were not confirmed by any source retrieved in this session; TARGET-OS publication numbers were not retrieved. These need direct verification (Chen 2014; Sayles et al. 2019 Cancer Discovery; TARGET-OS) before use.
- Cohort size and composition of the 2025 *Cell* WGS collection not confirmed from the retrieved pages.
- No retrieved source quantifies ecDNA prevalence specifically in pediatric OS clinical samples; current evidence is cell lines, abstracts, and bulk-RNA-derived "ecDNA-related gene" prognostic models (e.g. Feng et al.) that do not measure ecDNA at all — a notable weakness in that literature.

## Intratumoral heterogeneity, clonal evolution to lung metastasis, timing of chromothripsis

### Takeaway
There is a genuine, unresolved contradiction: a longitudinal multi-sample WGS study times complex rearrangements to before diagnosis (macroevolutionary "big bang"), whereas the 2025 multi-region *Cell* study finds chromothripsis ongoing and subclonal in 74% of tumors. Metastatic seeding appears often monoclonal, and some apparent heterogeneity may be treatment-induced.

### Cited findings
- Longitudinal WGS of 8 patients / 37 samples (2023): chromosomal duplication timing analysis indicated complex genomic rearrangements typically occurred prior to diagnosis, supporting a macroevolutionary model — [PMC10646480](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10646480/)
- Contradicting that: chromothripsis is an ongoing mutational process, subclonal in 74% of OS (multi-region WGS, *Cell* 2025) — [Máxima Centrum record](https://pure.prinsesmaximacentrum.nl/en/publications/ongoing-chromothripsis-underpins-osteosarcoma-genome-complexity-a/); [ScienceDaily summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/01/250114124904.htm)
- Brady et al. (Princeton), "The clonal evolution of metastatic osteosarcoma as shaped by cisplatin treatment": sequencing of 14 metastases from 4 patients detected single-cell seeding of metastasis in most metastatic samples, and attributes much heterogeneity to cisplatin-induced mutagenesis — [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/the-clonal-evolution-of-metastatic-osteosarcoma-as-shaped-by-cisp/)
- WES-based evolutionary analysis of 61 TARGET OS cases (*J Transl Med*, July 2025): linear evolutionary trajectory in 62% of patients; ATRX mutations proposed as early events contributing to metastasis — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/0b28d55dda1247a9ae2b4172b6381c98)

### Inferences
- The timing contradiction may be reconcilable: an early LTA catastrophe fixing TP53 loss plus genome doubling, followed by continued low-grade chromothriptic activity — but the two studies differ in sampling design (longitudinal single-region vs multi-region) and in method (duplication timing vs subclonality), which is itself the under-examined methodological issue.
- If cisplatin shapes metastatic clonal structure, then every evolutionary study using post-chemotherapy resection specimens (the norm, since OS is resected after neoadjuvant chemo) is confounded. Few studies retrieved here use truly treatment-naive biopsies paired with metastases.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source gives primary-to-lung-metastasis divergence times, or whether metastatic clones pre-exist at diagnosis in detectable form (e.g. by ctDNA). ctDNA/MRD literature in OS was not searched in this session.
- Single-cell DNA sequencing (vs single-cell RNA) of OS appears sparse; no source retrieved.

## Tumor microenvironment

### Takeaway
Single-cell work consistently describes a macrophage-dominated, T-cell-poor ("cold") microenvironment with osteoclast, CAF and perivascular stem-like niches; the field's main limitation is tiny sample numbers, and chemotherapy appears to make the TME more immunosuppressive.

### Cited findings
- The widely used reference atlas is the 2020 *Nature Communications* single-cell atlas of advanced OS: 100,987 cells from 11 patients (Zhou et al.) — [PMC7730477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7730477)
- Spatial + single-cell OS atlas (*J Hematol Oncol*, 2024): 49,356 single cells from OS tissues, integrated analysis extending to 194,083 cells; explicitly notes that rarity had limited earlier work to only 17 samples across existing datasets; defines compositional subgroups, spatial niches, and candidate chemoresistance mechanisms; data under GSE162454 and GSE159048; publisher page carries a change-history notice dated December 2025 — [J Hematol Oncol 10.1186/s13045-024-01598-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13045-024-01598-7); [PMC11337771](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11337771)
- Chemotherapy-induced TME remodeling (*J Cancer Res Clin Oncol*, 2024): comparing treatment-naive vs post-neoadjuvant tissue, chemotherapy increased mesenchymal and endothelial infiltration while producing an immunosuppressive microenvironment — [PDF](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00432-024-05787-2.pdf)
- Spatial immune-evasion niches (Front Oncol mini-review, July 2025): describes a chemoresistance-linked perivascular niche containing SOX9+ stem-like tumor cells and CXCL12-expressing CAF subsets — [Front Oncol 2025.1640645](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2025.1640645/epub)
- Immune-focused reviews/analyses draw on scRNA-seq from primary, locally recurrent and metastatic disease — [Front Immunol 2024.1394284](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2024.1394284/epub); M2-macrophage marker prognostic scRNA work — [J Cancer 16:1873](https://www.jcancer.org/v16p1873.pdf); multi-omic mitophagy/metabolism analysis reusing GSE162454 and GSE237070 with CellChat — [Front Immunol 2025.1680554](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2025.1680554/epub)

### Inferences
- A large share of 2024-2025 "OS single-cell" papers are reanalyses of the same two or three GEO datasets (GSE162454 recurs repeatedly). Apparent reproducibility across papers is therefore largely dataset re-use, not independent replication — an important caveat for anyone mining this literature for targets.
- Osteoclast biology and bone remodeling as a metastasis-permissive axis (e.g. RANKL/denosumab, bisphosphonates) was not surfaced by these searches despite being mechanistically obvious; likely under-studied in the single-cell era.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source quantifies T-cell fractions, TMB/neoantigen load, or MHC-I loss frequency in pediatric OS; the "cold tumor" claim is qualitative in everything retrieved here.
- Lung-metastasis-specific TME (why lung) not addressed by any retrieved source.

## Epigenetics, molecular subtyping, germline predisposition

### Takeaway
Multiple competing subtype schemes exist (2, 3, and 4 subtypes) built largely on the same ~86-98-sample TARGET dataset with no convergence or clinical adoption — molecular subtyping remains an unmet need. Germline predisposition is far more common than classically assumed: 28% of unselected OS patients of European ancestry carry a pathogenic variant in a cancer-susceptibility gene.

### Cited findings
- Multi-omic subtyping of 86 OS tumors (CNA + expression + methylation) identified three molecularly distinct subtypes, validated in a separate cohort (*Cancers*, Oct 2023) — [PubMed 37894336](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37894336/); [MDPI 15/20/4970](https://mdpi.com/2072-6694/15/20/4970)
- A Cluster-of-Clusters Analysis of TARGET mRNA, lncRNA, miRNA, methylation and mutation data from the same group reports two subtypes; Southekal et al. (*Cancers* 2023) likewise report two subtypes with metastasis-linked differential expression — [MDPI 15/7/2134](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6694/15/7/2134)
- Zheng et al. (*Front Oncol*, Feb 2023): NMF on expression data from 98 patients yielded four subtypes (S-I to S-IV); S-II had highest immune infiltration; SQLE proposed as a target; also cites prior methylation-based division into three subgroups — [Front Oncol 2023.1111570](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2023.1111570/full)
- Mirabello et al. 2020 (*JAMA Oncol* 6:724-734, DOI 10.1001/jamaoncol.2020.0197): exome sequencing of 1,244 OS patients from 10 international centers; among 1,004 patients of European ancestry, 28.0% carried a pathogenic variant in one of 238 cancer-susceptibility genes vs 12.1% of healthy controls and 9.3% in ExAC; autosomal-dominant gene carriers 18.4%, recessive 9.2%; most common dominant genes TP53 (4.4%) and CDKN2A (1.2%); most common recessive gene RECQL4 (1.0%); higher-than-expected pathogenic/likely pathogenic variant frequency in genes not previously linked to OS, including CDKN2A, MEN1, VHL, POT1, APC, MSH2 and ATRX; authors argue germline testing may be warranted for all OS patients — [DCEG summary](https://dceg.cancer.gov/news-events/news/2020/osteosarcoma-susceptibility); [ESMO news](https://oncologypro.esmo.org/oncology-news/daily-news/oncologynews-archives-2020/rare-pathogenic-germline-mutations-highlighted-among-osteosarcoma-patients); [UCL Discovery 10096599](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10096599)
- Germline TP53 intron-1 structural variation can itself cause Li-Fraumeni syndrome (445 kb inversion segregating in a family), i.e. standard TP53 exon sequencing/panels can miss causative germline events — [PMC4480712](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4480712)

### Inferences
- The 28% germline figure combined with the finding that germline TP53 intron-1 inversions are invisible to exon-focused assays implies true predisposition prevalence is higher than 28% and that structural-variant-aware germline analysis of OS cohorts is an under-exploited study.
- Subtype schemes disagreeing (2 vs 3 vs 4) while drawing on overlapping TARGET samples suggests the problem is not analytic method but that OS's dominant axis of variation may be structural/copy-number catastrophe burden (e.g. LOH burden, LTA-positive vs -negative) rather than expression programs. Classifying by LTA status and LOH burden — available from the 2025 WGS data — is an obvious untried subtyping axis.

### Gaps
- Germline variant prevalence in non-European-ancestry OS patients: Mirabello's case-control analysis was restricted to European ancestry; no retrieved source reports comparable rates in other populations.
- No retrieved source on frequencies of Rothmund-Thomson (RECQL4), Werner, Bloom or Diamond-Blackfan syndromes as OS predisposition beyond the RECQL4 1.0% figure, and none on hereditary retinoblastoma survivors' OS incidence.
- No retrieved source gives a DNA-methylation classifier for OS of the Capper/sarcoma-classifier type, nor evidence that OS is separable into methylation classes with clinical validity.

## Disease models

### Takeaway
Spontaneous canine OS is the best-validated non-murine model, with demonstrated genomic concordance (low SNV burden, structural complexity, shared CNAs including DLG2, MYC, CDKN2A/B, RB1, PTEN) and a short, informative natural history; GEMMs reliably make tumors but their cell-of-origin claims are confounded by Cre leakiness, and retrieved sources did not substantiate zebrafish or organoid platforms.

### Cited findings
- Gardner et al. 2019 (*Communications Biology*, DOI 10.1038/s42003-019-0487-2): 59 dogs with primary and metastatic appendicular OS; WGS and WES of matched tumor/normal from 24 and 13 dogs respectively; RNA-seq from 54 primary tumors; recurrent mutations in SETD2 and DMD; canine OS shares low mutation rate, structural complexity, altered pathways and metastasis-specific features with human OS; prior canine work covered few breeds so variants may have been breed-specific, and no canine OS WGS existed before — [PMC6642146](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6642146); [press release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/502662)
- Clinical natural history in dogs: >90% of dogs succumb to metastatic disease within two years, which the authors use to justify dogs as a comparative therapeutic-development model — [PMC6642146](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6642146)
- Cross-species aCGH concordance: DLG2 deletion in 42% human / 55.6% canine OS; shared MYC, CDKN2A/B, RB1, PTEN changes — [PMC6642146](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6642146); [Cornell summary](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/osteosarcoma-human-canine-genetic-similarities)
- Perry et al. 2014 used a comparative-oncology (canine) arm as one of three converging approaches that nominated PI3K/mTOR — i.e. canine data has already driven a human target nomination — [PMC4280583](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4280583)
- GEMM caveat: an osteoblast-lineage Cre comparison study warns a prior OS model may reflect leaky/pre-osteoblast Cre expression — [PMC record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmid/25347737)
- Human-cell engineered model: stepwise genetic transformation of human osteoblasts to sarcoma, supporting an MSC-to-pre-osteoblast intermediate origin — [PMC5396624](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5396624/)
- Cell-line limitation relevant to genomics: the AACR 2024 optical-mapping study was done in 11 OS cell lines, and found candidate ecDNA, indicating cell lines retain structural complexity but also that ecDNA content in lines may not reflect patient tumors — [abstract index](https://synapsesocial.com/papers/W4393085976)

### Inferences
- Because Trp53/Rb1-floxed GEMMs delete tumor suppressors cleanly, they cannot reproduce LTA chromothripsis (DSB-initiated TP53 loss plus translocation-amplification). No current mouse model is known from retrieved sources to generate the human-like chromothriptic genome — arguably the single largest model gap, and the reason mechanistic questions about punctuated evolution and ecDNA cannot be answered in mice.
- Canine OS likewise is not reported to have been assessed for LTA chromothripsis or ecDNA; applying the 2025 *Cell* framework to the Gardner WGS dataset is a concrete, low-cost, apparently unperformed analysis.

### Gaps
- No sources retrieved on OS PDX collections (e.g. PPTC/PIVOT) and their engraftment-bias limitations, on zebrafish OS models, or on OS organoids/bone-on-chip systems; these were not covered by the searches run and should be treated as unresearched rather than absent.
- Canine OS annual incidence figures and NCI Comparative Oncology Trials Consortium trial readouts were not retrieved.

## Under-exploited biological vulnerabilities

### Takeaway
Replication-stress targets (ATR/CHK1/WEE1) and HRD-directed PARP inhibition have repeated preclinical support in OS and HRD sarcoma, but no OS clinical trial data surfaced in these searches; IGF1R has biomarker-defined rationale (14% amplification) with an explicit call for a biomarker-selected re-trial; PI3K/mTOR has had three independent lines of evidence since 2014.

### Cited findings
- The ATR-CHK1-WEE1 pathway is described as a critical dependency in the context of DNA damage and replicative stress in osteosarcoma (Sayles et al., Clin Cancer Res abstract B004) — [UCSF index](https://womeninscience.ucsf.edu/node/23646)
- Inhibition of ATR, CHK1 and WEE1 elicited synthetic lethality in sarcoma cells with HR deficiency, which showed concomitant upregulation of ATR signaling — [MedChemExpress publication record](https://www.medchemexpress.com/publications/41651400.html)
- Combining ATR inhibitor VE822 with WEE1 inhibitor MK1775 in U2OS OS cells plus four lung lines did not fully overcome resistance to WEE1 inhibition and reduced viability only variably — a negative/limiting result worth flagging — [AMiner record](https://aminer.org/pub/610fb2ed5244ab9dcbbb2ce5/differential-effects-of-combined-atr-wee-inhibition-in-cancer-cells)
- WEE1 was linked to cisplatin resistance in OS via RelA/NF-kB signaling, with the authors proposing WEE1 inhibition for OS with that signature (*Cancer Res Treat*, 2022) — [PMC8756126](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/9b070a2679dd20e73294f3e89f94ac22/PMC8756126.pdf)
- A 2024 review states ATR, CHK1, WEE1 and MYT1 inhibitors are in clinical evaluation as monotherapy or combined with chemotherapy, PARP inhibitors or checkpoint inhibitors (not OS-specific) — [PMC10816012](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10816012/)
- Kovac et al. 2015 explicitly framed acquired BRCA-like traits in OS as therapeutically exploitable — [record](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/154710/1/154710.pdf)
- IGF1R: amplified in 14% of 87 OS by FISH; press coverage of Behjati 2017 states the results support a re-trial of IGF1R inhibitors in the genetically selected subset — [UCL news](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/cancer/news/existing-drugs-could-benefit-patients-bone-cancer-genetic-study-suggests); [GEN](https://genengnews.com/news/whole-genome-study-shows-igf1r-inhibitors-may-help-some-osteosarcoma-patients)
- PI3K/mTOR: nominated by converging exome/genome/RNA sequencing, canine comparative data and shRNA screening, with 24% of patients carrying pathway alterations — [PMC4280583](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4280583); [commentary](https://shokatlab.ucsf.edu/pdfs/25512494.pdf)
- SQLE (squalene epoxidase, cholesterol biosynthesis) nominated as a therapeutic target in the immune-infiltrated expression subtype — [Front Oncol 2023.1111570](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2023.1111570/full)

### Inferences
- The clearest translational gap: biomarker-selected trials. IGF1R inhibitors failed unselected; HRD/BRCAness and LOH burden are measurable; WEE1 sensitivity may track with a cisplatin-resistance/NF-kB signature. None of the retrieved sources describes an OS trial that enrolled on any of these biomarkers.
- The U2OS combination result argues against naive ATR+WEE1 doublets and in favor of stratification — a nuance the enthusiasm literature tends to omit.
- Ideas transferable from other diseases that these searches did not find tested in OS: HRD-directed platinum/PARP strategies as used in BRCA-mutant breast/ovarian; ecDNA-directed CHK1 inhibition as developed in neuroblastoma/glioma ecDNA biology; chromothripsis/micronuclei-cGAS-STING innate-immune activation to warm a cold tumor; RANKL blockade repurposed from giant cell tumor/bone metastasis.
