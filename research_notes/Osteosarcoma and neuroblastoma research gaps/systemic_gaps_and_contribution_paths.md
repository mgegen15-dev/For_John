# Systemic and Structural Gaps in Pediatric Bone/Solid-Tumor Research (Osteosarcoma primary, Neuroblastoma secondary) and Contribution Paths

Notes compiled 2026-11-13. Items marked "verify" came from memory or secondary sources and could not be confirmed this session.

## Funding: pediatric share, osteosarcoma share, key funders, CCDI, and NIH/NCI budgets 2025-2026

### Takeaway
Pediatric cancer gets less than 5% of NCI's budget, and nobody publishes a dollar figure for osteosarcoma. Most osteosarcoma-specific money comes from small family-funded foundations (MIB Agents has given about $2.9M in total since 2016). In FY2026 Congress turned down the proposed ~40% NIH cut, and CCDI moved to $100M a year.

### Cited Findings
- AACR: pediatric cancer research is "less than 5 percent" of NCI's annual budget. AACR also says NIH and NCI report pediatric spending only as a broad category, with no breakdown by disease — [AACR Pediatric Cancer Progress Report 2025](https://cancerprogressreport.aacr.org/pediatric/pcpr25-contents/pcpr25-a-message-from-the-aacr/); [AACR policy chapter](https://cancerprogressreport.aacr.org/pediatric/pcpr25-contents/pcpr25-advancing-pediatric-cancer-research-and-patient-care-through-evidence-based-policies/)
- Older NCI pediatric investment figures: $179.6M (FY2006) and $197.1M (FY2010) — [NCI Pediatric Snapshot (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20120915081530/http://www.cancer.gov/aboutnci/servingpeople/snapshots/pediatric.pdf). NCI also publishes a pediatric portfolio analysis — [NCI pediatric analysis PDF](https://www.cancer.gov/types/childhood-cancers/research/pediatric-analysis.pdf)
- Osteosarcoma has about 800 US cases a year, and its prognosis is described as roughly unchanged since the 1970s — [Drug Discovery News](https://www.drugdiscoverynews.com/nci-grant-pushes-forward-bone-cancer-research-9981)
- No NCI funding figure specific to osteosarcoma turned up. NIH RePORTER (reporter.nih.gov) can be used to estimate one.
- FY2026 NIH: the enacted program level is $47.493B, up $458M (+1.0%) over FY2025. The President's request was $27.915B (−40.6%). Enacted in the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, signed Feb 3, 2026, which did not adopt the proposed NIH restructuring — [CRS R43341 (May 2026)](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-05-18_R43341_3cc14d4f8e161830185f61cc3883386300b058ba.html)
- The FY2026 NCI line item is not confirmed. A secondary source says NCI rose by $128M — [GrantedAI](https://grantedai.com/news/fy2026-federal-budget-delivers-major-boost-for-cancer-research-nih-up-415m-nci-u). Primary source to check: [FY2027 NCI Congressional Justification](https://www.cancer.gov/about-nci/budget/congressional-justification/fy2027-nci-congressional-justification.pdf)
- CCDI: a September 2025 executive order and an HHS announcement doubled CCDI from $50M to $100M, with an emphasis on AI — [HHS press release](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-doubles-ai-backed-childhood-Cancer-research-funding.html); [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/amid-funding-cuts-white-house-pledges-100m-pediatric-cancer-data-initiative). The FY2026 request had $50M for CCDI. Advocates say the final FY2026 amount was $100M, but an earlier House bill had $55M, so this conflicts — [Children's Cancer Cause, Appropriations Watch](https://www.childrenscancercause.org/blog/appropriations-watch); [Action Days 2026](https://www.childrenscancercause.org/blog/action-days-2026). The FY2027 President's budget includes $100M for CCDI — [FY2027 NCI CJ](https://www.cancer.gov/about-nci/budget/congressional-justification/fy2027-nci-congressional-justification.pdf)
- Advocates are asking for $7.99B for NCI in FY2027 — [Alliance for Childhood Cancer FY27 ask](https://www.aphon.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Alliance-House-FY27-LHHS-NIH-NCI-feb-26-FINAL.pdf)
- MIB Agents (osteosarcoma-specific):
  - Through its OutSmarting Osteosarcoma program it has awarded $2.9M to 37 investigators. Grant sizes are a $100K Hero Grant and a $50K Young Investigator award.
  - The 2025 cycle gave $700K to 10 scientists.
  - Money comes from Family Funds, and those families sit on the peer review as stakeholder reviewers.
  - Letters of Intent for the 2027 cycle are open.
  - [Clinical Research News 2025](https://www.clinicalresearchnewsonline.com/news/2025/06/12/mib-agents'-community-of-families-has-awarded-2-5m-in-grants-to-research-pediatric-bone-cancer); [PRNewswire 2024](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/mib-agents-community-of-families-has-awarded-over-1-9m-in-grants-to-research-pediatric-bone-cancer-302170312.html)

### Inferences
- Osteosarcoma is a rare cancer inside an underfunded category (pediatric). Each year's specific funding is probably in the low tens of millions of dollars across all sources. This could not be verified, and estimating it from RePORTER is itself a useful project.
- Federal funding held steady in FY2026 even after the cut was proposed. Budget risk returns with every cycle, which keeps appropriations advocacy useful.

## Drug development incentives and regulation

### Takeaway
The RACE Act has increased the number of required pediatric studies, but few labels or approvals have come from it so far. The Rare Pediatric Disease PRV program lapsed in December 2024 and was reauthorized to September 30, 2029 in February 2026. The EU reached political agreement on its pharma reform, including paediatric rules, in December 2025, and formal adoption was still in progress in 2026. Osteosarcoma still has no approved targeted therapy or immunotherapy in the US.

### Cited Findings
- PRV program:
  - Section 6604 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (signed Feb 3, 2026) extends it through Sept 30, 2029 and requires a GAO study of how well it works.
  - The same law narrowed orphan exclusivity to the same approved use.
  - A voucher sold for $200M in January 2026 (Jazz).
  - [Lowenstein Sandler alert, Feb 11 2026](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/congress-reauthorizes-pediatric-priority-review-voucher-program-in-fy2026-budget-renewed-incentives-for-rare-pediatric-drug-development-fdalife-sciences); [WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/congress-reauthorizes-the-rare-pediatric-disease-priority-review-voucher-program-and-clarifies-orphan-drug-exclusivity.html)
- RACE Act:
  - Enacted in 2017 under FDARA and in effect since Aug 18, 2020. It requires molecularly targeted pediatric studies when an adult cancer drug's target is relevant to a pediatric cancer.
  - Before RACE, pediatric trials were waived or exempt for all such therapies. After RACE, 42.9% of approved cancer therapies required pediatric research, though many requirements were still waived.
  - FDA issued 131 initial Pediatric Study Plans and 22 PREA postmarketing requirements in 2020–2023.
  - The median time from approval to the final report due date for a pediatric study requirement is 5.96 years.
  - [Friends of Cancer Research](https://friendsofcancerresearch.org/blog/assessing-the-impact-of-the-research-to-accelerate-cures-and-equity-race-act/); [AACR release](https://www.aacr.org/about-the-aacr/newsroom/news-releases/race-act-increased-the-number-of-cancer-drugs-with-required-studies-for-use-in-pediatric-patients/); [FDAnews](https://www.fdanews.com/articles/213703-pediatric-oncology-drug-studies-boosted-by-race-act-fda-says)
- GAO counted 32 planned pediatric studies from Aug 2020 to Aug 2022, all early-phase or not yet started — [GAO-23-105947](https://gao.gov/products/gao-23-105947)
- IQVIA: more than 70% of ongoing cancer trials involve drugs with RACE-defined molecular targets, but only 6.9% include pediatric-age participants. Advocates want FDA to report annually on RACE — [Children's Cancer Cause](https://www.childrenscancercause.org/blog/race-act-effectiveness)
- EU:
  - Political agreement on 11 Dec 2025 replaces Directive 2001/83/EC and Regulation 726/2004 and updates the orphan and paediatric rules, including iterative PIPs.
  - Compromise texts were published 6 Mar 2026, and the SANT committee approved them 18 Mar 2026.
  - The acts are expected to enter into force in 2026, with a transition period through 2028. Final plenary adoption and Official Journal publication were not verified.
  - [European Commission](https://health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/reform-eu-pharmaceutical-legislation_en); [EMA](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/about-us/what-we-do/reform-eu-pharmaceutical-legislation); [Sidley, May 2026](https://goodlifesci.sidley.com/2026/05/18/eu-pharma-package-compromise-text-published-best-efforts-required-part-2/)
- Osteosarcoma market:
  - No approved targeted therapy, no approved US immunotherapy, and no approved option for relapsed disease. Mifamurtide (MEPACT) is approved in the EU but not the US.
  - Khapzory (2018) is a methotrexate rescue agent, not an osteosarcoma therapy.
  - Source is a commercial blog, so treat with caution — [DelveInsight](https://www.delveinsight.com/blog/?p=36310)

### Inferences
- Why pharma rarely runs osteosarcoma trials:
  - The market is small (about 800 US cases a year).
  - Osteosarcoma is a complex-karyotype tumor without recurrent druggable drivers (see Pediatric MATCH's 13% match rate below), so the "relevant molecular target" trigger in RACE seldom applies.
  - There are no validated historical controls or response endpoints for relapsed disease.
  - The PRV is worth about $100–200M per voucher, which is the main financial carrot.
- Known general facts to verify against FDA OCE/Pediatric Oncology pages before citing:
  - The Best Pharmaceuticals for Children Act is a voluntary incentive that grants 6 months of exclusivity in exchange for a Written Request. It was made permanent in 2012 under FDASIA.
  - FDA keeps a Pediatric Molecular Target List.
  - Only a few drugs have been approved first for pediatric cancer, for example dinutuximab for high-risk neuroblastoma (2015) and naxitamab (2020).

### Gaps
- No authoritative count of drugs approved first for pediatric cancers was retrieved this session.
- No 2025–2026 FDA RACE impact report was found.

## Trial infrastructure

### Takeaway
Precision-medicine trials are a poor fit for osteosarcoma: only about 13% of patients in Pediatric MATCH had a match. Relapsed-osteosarcoma phase II trials are too heterogeneous to compare. Europe's FOSTER consortium is building common endpoints and a randomized phase III trial.

### Cited Findings
- Pediatric MATCH (NCI-COG):
  - Actionable alteration matched to an arm: 29% (112 patients). Assigned to treatment: 24% (95 patients).
  - Match rates among non-CNS tumors ranged from 13% for osteosarcoma to more than 50% for rhabdomyosarcoma.
  - [Healio 2019](https://www.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20190517/pediatric-tumorsequencing-trial-finds-higher-rate-of-targetable-alterations-than-expected); [Oncology Pharmacist](https://www.theoncologypharmacist.com/issues/2019/august-2019-vol-12-no-3/nci-cog-pediatric-match-trial-shows-high-rate-of-targetable-molecular-alterations)
- In the samotolisib (PI3K/mTOR) arm, osteosarcoma was a common diagnosis (n=6), and the arm showed no activity — [PMC9273376](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9273376)
- A FOSTER systematic review covering about two decades of relapsed/refractory osteosarcoma phase II trials found high heterogeneity in patient selection, design, and endpoints. It also found no good historical cohorts, which limits whether results can be used in practice — [Cancer Treatment Reviews 2023 (UCL)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10178055/). A FOSTER relapsed-osteosarcoma prognostic-factors paper is also available — [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10220907/1/Wilson_Relapsed%20OS%20prognostic%20factors%20accepted.pdf)
- FOSTER-CabOS is a European randomized phase III trial of cabozantinib for newly diagnosed high-grade osteosarcoma, planned to open in 2025 — [Onkologi i Sverige](https://onkologiisverige.se/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/OIS-nr-5-Kliniska-studier.pdf)

### Inferences
- The binding constraint is not trial slots. It is the shortage of credible agents to test and the lack of a shared benchmark against which to judge single-arm trials. A pooled historical-control dataset would therefore have high leverage.
- From memory, verify before citing:
  - COG and SARC run most US osteosarcoma trials.
  - PIVOT, the NCI successor to PPTP, does preclinical in vivo testing.
  - ACCELERATE runs multi-stakeholder Paediatric Strategy Forums, including one on osteosarcoma.
  - ITCC runs early-phase trials in Europe.
  - AYA patients (15–39) enroll in trials far less often than children. Osteosarcoma peaks in adolescence, so it is directly affected.

### Gaps
- Current details on PIVOT, ACCELERATE forum outputs, and AYA enrollment statistics were not retrieved this session.

## Data and tissue

### Takeaway
Osteosarcoma genomic data is small and scattered across controlled-access silos. Paired relapse samples are scarce. Patient-partnered genomics (the Osteosarcoma Project) is the most open new source.

### Cited Findings
- NCI TARGET program osteosarcoma page (genomic data on dbGaP/GDC) — [NCI CCG TARGET-OS](https://www.cancer.gov/ccg/research/genome-sequencing/target/studied-cancers/osteosarcoma)
- Osteosarcoma Project (Count Me In, Broad/Dana-Farber):
  - Open to anyone ever diagnosed with osteosarcoma in the US or Canada, at any age.
  - Patients send records, saliva or blood, and archival tumor.
  - Data is released in regular batches.
  - Individual results cannot be returned in New York or Canada.
  - [Dana-Farber/Boston Children's](https://danafarberbostonchildrens.org/news/count-me-launches-osteosarcoma-project); [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/count-me-in-launches-osteosarcoma-project); [MIB Agents OS Project page](https://mibagents.org/post/os-project)
- The NIH award behind the OS and LMS projects targets 3,000 consented patients. No current enrollment count was found — [NIH RePORTER](https://reporter.nih.gov/search/5h7ZK5hRxEOvelcFgGuMBA/project-details/10048560); [PE-CGS Count Me In](https://pe-cgs.containers.it.osu.edu/all-research-centers/count-me-in/)

### Inferences
- From memory, verify before citing:
  - St. Jude Cloud, Kids First/CAVATICA, PedcBioPortal, the CCDI Molecular Characterization Initiative, UCSC Treehouse (open RNA-seq compendium), and DepMap all have some osteosarcoma or neuroblastoma data.
  - Most raw data needs dbGaP authorization, which requires an institutional signing official. That is the main barrier for independent researchers.
  - Treehouse, cBioPortal-processed data, DepMap, and Count Me In releases are open-tier.

## Patient/family-driven research models

### Takeaway
Family-funded models work best when they pair money with structure: peer review that includes families as stakeholder reviewers (MIB Agents), patient-partnered direct-to-patient data donation (Count Me In), and consortium-scale commitments.

### Cited Findings
- MIB Agents: Family Funds raise money in honor of a patient, and those families join scientific peer review as stakeholder reviewers — [Clinical Research News](https://www.clinicalresearchnewsonline.com/news/2025/06/12/mib-agents'-community-of-families-has-awarded-2-5m-in-grants-to-research-pediatric-bone-cancer)
- Count Me In was co-designed with patients and advocacy groups — [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/count-me-in-launches-osteosarcoma-project)

### Inferences
- From memory, verify before citing: other parent-led models that worked include the neuroblastoma NMTRC/Beat Childhood Cancer consortium, which families funded; DIPG parent consortia such as the Defeat DIPG network; and ALSF's grassroots model.

### Gaps
- No evaluation studies of what made these models effective were retrieved.

## Concrete contribution paths for non-specialists or small teams

### Takeaway
The leverage points that fit data, finance, or organizing skills best are:
1. A living, standardized evidence map and pooled historical-control dataset for relapsed-osteosarcoma trials (the FOSTER review names this gap).
2. Directing families to the Osteosarcoma Project to donate tissue and data.
3. Funding catalytic grants through vehicles like MIB Agents Family Funds.
4. Policy advocacy: CCDI and NCI appropriations, RACE reporting, and the upcoming GAO review of the PRV program.

### Cited Findings
- Relapsed-osteosarcoma trials lack harmonized endpoints and historical cohorts — [FOSTER review](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10178055/)
- The Osteosarcoma Project accepts direct enrollment from patients in the US and Canada — [OS Project via MIB Agents](https://mibagents.org/post/os-project)
- MIB Agents takes 2027-cycle Letters of Intent, and anyone can start a Family Fund — [Clinical Research News](https://www.clinicalresearchnewsonline.com/news/2025/06/12/mib-agents'-community-of-families-has-awarded-2-5m-in-grants-to-research-pediatric-bone-cancer)
- Advocacy channels: Children's Cancer Cause runs appropriations campaigns and a RACE annual-report push — [Appropriations Watch](https://www.childrenscancercause.org/blog/appropriations-watch); [RACE effectiveness](https://www.childrenscancercause.org/blog/race-act-effectiveness). The PRV law requires a GAO effectiveness study, which advocates can feed into — [Lowenstein](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/congress-reauthorizes-pediatric-priority-review-voucher-program-in-fy2026-budget-renewed-incentives-for-rare-pediatric-drug-development-fdalife-sciences)

### Inferences
- Feasible projects:
  - An osteosarcoma funding tracker built from NIH RePORTER plus foundation awards. No public figure exists.
  - A ClinicalTrials.gov-based tracker of osteosarcoma trial pipelines and outcomes.
  - Analyses of open-tier data (Treehouse, DepMap, cBioPortal, Count Me In releases).
  - Partnering with an academic lab to get dbGaP access.
- Finance skills apply to modeling PRV economics and incentive gaps for osteosarcoma.

- Lists of which organizations formally accept outside data analysts or volunteers were not verified.
