## Top 10 must-fix

1. **The FAK framing is wrong in the brief (lines 14, 19, 23, 80) and the leads report (lines 23, 95).** Defactinib itself reduced lung metastasis in an orthotopic osteosarcoma mouse model: 2 of 6 treated vs 6 of 6 control mice (P = 0.03; Genes & Diseases 2023, PMID 37013056). It is small and used engineered cells, but "no mouse evidence against metastasis" and "the only mouse study did not measure metastasis" are false.
2. **The DKK1 dog angle is missing (brief lines 45, 48, 73; leads line 99).** Gregory's NIH R21 CA286523 is developing a *canine* DKK1 morpholino "with the ultimate goal of performing veterinary clinical trials in client-owned dogs". The brief asks who could run a canine DKK1 study without saying that one is being prepared.
3. **Wrong citations a scientist will click.** (a) Gaps line 103: the cabozantinib 11.9% response rate is cited to PMC9918141, a real-world series with zero responses; CABONE (Lancet Oncol 2020) is the source. (b) Gaps line 89: the "US registry" 71.8%/30.4% figures are cited to a Korean registry paper that has neither number. (c) Gaps line 97: PMID 12586810 is Ferrari 2003, not Kempf-Bielack 2005 (that is PMID 15659502). (d) Gaps line 174: PMC9273376 is the Pediatric MATCH screening paper, not a PI3K/mTOR result. Gap 4 says ctDNA "predicted outcome" in osteosarcoma, which is wrong; the significant result was in Ewing sarcoma.
